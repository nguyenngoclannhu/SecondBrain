#### 1. Health Catalyst sold 60% of its adjusted EBITDA to become debt-free

Stephanie’s two biggest losers of the Q2 season were Teladoc and Health Catalyst. [Teladoc I expected](https://substack.com/redirect/57953581-c65b-465b-9ec0-eca60aedb0f9?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). Health Catalyst: not really. I said on air that they are one of the good guys, and Stephanie’s reply was the line of the episode: “They’re a great product, but their stock wasn’t one of the good guys.”

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!rLqX!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F74d5796d-39f6-4e74-a3fa-0be2e7603c31_1600x900.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/a3cf8642-a716-4c47-bc13-d276aa92eacc?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)**One-day stock moves on the first session after each Q2 print. Source: stockanalysis.com.**The HCAT stock bottomed at [$0.96 on April 10](https://substack.com/redirect/48c53690-d030-4b5f-b13a-eef8c2607705?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), or $73M in market cap. On June 4, Health Catalyst announced the sale of Vitalware to Med-Metrix for [$147M in cash](https://substack.com/redirect/f37b5678-6a6a-4597-8cb7-f2239aaeaff8?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), [more than the company’s entire market cap](https://substack.com/redirect/a58c9b37-209b-47d9-8d68-95cd9c1a7f37?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) at the time! The shares jumped 46% in a day, and by the eve of the Q2 print the stock had more than doubled off the April low. Heroic, until you read the fine print. 😉

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!S3kG!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fab76a980-12eb-4922-96f9-120e43275698_1600x1000.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/70ffc5ec-c2f3-461b-85e5-27137dba79c7?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)**Health Catalyst’s Aug 6 guidance, annotated.**On August 6, the company [cut 2026 revenue guidance](https://substack.com/redirect/1d4ab795-ce0c-4e81-a289-32d471e8dd90?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) to $246M to $249M from the [$260M to $265M](https://substack.com/redirect/2d9d5b90-9d11-4ae2-9f5a-92b18e3f9519?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) it guided in May, and cut adjusted EBITDA guidance to $18M to $18.5M from $30M to $33M. Vitalware had contributed $11.4M of the $19.1M in first-half adjusted EBITDA. They sold roughly 60% of their adjusted EBITDA, and adjusted EBITDA is the generous yardstick. Health Catalyst has never(!) reported a GAAP net profit, and it is not about to start. Q3 adjusted EBITDA guidance is $0 to $0.5M. The stock [fell 25% the next day](https://substack.com/redirect/9fa63c2e-5967-430a-903d-2b10719d6edc?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), and Canaccord [downgraded it to Hold](https://substack.com/redirect/89004bfc-bc6f-40d3-94d5-2227f34b3b77?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) on August 20.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!jzdu!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff65f58ad-d9c5-4479-ae10-8a1878934f9f_1600x900.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/91f0a956-240a-4b22-858a-24601511a292?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)**Health Catalyst adjusted EBITDA: first half actual versus second half implied, and the guidance cut. Source: company filings.**The proceeds [repaid the credit facility in full](https://substack.com/redirect/208b7eca-e038-466c-86a6-725700cc908d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), so Health Catalyst is now debt-free. 

That is survival, not a growth story. 

What is left is a healthcare data company with under $250M in revenue, a second half guided to roughly break-even adjusted EBITDA, a [$122.5M goodwill impairment](https://substack.com/redirect/38c7fb20-c173-4e64-a014-55e19f0283f4?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) already booked this year, and a [$118M market cap](https://substack.com/redirect/cb8dc735-bc73-464e-a5f0-ae8d35367a94?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). Private rival Innovaccer last priced at [$3.45B](https://substack.com/redirect/29d894a9-e8b1-435f-adc6-8296a2691c88?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), roughly 29 times that. 

Stephanie’s verdict: they need to change the narrative, and she does not know how they dig out of it. Neither do I.

#### 2. Epic UGM 2026: promises, partners, lawsuits, and a cowgirl hat 🤠

Judy Faulkner gave her executive address on August 18 [dressed as a cowgirl](https://substack.com/redirect/94bf63c9-b54b-458b-8804-05219b98df40?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), in keeping with the county-fair theme “[Meet Me at the Midway](https://substack.com/redirect/01aa739a-a5d5-4480-b18a-1577b76c1840?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g).” The list was enormous: [Ergo](https://substack.com/redirect/8da3526f-6679-4a99-b938-16c58c2afa25?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), Epic’s new “healthcare intelligence” layer built around its Art charting assistant (Ergo is Latin for “therefore”), an [Agent Factory](https://substack.com/redirect/94aee3be-c2cd-491b-a815-3e91701c769f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) with about 120 agent features live, real-time prior authorization checks, and EpicOps, Epic’s new enterprise resource planning (ERP). My problem is the same every year: nobody knows what is real and what is “in the works.” To be fair, Judy’s own scorecard says 84 of the 167 projects promised at UGM 2025 shipped on time. 

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!STjf!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fae855e37-712e-4742-b10a-74e5e083c277_1600x1000.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/a338a5a3-1fe3-4c50-bfdc-6aaa5b09ca2a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)**Judy Faulkner’s scorecard for last year’s promises, per Becker’s, annotated.****Epic is going down market.** Organizations that cannot get Community Connect are now offered [Sonnet, Orchard and Garden Plot](https://substack.com/redirect/f5b1a266-937a-4c00-ae34-a1d5fe43a254?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), Garden Plot is being extended toward smaller hospitals, and a “Flower Pot” tier for very small practices is in development. That is eClinicalWorks and athenahealth territory, and Epic already holds [43.7% of the U.S. acute care EHR market](https://substack.com/redirect/7eb20209-8d12-4acc-8df0-2a3da1c863fe?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). Bailey’s read was the most honest in the room: small practices are getting rolled up by private equity, and being on Epic makes a practice easier to integrate and “sexier” to sell. Good for the industry, maybe. Bad for patients. Patients always pay.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Eb7_!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff3be6f63-c323-4b4b-8cfe-e4ea28666322_1600x900.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/4bf249c8-d7d9-428a-ae90-d0ca4ee9f312?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)**Acute care EHR share and net hospital changes in 2025. Source: KLAS via Fierce Healthcare.****Abridge and Epic now compete at Epic’s own conference.** On August 17, the first morning of UGM, Abridge made its clinical decision support agent [available to every clinician at its partner health systems](https://substack.com/redirect/01bd2f2a-7992-4215-894a-93e007391b92?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), including clinicians who do not use the Abridge scribe. The next day Epic unveiled Ergo. Both lean on Wolters Kluwer’s [UpToDate](https://substack.com/redirect/b3db8f22-1d8a-4ee5-a455-da6e92ff8184?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) for evidence. Neither mentioned OpenEvidence, which after the Nature Medicine paper ([my take here](https://substack.com/redirect/7f6309cb-a343-4cd5-8053-5a64c290d14d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)) and the endless Doximity litigation looks more and more like the kid nobody wants in the sandbox. When we [interviewed Shiv Rao](https://substack.com/redirect/077f4413-8b43-4d5b-8d9c-1e6d4605988f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) on this show in April, his answer on FDA clearance was that a scribe transcribes and assigns codes, so there is nothing to clear. Once the product starts interpreting results, that answer stops working, as I argued in “[Mayo Clinic + Abridge: Not FDA Approved!](https://substack.com/redirect/cfc1d368-7c1d-46d8-9e9c-a81ea1ba6951?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)“ Also absent: Microsoft, whose [Dragon technology](https://substack.com/redirect/788ddb6f-27f7-475a-9fb4-1368a483b7c3?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) was supposed to power Epic’s scribe a year ago. With Judy, nothing is exclusive. I wrote a year ago that [Epic picked the wrong horse in the AI scribe race](https://substack.com/redirect/b611ccbb-631b-415a-a7fb-0143d7d6e3cf?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g).

**Real-time prior auth is a “no” delivered faster.** Epic’s prior authorization check went live at four health systems with [UnitedHealthcare, Aetna and Network Health](https://substack.com/redirect/13f03495-1643-4977-bfef-e4ff3f908d61?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), five months ahead of the January 1, 2027 payer API deadline under [CMS-0057-F](https://substack.com/redirect/be20d72a-87b0-460f-b139-384202b426ba?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). Bailey, who lives inside payer rules every day, shrugged: any practice that has worked with payers long enough already knows which CPT codes need an auth. My concern is different. If the denial rate stays the same, or goes up, I do not care that the “no” arrives in real time. What Epic bought with this launch is a seat at the table in Washington as the compliant one.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Yhcq!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9a9e91e4-ab99-44b4-825d-2bda86a22f82_1600x1000.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/5e5c67a7-de51-48f1-89a6-af3dbae529d2?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)**Epic’s real-time prior authorization release, annotated.****The monopoly debate.** Stephanie’s counter to my monopoly complaint was Apple: similar dominance, and nobody says Apple is bad for phones. My answer is that Apple innovates because regulators and Android breathe down its neck, while many of Epic’s “features” are partner apps under an Epic label. Until there is real pressure, Epic will keep maximizing revenue with minimum effort. And here is what I underplayed on air: three days before UGM, Reuters reported the [FTC is probing Epic](https://substack.com/redirect/19b106d7-4a89-4460-8044-4a5d90209c18?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) over data access, STAT reported [federal and state investigators](https://substack.com/redirect/160e191a-2b25-4ac3-aba1-6da8fda26a3e?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) are looking at its NDAs, the [Texas attorney general sued](https://substack.com/redirect/21bf2568-e101-4e8b-be00-d0b8c5cd4182?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) in December, and the [Particle Health antitrust case](https://substack.com/redirect/86b5d10f-6f3c-4451-8672-2008b5f3eba6?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) is in discovery. 

That’s probably more regulatory and legal pressure than Epic has faced in its 47-year history. And yet, it still doesn’t seem like much. Judy didn’t even break a sweat in her cowgirl outfit. 🤠

#### 3. Cosmos is the one Epic product I am rooting for
