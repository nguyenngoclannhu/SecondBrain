[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!lenv!,w_250,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1714637f-8d9a-481c-bf23-eb4aa211f8f6_514x924.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/b5e66ad2-1303-4be2-90a1-37a7a8e11249?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)AI is making medical upcoding easier, faster, and vastly more scalable. At its core, upcoding means billing for diagnoses, conditions, or care that were not actually documented or delivered. That is fraud.

The laws are already on the books. The problem is enforcement. “Maximizing billing and coding” has become one of the fastest-growing businesses in American healthcare, and AI is transforming what was once a manual compliance problem into an industry of its own.

I examine some of the most recent AI-related upcoding cases, then lay out the red flags to watch as a new generation of “autonomous” medical coding companies races into the market.

I also connect this AI upcoding saga to [a direct question I recently put to Heidi Health CEO Tom Kelly](https://substack.com/redirect/1b7e11be-653c-4f29-99b8-7bb39f21cc74?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) on “Digital Health Inside Out.”

* **Last Wednesday, August 26, the DOJ [announced](https://substack.com/redirect/0b742766-1457-4c21-a622-205635c0cc8b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) a $541.5M False Claims Act settlement with The Villages Health System**, a Florida primary care group, over invalid Medicare Advantage diagnosis codes submitted to Humana, UnitedHealthcare, and Florida Blue. DOJ dates the conduct “from 2020 through 2024,” and the [settlement agreement](https://substack.com/redirect/edfa9521-aa8b-4d34-96f6-dd5fbb51cc0d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) pins it to January 2020 through December 2024. A bankruptcy judge approved it on August 25. It is the second-largest Medicare Advantage settlement I know of, arriving seven months after [Kaiser’s $556M](https://substack.com/redirect/6621d866-709a-4e2f-bcbb-f2fe16b07d72?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). It’s mind-blowing how healthcare organizations, big and small, can cough up more than half a billion dollars just to make the bad news disappear. 🤯
* **The mechanics are the story.** Per the [settlement agreement](https://substack.com/redirect/edfa9521-aa8b-4d34-96f6-dd5fbb51cc0d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), employees ran two internal programs called “Retrospective Amendments” and “Sprints,” inserting diagnosis codes into charts “sometimes months to over a year after the patient visit,” not initiated by the rendering provider, and routed for approval to a non-rendering provider such as the chief medical officer when the original doctor had left. The health system’s own consultant sampled 100 beneficiaries a year and found **unsupported codes rising from 28.6% in 2020 to 50.7% in 2024**.
* **Here is the part every healthcare AI CEO should read twice.** During that same window, The Villages Health publicly [celebrated](https://substack.com/redirect/698bd924-03c3-42f4-bf52-d741da85b16f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) an AI platform from KAID Health that mined its records for “care and coding gaps,” and Villages’ own chief medical officer, Dr. Jeffrey Lowenkron, [co-authored a case study](https://substack.com/redirect/8f0778db-5a61-483a-b17a-3bac21f4a58d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) reporting $2.5M in “net new revenue” and a “10-fold return on investment.” That case study is still on [KAID’s homepage](https://substack.com/redirect/8576cdc3-a798-4ac4-ba9c-87c9fcd8a203?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) today, August 31, 2026:[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!MAW7!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F30ba8226-2974-4398-93ed-f73553cf56f5_3380x1848.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/85a35a8f-3172-4545-9dbf-11c78d1e67ee?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)kaidhealth.com. Accessed August 30, 2026.
* **To be clear: DOJ does not name KAID or any technology vendor.** The settlement agreement contains no mention of software, algorithms, AI, or a third party. There is no public evidence that KAID generated, selected, or transmitted a single disputed code. I am not accusing KAID of anything. I am pointing at the overlap and saying it demands reporting.
* **The same day, Arintra [raised $25M](https://substack.com/redirect/376a21ad-092f-40fb-9f4f-7ff6bbad72d8?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)**, claiming $51M total (Dealroom and CB Insights count about $46M), to autonomously code every chart with, per its [product page](https://substack.com/redirect/81a2dc4f-2d6c-4b62-a0ce-d771a80d47ea?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), “Zero human touch” and “Zero coding errors.” Its own blog says [10% to 20% of charts](https://substack.com/redirect/6faa2868-80c1-425e-b19d-d90f289c2f7d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) go to a human. Its Bengaluru [job ad](https://substack.com/redirect/1df0e69e-df82-4efb-ac39-d2609ba5bca3?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) says the goal is to “maximize revenue for healthcare providers.”

Accessed August 30, 2026.* **On August 12, [I asked Heidi CEO Tom Kelly on our podcast](https://substack.com/redirect/1b7e11be-653c-4f29-99b8-7bb39f21cc74?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) where the line is.** His answer, in full below, is the most honest thing a vendor has said on this: never fabricate, and the only real fix is scribes and payers working from one shared record. Otherwise, in his words, “everyone will have to use something like Heidi or Abridge to be claiming and upcoding.”
