##### Filler Content & Strategy Finale

Two, as I’ve been rating a lot of these social media formats, one thing’s clear… a majority of the content we create for social media is meant to be a filler episode. The real show/strategy unfolds slowly as you experiment with ideas that are not confirmed to perform well and need room to breathe.

Right now, a lot of brands see “[let’s start a content series](https://substack.com/redirect/d42f23e1-8b5a-4d30-a0a9-9d5be4b69cc2?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)” as the most obvious answer… that too will go out of fashion. Yes, serialised content gives your content a new identity, but I remember when [Veronica Slowikowska’s TikTok series](https://substack.com/redirect/6bdb68b1-cc51-4372-8de1-62b094764cb6?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) was all the rage or people couldn’t get enough of the lore being dropped by [Nutter Butter](https://substack.com/redirect/75c575bc-0abc-40a4-8738-18f90b764456?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). Both Veronica and NB are still killing it with their content. But that demand for their content as something of a show to follow has died down.

And if you don’t end a social series, you can’t control the algorithm like Disney controls cinema screenings to keep its sequels profitable. Social media is a ticking clock. We all know a little bit of inactivity could gut the reach. Yeah, we can argue that’s not how it works.

Okay, senior social media manager, we get that at your last job you revived a dead account. Good for you. I am talking in general. Social media marketing is a pressure cooker. Now, I don’t know how to completely make sense of the metaphor I wanted here.

> Your potatoes in the cooker are the strategy, positioning, and content ideas you want to save for a while and eat when needed. Without filler content like memes, trends, and all of the formats I am about to mention, chances are the cooker will be either opened too early or too late. Whatever happens, you are forced to eat potatoes in states you don’t like.

Every brand should build its strategy around how much filler content it needs so it doesn’t run its socials like it’s an ER. The brands that are good at reactive marketing aren’t doing it all thanks to social listening or chronically online managers. Their secret is not getting stuck on content ideas that aren’t true to their brand. Guess what, that’s most of the content we post.

Oh, but we added our product to this meme/format, so it’s important. Really? The only difference is that you are posting a starter pack meme today and your competitor did it a week ago.

Filler content doesn’t happen to be only your posts. You should be working with micro-creators and real customers to fill the void while you work on ideas that require some time.

While technically not the best example, I’ve been [running my page with 400k followers](https://substack.com/redirect/47adc128-5d2f-4b4d-b0c6-8ca538fc6e30?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) for more than four years now. Yes, for most of that time, it was at 1,000 followers. We ignore that part of the story. It took me more than half a year to go from 390k to 400k, but I went flying to 300k followers within two years. I, too, had my content series, which helped me gain a ton of followers in a short span, but one day, the algorithm or my audience, whoever we want to blame, decided that was it.

Since that day, there have only been two or three filler formats that have kept me engaged with my audience while I take my time to figure out what type of content and strategy makes sense for me. And that’s the only reason I even grew to 400k. I’m not ashamed to say sometimes my posts don’t even reach 200 likes, and sometimes they’ve hit 200k.

The formats/trends below might not help you achieve the state of making people care or crash out, but they will be the filler episode for your finale, which could be either on the level of The Sopranos or How I Met Your Mother.

P.S. Why are we putting ad spend behind filler content, aka meme formats and trends? I’ve seen so many brands straight up turn these organic posts into ads. Like, how is that helping your brand? Yeah, you may gain followers, but that trend or format isn’t native to your brand. You should be putting money behind something original you created as a brand or team.

2. [View inside bag](https://substack.com/redirect/2f226307-e08a-40ea-b4cf-6d2d289957f5?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g): A format, best used this year by [LV’s In My Bag campaign](https://substack.com/redirect/1e1e1b5a-af93-4eaf-ac34-896667755bf2?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), this format is a great way to build a story around your product by showing what else surrounds it. 
	1. You can also replace the bag with other objects, like [Emi Jay](https://substack.com/redirect/f99d61e5-d300-4919-9a58-6f5b2704291e?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) used a sports cap.

This format is also the one for those who can’t afford to invest in a social series.

[Whimsical/Bedazzle](https://substack.com/redirect/76906656-0047-4f75-a020-8a201402be74?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g): Again, a very similar trend to Kinda Chic. [If your girl](https://substack.com/redirect/d0ef2d96-5bcb-4c37-8920-76ddaa647b39?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g): Copywriting-wise, it’s not as good as the “Stop using anti-XYZ language” trend, but brands still need to add some humour and relevance to the post. [Netflix documentary](https://substack.com/redirect/b24c9de3-bac6-4a49-aee1-e17d45c8750a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g): A bit of a generous rating, as many brands, specifically retail branches, highlighted the cringe/funny stories of their employees. ##### U.S. Open Influencer fallout — Blame the brands!

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!nRFh!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0c026675-ca6e-4a22-9a61-d946d1cba655_1338x353.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/05389ea5-31aa-46e3-9009-3f99682e3091?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)I guess we weren’t done talking about influencers, as the behaviour of influencers at the U.S. Open has become a national tragedy? It’s giving one of the biggest moneymakers, yet one of the less respected Grand Slams, an even worse reputation. The tennis elites are mad.

As [Mara Dettmann](https://open.substack.com/users/64182847-mara-dettmann?utm_source=mentions) and I mentioned in [our collab post about influencers](https://substack.com/redirect/f834ceb2-1bbc-4467-a170-361b3f2eb155?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), you should probably put some of the blame on the brands inviting these influencers to film content while simultaneously not giving them guidelines on how to behave at the event and so on.

> Who’s to blame? Social platforms built this system and brands sustained it. The reason your privacy at a store is at risk is not all influencers’ fault, it’s also the brand. Incentives have been set by companies to encourage filming content at their stores and give them free exposure. 

> — [Welcome To The Post-Influencer Era](https://substack.com/redirect/f834ceb2-1bbc-4467-a170-361b3f2eb155?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)

Apparently, a few of the [influencers got their credentials revoked](https://substack.com/redirect/95d9aad4-5158-422b-812e-61256283ded7?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) and are asking for a second chance to attend the event. It’s a messy situation. I hope this doesn’t lead to brands or influencers straight up using AI to insert themselves into the empty seats.

One other story, where I don’t know if that brand’s influencers were being annoying. [U.S. Open debuted The Moët Serve to rival the iconic Grey Goose Honey Deuce](https://substack.com/redirect/82400b83-afd2-41b6-9824-6f3303976bbc?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). And a lot of influencers have been posting about it. IDK if they created this mess. But I can tell you the [Moët Serve is a sustainability nightmare](https://substack.com/redirect/02ff90ec-f0be-4032-9675-1ca86b0061ee?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), with so much wasteful packaging. And, like, their attempt at making the design more tennis-focused still doesn’t have the classy look and collector’s psychology attached to it. No one is going to keep the can as a memory. Everyone wants the glass.

Influencers, am I right? Brands need to do a better job of making influencers stay away from deinfluencing, which they kind of did for a lot of people wanting to visit the U.S. Open.
