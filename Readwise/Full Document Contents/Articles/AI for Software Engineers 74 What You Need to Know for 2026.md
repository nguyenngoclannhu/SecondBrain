##### What’s Been on My Mind

This past week has seen a shift in the AI industry toward practicality. Recently, we’ve seen influential voices mention that the economic impact of AI hasn’t been living up to the hype. Most notably, we’ve seen [Andrej Karpathy](https://substack.com/redirect/32ed9269-6933-4b20-a71e-af2cfac4e958?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) and [Ilya Sutskever](https://substack.com/redirect/bf0799a5-930a-4173-b753-b5ab29e9e0fb?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) mention this during their most recent appearances on the Dwarkesh podcast.

I’ve been thinking about this a lot for two reasons:

1. The actual statistics about the job market don’t match what I’m noticing about everyday work.
2. I’ve been working on AI integration into developer workflows at work with world-class engineers and it’s much more complicated and cutting edge than we had anticipated.

First, a recent study reported a [severe decline in job listings for junior developers](https://substack.com/redirect/d5692c60-ffd3-4a8b-ace1-6be15d1cc11d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). Almost frighteningly so—to the point that the industry will be heavily impacted in the coming years as we don’t have enough junior engineers to fill the demand we need.

Everyone said AI would kill software engineering, but it turns out this has very little to do with it actually taking jobs and more to do with AI hype convincing leadership that it can.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!NqO0!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2fd2123b-685d-4ad7-953a-6188e8c01493_1196x1316.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/31eab06c-3b84-44dd-80bf-26a24a1255f3?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)From my research and daily work, I would expect the number of junior developer positions to have increased. AI makes junior developers much more capable when given to them at a company with a good engineering culture (I’ll include more on this in a separate article next week. There’s actually an entire study to prove this is the case and it’s super interesting).

Honestly, It’s kind of a cheat code for companies to hire junior engineers in a market like this. Companies that get their pick of the most talented engineers for less. We’ve also never had so many tools to increase onboarding velocity and enable developers to build more.

What my team at Google is seeing are tons of opportunities to apply AI to developer and machine learning workflows and speedups, but applying these properly is much more complicated than one might think. A lot of thought needs to go into security and ensuring system performance. AI evals are much more difficult than regular test suites.

In 2026, we’ll see more applications of AI explored and productionization of agents mature. **It’ll be even easier for software engineers to get involved with AI** as companies realize the useful applications of AI and the headcount required to achieve it.

If you want to get into AI as a software engineer in 2026, these are the top three skills I’d focus on:

* **Building agents**. Agents will continue to build in 2026 and companies will narrow on their most impactful applications. These applications will far outnumber the supply of developers able to build them.
* **MLOps**. This has been an incredibly valuable skill for about a decade now and will only get more valuable in the coming years. More companies using AI means more models are being trained. Companies will need engineers that understand that training process and can build the infra necessary to make it happen.
* **AI Cybersecurity**. You wouldn’t believe the security and privacy complexities non-deterministic systems introduce. This is another article I’ve got in the works and something we’ve been deeply exploring at Google. If you can understand this, there will be opportunities available.

Links to learn each are included in the ‘Learning Resources’ section at the bottom.

##### Things you should know about

**Software engineering is going agentic**

We already know that [nearly 90% of organizations are using AI to code](https://substack.com/redirect/64bdf1cb-1440-4ea4-a96a-6710891fa3f1?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), but now agents are making their way into enterprises. [57% of organizations are deploying agents for multi-stage](https://substack.com/redirect/0dab4fb5-1f29-4f09-a4b7-f9c3065b9562?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) workflows with 16% of those being cross-team workflows. In 2026, 81% of teams plan to use agents with 39% of those agents being developed for multi-step workflows.

Interestingly, [80% of organizations are reporting a return on their investment](https://substack.com/redirect/0dab4fb5-1f29-4f09-a4b7-f9c3065b9562?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). As we’ve mentioned previously, this is a number that is *very* difficult to quantify. What does ROI actually mean in multi-step agentic workflows? It greatly depends on the workflow and the goals it aims to achieve. There isn’t a universal standard for quantifying this uptick in velocity.

The use of agents and AI is extending beyond traditional software engineering tasks (code planning, generation, document, review, etc.—where they’re seeing a 59% increase in productivity) to tasks like data analysis and report generation where they’re seeing similar gains.

I highly recommend reading [Anthropic’s 2026 State of AI Agents Report](https://substack.com/redirect/64bdf1cb-1440-4ea4-a96a-6710891fa3f1?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), even if you only read the foreword. If you want me to go more in-depth into this so we can really get into how enterprises are using AI and the ROI they’re achieving, comment at the bottom of this article.

**OpenAI declares a ‘Code Red’**

OpenAI [declared a Code Red](https://substack.com/redirect/c7a541da-048f-49b5-bff9-0b14671bd68d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) internally as competitors have started stealing market share. Most notably, Google is stealing is gaining both consumer and enterprise market share in AI tools and Anthropic has increased their revenue considerably to the point where they’re [considering IPO’ing in 2026](https://substack.com/redirect/2ed13083-596d-43ba-9e1b-ab8de12890f0?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g).

I believe this is being largely overblown by the public. OpenAI has revolutionized the consumer understanding of LLM products and [continue to lead in that area](https://substack.com/redirect/604f6d96-297d-4eda-8b08-f94be2dea432?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). Over the past three years, they’ve been competing with (and beating) a company of Google’s size as a startup. In reality, Google’s resources far outnumber OpenAI’s own. What we’re seeing is OpenAI figuring out the focus of their product offerings to become revenue positive.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Sdip!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F612ab909-7bda-4c0b-917d-9a5aea71da55_2048x1456.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/73b1a71e-3997-4a0e-8e02-192754e36552?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)Anthropic is a great example of why this is important. Six months to a year ago, many individuals counted Anthropic out of the AI race because of their size and less prominent standing. In reality, they’re almost revenue positive and are consistently beating larger companies in their areas of focus, the most notable of which is coding.

We’re seeing OpenAI figure out the same right now as they continue to push their product offerings forward.

**The Linux Foundation now owns Model Context Protocol (MCP)**

The Linux Foundation is a non-profit organization that has been dedicated to fostering the growth of open-source software for decades. Some examples of software they steward are The Linux Kernel, Kubernetes, Node.js, and PyTorch. They have a history of maintaining vendor neutrality and facilitating open collaboration.

The [Agent AI Foundation (AAIF) is a directed fund under The Linux Foundation](https://substack.com/redirect/47fa5df6-44fa-414c-82bd-dd39c77a22b9?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). It’s a joint partnership between major players in AI including OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic. The goal of the AAIF is to maintain agentic AI transparency and collaboration to ensure the technology benefits everyone and open standards continue to be developed and maintained.

MCP will join the AAIF as a founding project to bring AI open standards under one roof. This is great news for all of us as open standards make it much easier to actually build and apply new technologies.

##### The state of the market

[CEOs are still betting huge on AI in 2026](https://substack.com/redirect/70d404c6-a155-40a8-897d-9cf682983c63?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). As mentioned above, there’s a huge demand for developers that can build agentic AI systems. This means taking a problem, prototyping an agentic solution as needed, and building the entire system. This means understanding complex, multi-step workflows and the work that go into ensuring these systems are productionized.

To learn this, I’d focus on understanding (resources for learning each at the bottom of this article):

* **Evals**. These are like tests for LLMs and agentic systems. All software engineers know that testing gets much more complex when systems are non-deterministic and that’s what makes evals so complicated.
* **Protocols**. If you haven’t spin up an MCP server so your favorite CLI tool can access a resource you need it to. MCP servers are huge for integration into agentic workflows and the best way to learn them is by building one.
* **Agentic workflow patterns**. There are certain patterns to building agents that are followed for specific use cases. I’ve linked a guide in the ‘Learning Resources’ section.

<h3 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.375em;">What’s Been on My Mind</h3>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>This past week has seen a shift in the AI industry toward practicality. Recently, we’ve seen influential voices mention that the economic impact of AI hasn’t been living up to the hype. Most notably, we’ve seen </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/32ed9269-6933-4b20-a71e-af2cfac4e958?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #eb5757;text-decoration: none;">Andrej Karpathy</a><span> and </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/bf0799a5-930a-4173-b753-b5ab29e9e0fb?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #eb5757;text-decoration: none;">Ilya Sutskever</a><span> mention this during their most recent appearances on the Dwarkesh podcast.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">I’ve been thinking about this a lot for two reasons:</p>
<ol style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;">
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">The actual statistics about the job market don’t match what I’m noticing about everyday work.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">I’ve been working on AI integration into developer workflows at work with world-class engineers and it’s much more complicated and cutting edge than we had anticipated.</p></li>
</ol>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>First, a recent study reported a </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/d5692c60-ffd3-4a8b-ace1-6be15d1cc11d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #eb5757;text-decoration: none;">severe decline in job listings for junior developers</a><span>. Almost frighteningly so—to the point that the industry will be heavily impacted in the coming years as we don’t have enough junior engineers to fill the demand we need.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Everyone said AI would kill software engineering, but it turns out this has very little to do with it actually taking jobs and more to do with AI hype convincing leadership that it can.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">From my research and daily work, I would expect the number of junior developer positions to have increased. AI makes junior developers much more capable when given to them at a company with a good engineering culture (I’ll include more on this in a separate article next week. There’s actually an entire study to prove this is the case and it’s super interesting).</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Honestly, It’s kind of a cheat code for companies to hire junior engineers in a market like this. Companies that get their pick of the most talented engineers for less. We’ve also never had so many tools to increase onboarding velocity and enable developers to build more.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">What my team at Google is seeing are tons of opportunities to apply AI to developer and machine learning workflows and speedups, but applying these properly is much more complicated than one might think. A lot of thought needs to go into security and ensuring system performance. AI evals are much more difficult than regular test suites.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>In 2026, we’ll see more applications of AI explored and productionization of agents mature. </span><strong>It’ll be even easier for software engineers to get involved with AI</strong><span> as companies realize the useful applications of AI and the headcount required to achieve it.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">If you want to get into AI as a software engineer in 2026, these are the top three skills I’d focus on:</p>
<ul style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;">
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;"><strong>Building agents</strong><span>. Agents will continue to build in 2026 and companies will narrow on their most impactful applications. These applications will far outnumber the supply of developers able to build them.</span></p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;"><strong>MLOps</strong><span>. This has been an incredibly valuable skill for about a decade now and will only get more valuable in the coming years. More companies using AI means more models are being trained. Companies will need engineers that understand that training process and can build the infra necessary to make it happen.</span></p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;"><strong>AI Cybersecurity</strong><span>. You wouldn’t believe the security and privacy complexities non-deterministic systems introduce. This is another article I’ve got in the works and something we’ve been deeply exploring at Google. If you can understand this, there will be opportunities available.</span></p></li>
</ul>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>We already know that </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/64bdf1cb-1440-4ea4-a96a-6710891fa3f1?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #eb5757;text-decoration: none;">nearly 90% of organizations are using AI to code</a><span>, but now agents are making their way into enterprises. </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/0dab4fb5-1f29-4f09-a4b7-f9c3065b9562?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #eb5757;text-decoration: none;">57% of organizations are deploying agents for multi-stage</a><span> workflows with 16% of those being cross-team workflows. <p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/70d404c6-a155-40a8-897d-9cf682983c63?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #eb5757;text-decoration: none;">CEOs are still betting huge on AI in 2026</a><span>. As mentioned above, there’s a huge demand for developers that can build agentic AI systems. This means taking a problem, prototyping an agentic solution as needed, and building the entire system. This means understanding complex, multi-step workflows and the work that go into ensuring these systems are productionized.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">To learn this, I’d focus on understanding (resources for learning each at the bottom of this article):</p>
<ul style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;">
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;"><strong>Evals</strong><span>. All software engineers know that testing gets much more complex when systems are non-deterministic and that’s what makes evals so complicated.</span></p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;"><strong>Protocols</strong><span>. There are certain patterns to building agents that are followed for specific use cases. I’ve linked a guide in the ‘Learning Resources’ section.</span></p></li>
