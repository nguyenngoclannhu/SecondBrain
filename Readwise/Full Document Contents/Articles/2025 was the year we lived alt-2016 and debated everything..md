##### January: The Great Rest

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!C1Tr!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2652fe95-5aac-4c58-a3d1-8d9a5d304af5.tif)](https://substack.com/redirect/146075f0-83ea-4cb1-bcad-1c885ab5c879?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)**Jaskaran**: In a way, the trends we saw at the beginning of 2025 set the tone and platform for AI, brands, and media platforms to reinvent their strategies. [Italian brainrot](https://substack.com/redirect/12f465df-00c7-47dd-a23f-276cf2b0cd8c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) was AI generated, and there are not many positives to how it affects kids, but it was not the same as companies trying to force AI content on consumers by replacing what already exists. The AI brainrot knew its place.

The brainrot shifted to YouTube Shorts as TikTok went through a temporary ban, which ignited a debate about influencer ethics as many creators started to admit to sketchy tactics, and the [migration to Rednote](https://substack.com/redirect/78a9ba23-1723-4e30-81a6-a93a3d382b51?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) happened. Interestingly, people humanized the algorithm as if it were a real person, giving rise to the “[Chinese spy](https://substack.com/redirect/f2575161-b61b-4f58-b778-63006997d0ed?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)” joke. In November, we saw a similar trend on Threads, with people adding “[dear algo](https://substack.com/redirect/f9a633c2-b5d8-442d-9908-5e82db765d28?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)” before their posts to get better reach, which later became an official feature. If it had not been a term like “Chinese spy,” TikTok would have likely co opted it, just like Meta.

A key trend signal this year was people creating fake invoices, online chats, and fabricated facts after Meta [removed](https://substack.com/redirect/bddff337-d436-445b-adad-95aef2f3c245?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) fact checkers. Even though some studies this year proved that community notes work, they have not been implemented widely enough on Instagram or Facebook.

Lastly, the [Walmart “Birkin” bag](https://substack.com/redirect/37e734bf-74e5-4e24-9406-684be686cbe1?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) was an ethics test for everyone, not just brands. The same people who supported Chinese bag manufacturers also criticized dupe culture when the clothing quality for the upcoming series American Love Story was not good enough. Dupe culture never truly went away. People only factor in ethics when they are not being influenced by scarcity.

In all of this chaos, Bad Bunny’s “[DtMF](https://substack.com/redirect/755bdded-e548-484e-999f-e9fe5954eb8b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)” became the song of the culture, inviting everyone to share their stories, with the Montblanc chair as the symbol.

Now this should have been a timely reminder that we need to be careful about how much trust and faith we put in our social platforms – but instead, we got heavier systemic anxieties, from political fragmentation to increasing economic pressures, which showed up in platform fragmentation brainrot culture.

##### RIP Namecore. Long Live?

**Jaskaran:** The 2022–24 era was filled with people, trend forecasters, and marketers renaming old trends as new, and everything slightly intellectual was labeled as XYZ-ification, maxxing, core, and so on. This year was not completely free of renaming either. We got fiber maxxing, and I cannot recall much else.

What replaced namecore is unfinished sentences and, in a way, the colonization of existing words. Yes, I am still mad about gooning getting an entirely new meaning this year. Focus.

This year, people were not looking for [full discourse](https://substack.com/redirect/66752e76-fcab-42ed-accf-ec0c8e6f9e3c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) to be built around what they like. Because, guess what, even a simple picture of you sitting on your couch can turn into rage filled discourse. Regular people went [short](https://substack.com/redirect/5fd1eae0-f7cf-41c7-a38b-e2b19846c28c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). 

Almost forgot this was the whole point → Girl, whatever → propaganda I’m not falling for → Just a Little Something To Take the Edge Off → man of the year → Holy AIR BALL….

You may think these are just random trends people engaged with to escape actual discussions around propaganda, men, lack of friends, and nostalgia. But these trends are born out of individual experiences people live through on social media. Most people sincerely do not like the debate culture of comment sections. It always turns into a clusterfuck.

That is why people have self censored and now communicate mostly through content, which gave birth to these kinds of trends. People are not trying to define what they feel anymore. They are tired of doing so after years of explaining themselves.

Who is to blame? Doomscrolling? I would say everything. People do not feel like they are given the space to breathe and think. So [namecore](https://substack.com/redirect/a4258e52-4b7d-4b48-9598-f94490c20a81?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) is dead. It is whatever core.

**Mara:** Cottagecore; goblin mode; office siren – in the first half of the 2020s, every aesthetic and subculture got its own name.

This year, it seems we stopped pretending micro-aesthetics are meaningful cultural movements. Instead, we got heavier systemic anxieties, from political fragmentation to increasing economic pressures, which showed up in platform fragmentation brainrot culture. substitute.

Basically: 2025 is the year we stopped trying to name our feelings and got deep instead – and shifted towards serious analysis (though with some lighter moments as well).

##### February: Resist or Takeover

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!GZzY!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7823aaeb-6f6e-4afe-8cdd-860475ddfe29_1920x1080.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/7f5ed0a9-8d0b-4db2-a5cd-ab47c3806be3?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)**Jaskaran**: After TikTok came back and Donald Trump gave another show of power, the sentiment of resistance vs. takeover was set up. Creators and users on TikTok felt there was [a difference](https://substack.com/redirect/41f5e35a-1141-4345-9ab6-fcc7e9a82e4a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) between pre and post ban, so even more self censoring occurred to make sure the message was delivered. All of that led to “[cute winter boots](https://substack.com/redirect/63e595c7-9dd3-4d70-97bc-597f3b6227e8?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)” and many other terms becoming new methods to signal support and spread messages through SEO.

As of now, keywords are the way many people support their GoFundMe pages and help others in difficult circumstances. People understand that the algorithm treats them as keywords and content, so everyone plays the game.

I think people on [Substack](https://substack.com/redirect/dff2cacd-ad94-4e60-b051-f7d4054ca3df?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) and at Sephora need to learn that if you want to resist a takeover by brands, you have to think about distribution first. Despite all the panic, neither the [Sephora kids](https://substack.com/redirect/723cdf8b-17e9-4e68-b3a7-2af2b9d58830?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) nor the [brands on Substack](https://substack.com/redirect/7ccaa845-d2cb-4f24-ae2d-3066ffc9b9c5?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) made their exits as they were told to. Platforms want this behavior, consumerist culture facilitates it, and the masses are the only ones with the distribution power to stop it.

The [tarot card trend](https://substack.com/redirect/9d9d4ddf-7aaf-49ad-a236-f4e173f567cd?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) came full circle last month with [Dior’s Jonathan Anderson](https://substack.com/redirect/2ce3a540-c433-4958-a3d5-7e6f25c5fd9c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) sharing his cards. Meanwhile, the [millennial burger joint](https://substack.com/redirect/482d4160-a463-4194-a00e-975310719ed9?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) was possibly the last big “millennials are cringe” moment, as the rest of the year transitioned into Gen Z developing a fondness for millennials and [the era they lived in](https://substack.com/redirect/4cf5f524-8e85-4e65-8562-505c1c56e687?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g).

Lastly, [Kendrick’s Super Bowl performance](https://substack.com/redirect/52d1f605-3edc-4de1-8f7d-9d9615c05f37?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) embodied the whole month. The game is rigged, but you still have to play it.

##### The Silent Resistance vs. Algorithmic Acceleration

The story that flew under the radar and came back in another form was the active cultural resistance taking place within certain online communities versus algorithms and AI accelerating the pace at which niche memes and trends go mainstream.

The [March Meme Drought](https://substack.com/redirect/51ee5411-e194-45b1-8c07-bd80eb3e85e9?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), which created a sense of panic around no original memes going mainstream or even staying niche, was the direct result of two things.

First, algorithmic acceleration. It used to be that memes stayed niche within certain communities and gradually moved from one platform to another. That is no longer true. Memes are constantly being scraped for Reddit karma, follower growth by theme pages, and often co opted by groups in ways that hinder their future life cycle. So even if [the meme ecosystem](https://substack.com/redirect/68388b13-f703-41f9-986f-14ad42df26bd?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) is not empty of original ideas, algorithms either speedrun everything and demand more memes, or content aggregators try to profit from original work. The pipeline is corrupt.

Second, silent resistance. What originally started as a movement called [Juggtok](https://substack.com/redirect/b9e335c1-e36f-4b76-bc9d-53023a1d614b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) to keep the use of AAVE and Black culture within specific communities eventually became a broader movement across many groups. A month prior to the meme drought, the Juggtok community received more attention with a proposal to replace the 💔 with 🥀, as the broken heart emoji had become too mainstream and uncool. Their attempt went mainstream, along with the very idea of keeping things niche.

TikTok users began creating alternative and constantly changing terms to keep communities niche. All of this hurt the meme and trend pipeline. Meanwhile, platforms like X and Threads have become deeply divided, to the point that once one group co opts a new meme, others deliberately ignore it.

The social media ecosystem that helped create mainstream versions of Pepe the Frog and Wojak is disappearing. Platforms are more isolated, and people care less about reference and culture. Every meme becomes just another piece of content.

P.S. The TikTok ban becoming the ultimate meme in January likely also contributed to the meme drought.

P.P.S. This entire trend may be the reason everyone is planning [a great meme reset](https://substack.com/redirect/85441510-91d9-46d7-9d50-bb2329073ef0?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) for January 1, 2026.

##### March: Revenge of Nerds

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!m2xq!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb568eef6-e53d-47c0-ac5d-19367c39020a.tif)](https://substack.com/redirect/a8a83ad4-f7e1-4874-88a0-085082b2b14e?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)**Jaskaran**: The release of Ezra Klein and Derek Thompson’s book [Abundance](https://substack.com/redirect/dfb26834-fdac-40c6-907a-e4e824489d23?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) created a broad debate around acceleration vs. renovation. I am simplifying here, but the strategy they discuss, creating an abundance of certain things, is what brands have been doing for the past few years. Everyone keeps stacking one tactic on top of another without making real changes to strategy.

That is why the release of the book [created a divide](https://substack.com/redirect/5630788c-9242-4561-8a05-49451dd07244?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) not just between liberals and leftists, but [across the board](https://substack.com/redirect/d63b1aea-769b-41cd-8988-d233cfb3c590?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). Everyone was divided, and discussions around the book continue to this day. The system and algorithms clearly reward abundance, while culture often resists or pushes back against it.

OpenAI introduced 4o image generation, and suddenly everyone was [ghiblifying](https://substack.com/redirect/3179d2ea-334a-4cde-8c8b-c1d48b9b2264?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) their images. [Hayao Miyazaki](https://substack.com/redirect/f559906d-450b-4b6c-ab82-5e902166401d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) was furious, but who cares? Everyone from Miyazaki to Wes Anderson is being replaced by AI.

This accelerated another debate about what art is and what it means in this century. The author has been “dead” for decades, thanks to media theory. AI art asks whether there is even a point in having the viewer assign meaning to what they see. 

In a way, this has always been at the core of artistic endeavors: You create to express yourself. BUT!! That argument falls apart as soon as people on public platforms are asked to view and critique the work. When creators of AI art do not highlight the meaning behind their work, and do not value what audiences see in it, the result becomes meaningless slop. 

This argument really only applies to people engaging thoughtfully with AI as a tool. What we saw in March, and later this year with Sora 2, was people trying to make themselves feel better by generating new slop trained on real art. It felt like a kind of revenge by people who feel neglected by a society that does not allow most people to engage with art in a meaningful way.

**Mara**: What happens when traditional coping mechanisms fail? It appears we turn to magical thinking – including commercialized witchery (for political but also personal gains). Some treat it as a joke, others very seriously, but generally with the underlying idea we don’t really understand how anything works and most cause and effect correlations are imagined rather than actual. 

This can be liberating but also dangerous since it creates space for false prophets (or witches and warlocks) to control the narrative.

##### April: The Panic of It All

[Skinnytok](https://substack.com/redirect/531e5c19-6016-412b-ba50-1196d9be546c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) got banned, but the conversation around everyone getting skinny and the use of GLP-1s is still alive. Just this past month, [Lizzo](https://substack.com/redirect/0e69e911-9067-4cae-bfb6-ef436798b1b6?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) joined Substack to talk about the topic, which got a mixed reaction, and [Jameela Jamil](https://substack.com/redirect/0e81f91c-a527-4f4a-989b-53e16ce33f8a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) shared her thoughts on both the issue and Lizzo’s write up.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!in6e!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F448f3a45-9166-460b-9a6a-2a083eed7e40.tif)](https://substack.com/redirect/7867467d-ecbe-4d7c-88a2-eef2293a76aa?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)Jaskaran: This month was kind of the perfect example of how things would play out if the internet went to war next week. Similar to [Rapture Tok](https://substack.com/redirect/ad197aac-3d9d-4ad7-b381-db707745bec6?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), you would have people predicting the end, and most would lean into that kind of community participation for short term gains and validation.

Creators of [AI slop](https://substack.com/redirect/943eeb93-867a-4a9d-bbfa-e929d4bb6c6b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) would be prompting ChatGPT to create videos in the style of Kurzgesagt to inform fellow citizens about the situation. The internal fetishes of oppressors, creators, and general audiences would lead to scenes similar to [One Battle After Another](https://substack.com/redirect/b57c0939-a9da-4d32-a96b-8ade2e9cae9c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). There would be [cancellations of TV shows](https://substack.com/redirect/0cdd2153-bd2d-4d9c-b266-6d0bd3828a39?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) to protect national interests and keep things limited.

False prophets, similar to [Peter Thiel](https://substack.com/redirect/4d624e41-1120-49f0-bd0b-78482d1036c2?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), would emerge, along with debates about who the Antichrist is. In the middle of all this, influencers would be trying to escape the chaos, and the [new dating app](https://substack.com/redirect/eff354ec-16c6-4e62-8bcd-794e324a9ffc?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) would be Facebook.

Jokes aside, September is hard to describe, but it is possibly the month every strategist should study. 

---

**Mara**: Of course everything is political – but this is the month it really came to a head. Specifically with Gen Z political protests and culture focused on free speech and resistance at the movies.

BUT it’s also the month when everything became religious, or at least about religion (thanks, Thiel) – and technology worked as the best way to spread religion, getting us all talking over the rapture.

##### October: Socialisation vs. 6-7

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!_2R-!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F49834209-e208-4b90-8b34-9ce69cbdd05b.tif)](https://substack.com/redirect/42066db6-4301-4387-ab7c-1499bc3b0d6a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)**Jaskaran**: I remember coming across the [6-7 meme](https://substack.com/redirect/b0e4406b-b24b-4339-8436-18a25c786cf6?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) last year, and it kind of died out. What helped the meme make a comeback was TikTok edits. So far this year, edit culture has been the most influential force in reviving old memes and keeping certain types of sports history and lore relevant. The “[my mom is kind of homeless](https://substack.com/redirect/29b07105-c947-4322-85a8-e28087d0313b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)” iShowSpeed meme also made a comeback thanks to viral edits.

Sports brands have embraced this segment of online culture. The [Orlando Magic team](https://substack.com/redirect/f75225be-8d9e-47b2-9a63-e674298aae03?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) is still very good at TikTok edits, while other brands that jumped on the edits trend in 2024 have moved on a bit. Don’t sleep on TikTok edits.

“[Pudding mit Gabel](https://substack.com/redirect/767de168-0f33-408b-afbb-92725f5dfe0b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)” in Germany became a socialization trend for Gen Z. Some creators in the U.S. also organized a version of that event, but the real moment came in November, with people in NYC [smoking a cigarette](https://substack.com/redirect/d17809ca-4d26-4318-a403-ea5dbdc13bfa?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) with a 75 year old man named Bob Terry. 

The algorithm also brought people together through the [Group 7 trend](https://substack.com/redirect/e9f317c2-d9c7-4646-a29b-e4f273c8d6bb?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), which was bound to happen, as earlier this year we saw another trend just like it. I can’t recall the name of that trend, but random accounts were creating images of [bears](https://substack.com/redirect/dc7598ac-de13-4a12-99f2-5eaa5ef21028?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), [fruits](https://substack.com/redirect/954acac0-4733-478f-b270-2cf4be50bffd?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), and other objects and saying that if you saw it on your feed, you were part of a specific group. A couple of Chinese astrology creators did something similar in January.

I think this tactic will be used heavily by brands in 2026, as they create more content to segment new, old, and core fan audiences by assigning new traits.

Media reported that the rise of ICE and GLP-1s has led to changes in consumer behavior. Many [Hispanic consumers](https://substack.com/redirect/0ebac2da-870c-4198-b8c0-8aea5d05a5ba?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) are retreating from public life, forgoing regular shopping trips and spending less to ensure financial security. Similarly, GLP-1 users are also changing their buying habits. A recent survey showed [grocery spending declined 6% to 8%](https://substack.com/redirect/7a9ca4cb-65c8-4e9e-8e3d-9638392b0fe3?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) within the first 12 months in households where a GLP-1 user was the primary food purchaser, a sharp contraction for a category that typically shifts slowly and predictably. At the same time, total household spending fell by only 2% to 3%, pointing to a reallocation of those savings toward other categories.

Both behavioral changes are influenced by very different forces, but they belong together in the sense that this whole year has been filled with articles claiming consumers are struggling, only to point out that they are still buying.

[Becca Bloom](https://substack.com/redirect/0b72afc5-3d9f-498f-80c6-e7409fabd73d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), [phone bans](https://substack.com/redirect/18f44ee1-86b6-4ed8-a55a-8158c21cb6f3?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), and “[boyfriends are embarrassing](https://substack.com/redirect/73e3dfd3-bbc4-4b00-aa48-365ce9843735?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)” were all topics that had more to do with the content ecosystem and the parasocial relationships audiences have with creators and their devices. Yes, the discourse around them was everywhere. But the underlying question was the same: how should viewers and creators conduct themselves? Do we [applaud](https://substack.com/redirect/97a7d12d-eaa4-4a72-9903-d766342b9efa?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) a wealthy creator like Becca for having taste? After all, 2025 was also the year everyone debated taste. Do we blame the phones or the system, and so on.

---

**Mara**: September signaled community, seriously built in unserious ways though shared rituals (Pudding mit Gabel), shared language (six seeeven), shared belonging (Group 7), and shared debates. The backlash against “this is how you do rich correctly” influencers like Becca Bloom is part of the same story: she (and creators like her) worked (genuinely) hard to manufacture community, but ultimately weren’t able to truly on it.

Ultimately, audiences shifted from mass followings to smaller groups where connection feels more equitable and mutual – at least for a while. And of course phones and digital platforms played a huge role in all of this since they both make shared meaning happen but also isolate us from other people.

##### November: Gamble or Goon

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!cHkQ!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6963f2ce-d5c1-42e7-aff9-8183a245973e.tif)](https://substack.com/redirect/1ab7312a-d96b-42b7-820f-3ad585fd361a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)**Jaskaran**: Rosalía calling her new album a “[human album](https://substack.com/redirect/4a79f7ae-7f32-4574-aec1-710193ebd1c3?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)” and explicitly stating there is no AI involved. [OpenAI](https://substack.com/redirect/b2ab5d72-bec9-4e6e-a886-a566a20a797f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) doing humane marketing, and Anthropic going even further, using studies and warnings about [cybercrime](https://substack.com/redirect/7eca6d86-e992-4e3f-9859-d1d42b8dcf60?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) as marketing and getting into pop up culture.

All of that is only 50% of the [anti-AI marketing movement](https://substack.com/redirect/bc1c10de-8ef3-4445-90a9-5c48b5bbf92c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). The rest is just brands engaging with art and genuinely good marketing. People will call your brand anti-AI if you create something good, like Apple did.

[The Goon Squad](https://substack.com/redirect/f340ad3f-54d2-42a1-9053-0b4721235e55?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) and prediction markets are just two sides of the same coin. People are looking for quick dopamine and success by gambling on everything. The article also ended with this premise: isn’t everything gooning these days? Because when generations feel the system is set against them, they take all the chances they can.

In November and December, both [Polymarket](https://substack.com/redirect/811e46b9-2661-4d91-8762-918775dbb7c2?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) and [Kalshi](https://substack.com/redirect/3bf47488-8340-439d-b497-f1971fe6aff2?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) became more mainstream and are now integrated with CNN, Google, and other platforms. These platforms are partnering with them not only because they want access to prediction markets. Polymarket is often used as the [primary tweet quoted](https://substack.com/redirect/e4d52be3-f3c8-4683-b17f-ce456f68a6c8?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) during breaking news on Twitter, or people screenshot it and share it on other platforms. These markets are also in the media business.

[Wicked](https://substack.com/redirect/d4975140-fe1d-4f84-9a79-e7a2ed8553a8?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) was the biggest IP collaboration play of the year. My prediction is that it might have been the last IP play involving large teams from all the brands involved. Something like an [OpenAI-Disney deal](https://substack.com/redirect/f7061bb0-76f4-4e91-b271-62c3b860d5ef?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) could simplify how IP collaborations are done. Disney already has manufacturing figured out, and with AI they could easily sell the entire collaboration, from licensing to manufacturing, to the ideal brand with money.

Lastly, and on a similar note, what we are seeing with “[kirkification](https://substack.com/redirect/43767ae5-bdf6-4c6e-b80e-a7865e6c1170?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)” memes is only the beginning. The new model of personalized content and memes through AI is already set up. Just like Giphy is filled with a small group of creators performing gestures for every keyword imaginable, we will see a library of memes with influencer faces in 2026 and brand faces in 2027. Everything personalized.

---

**Mara**: November crystallized another key discourse of 2025: we’re operating under ambient risk, with everything the logic of gambling – wagering all our resources (time, energy, money) for the chance of a dopamine hit or a moment of relief. 

Wicked: For Good did show that film releases still work as talkable cultural milestones – but while for Wicked, the brand collabs dominated the conversation, this release seemed to lose control of the narrative which became less about the film and more about the actors and their relationship and weight – and the general skinny discourse.

##### December: The Remix

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!pJ2d!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F509d44d1-7769-4782-823f-82f47c965dd6.tif)](https://substack.com/redirect/117d253a-93c4-4f70-9b9b-7b1208c4989d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)**Jaskaran**: Timothée Chalamet’s [speech](https://substack.com/redirect/6dc6b0ad-7845-4b27-abd1-e73a33b62340?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) earlier this year became the ultimate strategy deck image for marketers discussing the *cringe/irony to sincerity cycle*. The speech was applauded as sincere, and now [Timmy’s podcast appearance](https://substack.com/redirect/f3d923f8-aaeb-485d-9168-27b54bd2e467?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) is being ridiculed as somewhat cringe. We came full circle.

Another event that came full circle was the idea of having a normal life or house. It started with people [being appalled](https://substack.com/redirect/5845fb03-86f5-4bc1-8a8b-af6c0ff41d2a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) by Architectural Digest homes and influencer lifestyles, but it is ending with [a full debate](https://substack.com/redirect/4534fc48-5cb7-4ad8-9a8a-feb031b4d38d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) about friends enjoying [a snow day](https://substack.com/redirect/d90d4d5d-33cc-4725-ad3f-3b54cc3d8c27?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) together, leading to the conclusion that normal is not that normal.

The [Warner Bros Netflix deal](https://substack.com/redirect/b07b2db6-fff2-4d2a-b2ad-3d9ad65f8248?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) caused panic around the future of cinema, and my take is that we need to leave the term [relatable content in the past](https://substack.com/redirect/9e03ae32-2572-48a8-843e-98cab2110297?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). It allows platforms like Netflix and YouTube to rebrand their actions as consumer friendly.

Lastly, the [Intermarché Christmas campaign](https://substack.com/redirect/764a1776-467e-416e-9b6b-7e280198cac0?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) was the best of 2025, and the behind the scenes of the campaign going viral is a trend signal that people want brands to highlight their efforts. The process is also the story.

**Mara**: Some things are genuinely slowing down, which means Chalamet’s film promo actually had space to cut through. Culture started looking both forward (Pantone’s Color of the Year, which faced all the derision) and backwards (manufactured nostalgia for a Ralph Lauren Christmas that never was), in addition to the start of the normal self-optimization discourse. 

All setting us up for yearning for the past and bracing for the future – but with huge potential to start 2026 on a note of reset and optimism.

##### **Big shifts of the year**

EVERYTHING *IS* THAT DEEP

We stopped naming vibes and started decoding what everything is – something with critical thinking, at other times overreaching.

EMBRACING THE UNHINGED

The bizarre became aspirational – a tiny rebellion against systems that demand coherence.

THE GREAT UNSHAMENING

Emotional softness made a comeback – in part because perfection culture finally bored us into quitting.

ANNOYANCE AS THE PRICE OF COMMUNITY

We realised community isn’t magic; it’s tolerating minor irritation in exchange for not feeling completely alone.

ENTERTAIN OR DIE

If it doesn’t amuse us, is it worth existing? (But also: will “boring” things have more authority?).

WE YEARN TO LEARN

Everyone wants to learn, but gently – preferably from a charismatic creator who knows the difference between clarity and condescension (but also creating ample room for the less well-informed and intentioned). And especially TikTok continues to be the channel through which we learn about other cultures.

EVERYTHING IS A GAMBLE

When nothing is stable enough to take at face value, every aspect of our lives can become a risk portfolio.

A COUNTER-DISCOURSE FOR EVERY DISCOURSE

Every opinion now comes pre-packaged with its own backlash, and every trend with its own opposition – the internet refuses to let any take live unchallenged.

WE CAN’T OUTSMART THE SYSTEM *THROUGH* THE SYSTEM

Many of us genuinely seemed to believe we could at the start of 2025 – but as the year comes to an end, the choices seem to be complacency or frustration. BUT: how can we take a different tack for 2026?

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">The story that flew under the radar and came back in another form was the active cultural resistance taking place within certain online communities versus algorithms and AI accelerating the pace at which niche memes and trends go mainstream.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>The </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/51ee5411-e194-45b1-8c07-bd80eb3e85e9?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">March Meme Drought</a><span>, which created a sense of panic around no original memes going mainstream or even staying niche, was the direct result of two things.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>First, algorithmic acceleration. It used to be that memes stayed niche within certain communities and gradually moved from one platform to another. That is no longer true. Memes are constantly being scraped for Reddit karma, follower growth by theme pages, and often co opted by groups in ways that hinder their future life cycle. So even if </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/68388b13-f703-41f9-986f-14ad42df26bd?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">the meme ecosystem</a><span> is not empty of original ideas, algorithms either speedrun everything and demand more memes, or content aggregators try to profit from original work. The pipeline is corrupt.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>Second, silent resistance. What originally started as a movement called </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/b9e335c1-e36f-4b76-bc9d-53023a1d614b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Juggtok</a><span> to keep the use of AAVE and Black culture within specific communities eventually became a broader movement across many groups. A month prior to the meme drought, the Juggtok community received more attention with a proposal to replace the 💔 with 🥀, as the broken heart emoji had become too mainstream and uncool. Their attempt went mainstream, along with the very idea of keeping things niche.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">TikTok users began creating alternative and constantly changing terms to keep communities niche. All of this hurt the meme and trend pipeline. Meanwhile, platforms like X and Threads have become deeply divided, to the point that once one group co opts a new meme, others deliberately ignore it.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">The social media ecosystem that helped create mainstream versions of Pepe the Frog and Wojak is disappearing. Platforms are more isolated, and people care less about reference and culture. Every meme becomes just another piece of content.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">P.S. The TikTok ban becoming the ultimate meme in January likely also contributed to the meme drought.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>Media reported that the rise of ICE and GLP-1s has led to changes in consumer behavior. <p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Both behavioral changes are influenced by very different forces, but they belong together in the sense that this whole year has been filled with articles claiming consumers are struggling, only to point out that they are still buying.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">All setting us up for yearning for the past and bracing for the future – but with huge potential to start 2026 on a note of reset and optimism.</p>

<h3 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.375em;"><strong>Big shifts of the year</strong></h3>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>EVERYTHING </span><em>IS</em><span> THAT DEEP</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">We stopped naming vibes and started decoding what everything is – something with critical thinking, at other times overreaching.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">EMBRACING THE UNHINGED</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">The bizarre became aspirational – a tiny rebellion against systems that demand coherence.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">THE GREAT UNSHAMENING</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Emotional softness made a comeback – in part because perfection culture finally bored us into quitting.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">ANNOYANCE AS THE PRICE OF COMMUNITY</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">We realised community isn’t magic; it’s tolerating minor irritation in exchange for not feeling completely alone.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">ENTERTAIN OR DIE</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">If it doesn’t amuse us, is it worth existing? (But also: will “boring” things have more authority?).</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">WE YEARN TO LEARN</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Everyone wants to learn, but gently – preferably from a charismatic creator who knows the difference between clarity and condescension (but also creating ample room for the less well-informed and intentioned). And especially TikTok continues to be the channel through which we learn about other cultures.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">EVERYTHING IS A GAMBLE</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">When nothing is stable enough to take at face value, every aspect of our lives can become a risk portfolio.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">A COUNTER-DISCOURSE FOR EVERY DISCOURSE</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Every opinion now comes pre-packaged with its own backlash, and every trend with its own opposition – the internet refuses to let any take live unchallenged.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>WE CAN’T OUTSMART THE SYSTEM </span><em>THROUGH</em><span> THE SYSTEM</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Many of us genuinely seemed to believe we could at the start of 2025 – but as the year comes to an end, the choices seem to be complacency or frustration. BUT: how can we take a different tack for 2026?</p>
