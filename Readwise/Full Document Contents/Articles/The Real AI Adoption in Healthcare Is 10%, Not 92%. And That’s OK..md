# The Real AI Adoption in Healthcare Is 10%, Not 92%. And That’s OK.

![rw-book-cover](https://substackcdn.com/icons/substack/apple-touch-icon-1024x1024.png)

## Metadata
- Author: [[Sergei from AI Health Uncut]]
- Full Title: The Real AI Adoption in Healthcare Is 10%, Not 92%. And That’s OK.
- Category: #articles
- Summary: McKinsey’s headline 92% AI adoption is misleading because their definition counts any regular AI use (even in HR or marketing).  
A broader U.S. Census measure shows only about 10% of healthcare organizations actually use AI to produce goods or services.  
The gap comes from different definitions and sampling issues, so 10% is a more realistic estimate of clinical AI adoption.

So let’s winnow the wheat from the chaff…

#### **Reason #1 Why AI Adoption in Healthcare is ~10%, Not 92%: Definition**

Let’s look at the footnotes.

Here is **McKinsey’s definition, versus the U.S. Census definition**:

* [McKinsey](https://substack.com/redirect/05bcc815-604d-4a2f-8b9a-cc537cc31e45?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) asks survey respondents whether they are regularly using AI in ***at least one business function***: knowledge management, marketing and sales, HR, risk, legal, compliance, and so on.
* [The U.S. Census](https://substack.com/redirect/f062e225-9fb9-47cb-ad11-1f61b0d4d78f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) asks survey respondents whether the business is using AI to ***produce*** goods or services.

I hope everyone understands this is a massive difference in definition. It is the difference between a company where someone in HR uses ChatGPT to do their job, versus a company that actually uses AI to manufacture, deliver, and service what it sells.

It is night and day. That is why you get a gap like 10% versus 92%.

#### **Reason #2 Why AI Adoption in Healthcare is ~10%, Not 92%: Sample Size**

Sample size. That damn sample size is always “on the way.” Why can’t we just ask a couple of our most trusted clients a few questions over beers, confirm what we already know, and call it a day. 😂

I’ve spent a big part of my academic career working on *effective* sample size estimation. That is the “true” sample size your stats actually need when the i.i.d. assumption does not hold.

I’ll cover this in a separate post, because I genuinely believe it is a topic every clinical researcher should know as well as the palm of their hand. I’ve also written and published a full paper on this called “[Advancing AI in healthcare: A comprehensive review of best practices](https://substack.com/redirect/3810422a-3912-4bee-a884-b65d3a979a4c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g).” Check it out.

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>In March 2024, </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/f062e225-9fb9-47cb-ad11-1f61b0d4d78f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: rgb(54,55,55);text-decoration: underline;">the U.S. Census Business Trends and Outlook Survey, or BTOS</a><span>, found that </span><strong>about 5%</strong><span> of U.S. healthcare organizations were using AI, with </span><strong>about 7%</strong><span> expecting to use AI within six months. If that expectation translated into real deployments, and if adoption then climbed from about 7% by late 2024 to </span><strong>about 10%</strong><span> now, that is how I get to current AI usage in healthcare today. By the way, in BTOS terms, “healthcare organizations” are defined by the NAICS sector “Health Care and Social Assistance” (NAICS 62).</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">So let’s winnow the wheat from the chaff…</p>
<h2 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.625em;"><strong>Reason #1 Why AI Adoption in Healthcare is ~10%, Not 92%: Definition</strong></h2>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Let’s look at the footnotes.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>Here is </span><strong>McKinsey’s definition, versus the U.S. Census definition</strong><span>:</span></p>
<ul style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;">
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;"><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/05bcc815-604d-4a2f-8b9a-cc537cc31e45?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: rgb(54,55,55);text-decoration: underline;">McKinsey</a><span> asks survey respondents whether they are regularly using AI in </span><em><strong>at least one business function</strong></em><span>: knowledge management, marketing and sales, HR, risk, legal, compliance, and so on.</span></p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;"><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/f062e225-9fb9-47cb-ad11-1f61b0d4d78f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: rgb(54,55,55);text-decoration: underline;">The U.S. Census</a><span> asks survey respondents whether the business is using AI to </span><em><strong>produce</strong></em><span> goods or services.</span></p></li>
</ul>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">I hope everyone understands this is a massive difference in definition. It is the difference between a company where someone in HR uses ChatGPT to do their job, versus a company that actually uses AI to manufacture, deliver, and service what it sells.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">It is night and day. That is why you get a gap like 10% versus 92%.</p>
<h2 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.625em;"><strong>Reason #2 Why AI Adoption in Healthcare is ~10%, Not 92%: Sample Size</strong></h2>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Sample size. That damn sample size is always “on the way.” Why can’t we just ask a couple of our most trusted clients a few questions over beers, confirm what we already know, and call it a day. 😂</p>

That is the “true” sample size your stats actually need when the i.i.d. assumption does not hold.</span></p>
