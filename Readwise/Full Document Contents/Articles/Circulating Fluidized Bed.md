A circulating fluidized bed is defined as a system where air is blown rapidly through a mixture of sand and fuel, causing the mixture to behave like a fluid and allowing for efficient combustion and enhanced heat transfer. This setup enables the separation and recycling of sand and fuel, making it adaptable to various fuel types and improving combustion control.

Circulating [fluidized beds](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/fluidized-bed) are popular for large-scale combustion operations, and they are also gaining attention for gasification of carbonaceous feedstocks. Circulating fluidized beds utilize the entire height of tall vessels for gas–solids contacting, with no distinct interface between bed and freeboard. The corresponding flow regime is usually fast [fluidization](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/fluidization) for combustion and gasification of solid fuels. Operating conditions, riser geometry, [hydrodynamics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/hydrodynamics), heat transfer, solids feed and particle recirculation all have significant influence on the operation and effectiveness of the system. This chapter reviews some of the most important [hydrodynamic](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/hydrodynamics) properties of circulating fluidized beds that are relevant in CFB gas–solid combustion and gasification reactions, with particular attention to results for columns of [rectangular cross](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/rectangular-cross) section. Gas and solid mixing are summarized also, and a brief survey of relevant heat transfer aspects is provided.

A circulating [fluidized bed](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/fluidized-bed) (CFB) is a fluidized bed system that includes a riser and a down-comer with the [solid particles](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/solid-particle) circulating between them. The riser is operated in a fast [fluidization](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/fluidization) regime, with solids carried over from top and returned to bottom of the riser via a [standpipe](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/standpipe) and feeding or control device. The operating variables for a CFB system include both the [gas flow](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/gas-flow) rate and solids [circulation rate](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/circulation-rate), in contrast to the gas flow rate only in a dense-phase fluidized bed system.

CFB systems have been widely used in the [petrochemical industry](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/petrochemical-industry) (e.g., in fluid [catalytic cracking](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/catalytic-cracking) (FCC)) and the utility industry (e.g., in coal combustion). Other commercial applications of the CFB systems include [maleic anhydride](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/maleic-anhydride) production from *n*-butane and Fischer–Tropsch synthesis of [liquid fuels](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/liquid-fuels) from [carbon monoxide](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/carbon-monoxide) and hydrogen. Typical applications are summarized in Table 8.2. Operating conditions of CFB systems can be significantly different, depending on process applications. For example, for [coal combustion](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/coal-combustion), [gas velocity](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/gas-velocity) and [solids flow](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/solids-flow) rate are typically 5–8 m/s and less than 40 kg/m2·s, respectively. For FCC, on the other hand, gas velocity at the riser exit and solids flow rate are 15–20 m/s and higher than 300 kg/m2·s, respectively.

The advantages of CFBs relative to bubbling beds include:

High gas throughputs

Limited backmixing of gas

Long and controllable residence time of particles

Uniform temperature profile without “hot” spots

Flexibility in handling particles of widely differing sizes, densities, and shapes

Effective contacting between gas and particles

Opportunity for complementary operations, such as catalyst regeneration in the return loop

However, CFBs also have the following disadvantages:

Very tall vessel required

Substantial backmixing of solid particles

Internals not viable because of wear and attribution

Heat transfer less favorable than that in dense-phase fluidization

Lateral gradients can be significant

Losses of particles due to entrainment

A [CFB reactor](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/fluidized-bed-reactor) mainly consists of four parts: riser, gas–solid separator, down-comer, and solids flow control device, as shown in Fig. 8.11. In the riser, gas and particles commonly flow cocurrently upward, exit at the top, and flow into the gas–solid separator. The separated particles then flow to the down-comer and return to the riser.

The riser is the main component of a CFB reactor system. In comparing with a [fixed bed reactor](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/fixed-bed-reactor) the design of the entrance and exit of a riser reactor has very significant influences on gas and particle flow behaviors, and therefore is critical for achieving the desired reactor performance. Cyclones are usually used for gas–solid separation, and their efficiency can affect the [particle size distribution](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/particle-size-distribution) and solids circulation rate in the system. The down-comer can be either a large reservoir that can help regulating solid recycling rate, as shown in Fig. 8.11A and B, or be a standpipe that simply serve as a pathway for solids to return to the riser, as shown in Fig. 8.11C and D. For reaction applications the down-comer can serve as a [heat exchanger](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/heat-exchanger) or as a catalyst [regenerator](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/regenerator).

To smoothly operate a CFB system, it is very important to effectively control the solids circulation rate to the riser. The [solid flow](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/solids-flow) control device has two major functions: one is preventing gas in riser flows to the down-comer, and the other is controlling solid circulation rate. Both [mechanical valves](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/mechanical-valve) (Fig. 8.12(A) and (D)) and nonmechanical valves (Fig. 8.12(B) and (C)) are used.

As mentioned earlier the riser of a CFB system is typically operated in a fast fluidization regime, and therefore the [fluid flow characteristics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/fluid-flow-characteristic) of fast fluidization beds need to be considered when developing a mathematic model of CFB reactor. Based on experimental observations at [macroscale](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/macroscale), along the [axial direction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/axial-direction) a fast fluidized bed will have a dense region at the bottom and a dilute region on the top. In the radical direction the solid movement will form a core-annulus structure, i.e., a lean core surrounded by a denser annulus in the [radial direction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/radial-direction). At [mesoscale](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/mesoscale) some solids will form particle clusters. Due to the heterogeneity at both macro- and mesoscales, a complete characterization of the [hydrodynamics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/hydrodynamics) of a CFB requires detailed description of the void fraction and [velocity profiles](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/velocity-distribution) in both axial and radial directions.

There are a number of mathematical models describing the flow pattern in a CFB. Models commonly contain a series of unproven assumptions and empirical constants. [Mechanistic models](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/mechanistic-model) that have intermediate complexity but can capture major relevant features are often the most useful for engineering design, optimization, and control. In the longer term, more sophisticated models, e.g., those based on multiphase [computational fluid dynamic](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/computational-fluid-dynamics) codes, are likely to have increasing impact. Here the one-dimensional model and core–annulus model are introduced.

Li and Kwauk proposed a one-dimensional model to predict the axial profile of average solids volume fraction based on equilibrium between diffusion and segregation of clusters. In this model, as shown in Fig. 8.12, the fast fluidized bed consists of a dilute suspension phase and a cluster phase. The dense clusters have a volume fraction of *f*, and the solids concentration in dense clusters is 1−*ε*a. The dilute continuum has a volume fraction of 1−*f*, and the solids concentration in it is 1−*ε*\*. The clusters move upward through a diffusive mechanism from a relatively dense region of the lower section, and then they tend to fall back to the lower region by virtue of buoyancy when arriving at a higher region, where the average bed density is lower. Dynamic equilibrium calls for equality of the diffusion and buoyancy fluxes.

The term ‘circulating fluidized bed’, commonly abbreviated CFB, originated in the 1970s when [fluidized bed](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/fluidized-bed) calcining of aluminum trihydrate and [phosphate rock](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/phosphate-rock) was carried out by Reh (1971) at [gas velocities](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/gas-velocity) well in excess of those normally practiced in stationary fluidized bed operation [fluidization](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/fluidization), such that there was a continuous decrease in suspension density with height, rather than a distinct bed surface. The [ore particles](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/ore-particle) were circulated continuously in a flow loop, with particles captured by cyclones returned immediately to the base of a vertical shaft, referred to here, and frequently in the literature, as a riser reactor. In the following years, this mode of operation was adopted for combustion of granular coal and other [solid particle](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/solid-particle) fuels, and later extended also to gasification. [CFB combustion](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/fluidized-bed-combustion) is covered in Chapter 16 of this book. Circulating [fluidized beds](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/fluidized-bed) are also highly appropriate for chemical looping operations and for sorption-enhanced gasification, subjects covered in Chapters 20 and 22 of this book, respectively. In this chapter, we consider the key [hydrodynamic](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/hydrodynamics) and reactor features of circulating fluidized beds, with emphasis on the Geldart Group B solids (typical mean particle diameters of ~ 200–400 μm) and flow conditions and configurations that are most relevant to CFB [combustors](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/combustor) and [gasifiers](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/gasifiers). While liquid–solid and gas–liquid–solid CFB systems are of some recent interest, we limit our attention in this chapter to gas–solid systems. For more extensive reviews of circulating fluidized bed [hydrodynamics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/hydrodynamics), see Horio (1997) and Grace *et al*. (2003).

One distinguishing feature of circulating fluidized beds is that there is generally no sharp interface between a bed of particles and the dilute phase found in the freeboard of low-velocity fluidized beds. Instead, the suspension density varies continuously over the entire height of the column, with, on average, ~1–25% of the reactor volume at any height occupied by the [solid particles](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/solid-particle). Another distinguishing feature is that there is a substantial external recirculation flux of particles, with solids carried out of the top of the riser returned continuously to the bottom of the riser via a gas-solid separator (usually one or more cyclones) and return system (typically a [standpipe](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/standpipe) equipped with a non-mechanical valve to prevent backflow of gas and usually, but not always, also to regulate the solids flow). The overall circulating fluidized bed facility is then considered to include the return system, as well as the riser.

The conditions of CFB operation, outlined below, create a number of potential advantages relative to low-velocity (stationary) fluidized beds:

The particles are well separated and in vigorous motion, helping to minimize agglomeration and promoting effective gas-solid contacting.

The tall height of the riser and limited gas [axial dispersion](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/axial-dispersion) mean that gas can enter at several different levels, facilitating, for example, reduction of NOx emissions.

There is significantly less axial dispersion of the gas and particles, so that the gas and particle flows are closer to plug flow than in stationary units, helping to achieve higher conversions and selectivities.

One also finds improved lateral mixing of solids, with the result that fewer feed points are needed per unit cross-sectional area.

The [heat release rate](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/heat-release-rate) per unit cross-sectional area is increased.

The return system provides a location where a complementary reaction or operation (e.g. heat transfer) can be realized.

In addition to these advantages relative to stationary fluidized bed systems, [CFB reactors](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/fluidized-bed-reactor) share several advantages with lower velocity fluidized beds relative to packed bed reactors. These include temperature uniformity, favorable suspension-to-surface [heat transfer coefficients](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/heat-transfer-coefficient), effective contact between particles and gas, low pressure drops, ability to handle wide particle size distributions, and capacity to add and withdraw particles without interrupting the operation.

Common disadvantages of CFB reactors relative to low-velocity units are increased reactor overall heights, augmented particle entrainment from the reactor, more limited particle size specifications, increased wastage of surfaces, higher rates of particle attrition, and somewhat lower suspension-to-surface heat transfer coefficients. The height requirement (tens of metres) is such that CFB systems are usually only adopted in preference to stationary fluidized beds for large-scale systems, e.g. ≳ 50 MWe in the case of combustion for [power generation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/power-generation).

Circulating fluidized-bed [gasifiers](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/gasifiers) (Figure 6.9) consist of a riser (operates either in turbulent or fast [fluidization](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/fluidization) regime) in which feedstocks undergo moisture evaporation, [pyrolysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/pyrolysis), and [char gasification](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/char-gasification), a high-temperature [cyclone separator](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/cyclone-separator) in which coarser particles are captured from the product gas and are recycled to the bottom of the riser through an air-driven loop seal, and a ceramic fiber filter unit for gas cleaning. Circulating fluidized-bed gasifiers have the ability to deal with high-capacity throughput by having a higher flow rate of fluidizing agent, and are useful for converting waste feedstocks into energy.

![](https://ars.els-cdn.com/content/image/3-s2.0-B9781782423614000066-f06-09-9781782423614.jpg)Figure 6.9. Schematic of circulating fluidized-bed gasifier.Adapted from Li et al. (2004).
Compared to classical [fluidized beds](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/fluidized-bed), circulating [fluidized bed](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/fluidized-bed) (CFB) operates at high [gas velocity](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/materials-science/gas-velocity) with solids circulation, which benefits gas–solid contact, bed-to-wall heat transfer, [catalyst regeneration](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/materials-science/catalyst-regeneration), and reaction [selectivity](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/selectivity). Therefore, it finds wide applications in processes such as [aluminum hydroxide](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/hydrated-alumina) [calcination](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/materials-science/calcination), fluid [catalytic cracking](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/catalytic-cracking) (FCC), [coal combustion](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/materials-science/coal-combustion) (Reh, 1996) and methanol-to-olefin (MTO) (Soundararajan et al., 2001). The whole loop of [CFB](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/fluidized-bed) consists of the riser, [downcomer](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/downcomer), and in between, cyclone and valve/siphon. Most of researches are focused on the riser section where reactions mainly occur, whose behavior is, however, connected with the other sections of the loop.

When the [gas velocity](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/gas-velocity) is increased further, beyond the bubbling [fluidized bed](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/fluidized-bed) regime, the solids will be distributed across the whole riser height and entrained by the gas at the top of the [gasifier](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/gasifiers). Particles are separated from the gas in a cyclone and are returned to the fluid bed near the bottom. Then it becomes a circulating fluidized bed (Fig. 1B). The advantage of circulating fluidized beds in mainly due to the longer overall residence time. The solids circulate in the outer loop, going up in the riser, leaving at the top, going down in the return leg and entering the riser again at the bottom. But there is also internal circulation of the solids, which fall back from the higher region of the riser and move downwards at the riser wall. Circulating fluidized bed [gasifiers](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/gasifiers) are normally used for large applications. It has enhanced flexibility over [bubbling fluidized bed](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/bubbling-fluidized-bed) gasifier for firing multi-fuels with high moisture content and significantly higher efficiency.

The circulating [fluidized bed](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/fluidized-bed) (CFB) system is the most common configuration for chemical looping conversions [7,19,137,140–152], and other similar processes such as dual bed systems used for fuel [pyrolysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/pyrolysis) and gasification [153,154]. The CFB has attracted great interests due to its inherent advantages on avoiding the undesirable product dilution and realizing poly generation. Fig. 6 shows the typically schematic diagram of the inter-connected fluidized bed system, where the oxygen carrier is the bed material. The fuel reactor is the [bubbling fluidized bed](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/bubbling-fluidized-bed), which permits long residential time and high capacity. When gaseous fuel is used, the gas also acts as the [fluidizing](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/fluidization) medium, which would not affect the reduction degree of iron-based oxygen carrier. However, when the solid fuel is used, steam and/or CO2 should be introduced as both the reactant intermediate and the fluidizing medium (called as iG-CLC), which could limit the reduction of Fe2O3 only to Fe3O4 due to the thermodynamic limitation [68]. The reduced oxygen carrier from the fuel reactor flows into the air reactor through a loop seal, which could prevent the air going to the fuel reactor for diluting the CO2 or burning the reductive gases, avoiding the dangerous factors. The air reactor generally consists of two parts: the bubbling [fluidization](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/fluidization) part (low) and the fast fluidization part (upper). The bubbling fluidized bed is designed to fully oxidize the oxygen carrier while the fast fluidization bed is used to transport the oxygen carrier to a high position and then back to the fuel reactor. The solid and gas from the air reactor are introduced to a cyclone, where the oxygen carrier particles are captured. The loop seal between the cyclone and fuel reactor prevents the mixing of CO2 and oxygen depleting air between these two reactors [155]. Even though several prototypes have been proposed to deal with different materials or to increase the fuel conversion, the basic mechanism of these reactors are almost the same. To date, gaseous, liquid, and solid fuel with synthesized iron oxygen carrier, [hematite](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/hematite), and [ilmenite](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/ilmenite) have been tested for chemical looping conversions with different capacities, as summarized in Table 3.

Circulating [fluidized bed](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/fluidized-bed) (CFB) combustion technology can be used for a broad variety of fuels. The popularity of [fluidized bed combustion](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/fluidized-bed-combustion) is due largely to the technology’s fuel flexibility and the capability of meeting sulfur dioxide and [nitrogen oxide](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/nitrogen-oxides) emission standards without the need for expensive add-on controls.

Basically, Circulating [fluidized bed boilers](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/fluidized-bed-boiler) consist of following key elements: a fuel feeding system, the primary air distributing system, the secondary air system, separated solid material circulating system and the arrangement of the heaters. Fuel flexibility is becoming increasingly important since there is an increased need to burn a broad spectrum of biomass and waste derived [biomass fuels](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/biomass-fuels). [Circulating fluidized bed combustion](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/circulating-fluidized-bed-combustion) is today a well established technology for biomass combustion for generation of heat and power [41,46–48].

There are a lot of researches concentrated on biomass circulating fluidized bed combustion. For circulating fluidized bed, silica-containing sand is usually used as the bed material, and potassium in biomass can react with silica to form a low temperature [eutectic](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/eutectics) during combustion, which will damage the operation [49–62] and some possible ways have been used to solve the [alkali metal](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/alkali-metal) related problems [63–77].

Circulating fluidized bed is well suited for co-firing biomass in existing large coal [power boilers](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/power-boiler) [78–82]. In a CO2 constrained future, increasing demands on efficient use of [biomass conversion](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/biomass-conversion) makes it likely that co-firing of biomass with coal becomes an interesting option as part of the bridge to a more sustainable [energy system](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/energy-systems).

A [fluidized bed](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/fluidized-bed) is a well-stirred reactor, characterized by excellent gas-solid contact, and uniformity of temperature and composition across the bed. [Fluidized bed processes](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/fluidized-bed-process) accounts for about 2% of [DRI](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/direct-reduced-iron) produced. Gas utilization in a single bed process is unfavorable, so all commercial processes use two or more beds in series; the gases and solids flow countercurrently. Fluidized bed processes are typically used next to sources of [iron ore](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/iron-ore), thus saving the cost of pelletizing. The product DRI is almost always converted to HBI.

The CIRCORED process uses two fluidized beds for reduction. The ore is preheated in two fluidized beds, and then prereduced in a circulating [fluidized bed reactor](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/fluidized-bed-reactor), with high [gas velocities](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/materials-science/gas-velocity) and a retention time of 20–30 minutes. The prereduced ore then passes into a conventional fluidized bed reactor for final reduction. After a retention time of 2–4 hours, it is flash-heated to 680 °C prior to roll [briquetting](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/briquetting). The operating pressure is 4 bars gauge, and the temperature is kept below 630 °C to prevent sticking. Natural gas is used as a [feedstock](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/materials-science/feedstock) to a steam reformer with a [CO](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/materials-science/carbon-monoxide)2 removal system, such that the reducing gas is mainly hydrogen.

The Fior/FINMET process consists of a fluidized bed dryer, four reducing reactors, and a roll briquetter. The reducing gas is produced by [steam reforming](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/steam-reforming) of natural gas, in a manner similar to that shown in Fig. 4. Reactor temperatures range from 550 °C in the [prereduction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/prereduction) reactor to 780–800 °C in the final reduction reactor. The throughput is enhanced by operating at pressures between 11 and 13 bars gauge; this greatly decreases the linear gas flow as compared to lower pressure operation. Ore fines between 13 mm and 150 μm are preferred.

[Fluidized bed](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/fluidized-bed) is another type of [incinerator](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/incinerator) where air is applied to sand to separate the sands and the plastics. An essential characteristic of a [fluidized bed](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/fluidized-bed) is the liquid-like behavior. With this behavior, a gas–solid mixture bears a linear [static pressure distribution](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/static-pressure-distribution), denser objects sink, and lighter objects float. Under the fluidized state, the [gravitational force](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/gravitational-force) on granular particles is offset by the fluid drag. The fluidized beds have improved gas-solid mixing and more uniform [temperature distribution](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/temperature-distribution) can be achieved. In the fluidized section, all the waste is mixed, diffused, agitated, and burnt instantaneously. Any kind of waste, even with low [heating value](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/calorific-value) or high moisture, can be burnt in it.

Usually, the mechanism is so that a hopper introduces the feed continuously. If the size of the feed is large, a [crusher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/crusher) will be used prior loading it. After the feed is introduced, [fluidizing](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/fluidization) air, which is usually preheated for better efficiency of the system, circulates both the sand and the feed to provide a fluidized zone with a uniform temperature of 650–750°C. Incombustible materials are separated from the sand by vibrating screens, then the sand is returned to the system. Considering the air velocity, the bed experiences different conditions, namely fixed bed, [bubbling fluidized bed](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/bubbling-fluidized-bed) (BFB), rotating fluidized bed (RFB), or circulating fluidized bed (CFB). In most of the systems, some regulations are considered for pollutants such as SOx, NOx, and HCl prior to exhausting them into the air. For controlling SOx and HCL, [calcium hydroxide](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/hydrated-lime) (Ca(OH)2) is applied to make [solid residues](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/solid-residue) collect on the filter and are then removed from the system. Moreover, NOx will only be generated at 1200–1400°C temperatures. So, by using fluidized bed systems in lower temperatures, the NOx amount will decrease significantly. Furthermore, flying ash will be [caught by](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/by-catch) a bag filter. Fluidized bed achieves high [thermal efficiency](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/thermodynamic-efficiency) up to 90%, and can work with a large range of fuel like liquid and solid waste or mixture of the two. Moreover, due to its simple design concept, the capital and maintenance costs are relatively low.

Despite the advantages, fluidized bed is still not a common technology. One of the limitations of these systems is that size and composition of the waste inside the combustor should be controlled and most of the time, [pretreatment](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/pretreatment) is required.

To improve this type of incinerator, pure oxygen can be supplied using [pressure swing adsorption](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/pressure-swing-adsorption). Also, a stream of [flue gas](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/materials-science/flue-gas) can be recirculated to the secondary combustion area to provide turbulent flow.
