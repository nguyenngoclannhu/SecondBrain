### [FROM THE ARCHIVE | Albert Camus on the Three Antidotes to the Absurdity of Life](https://us.list-manage.com/mFoqyN4q_Y_?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897)

[![](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/albertcamus_essays.jpg?fit=263%2C388)](https://us.list-manage.com/2CC4GR7cL3h?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897)

What an astrophysicist might have the perspective to eulogize as [“the incredibly improbable trip that we’re on”](https://us.list-manage.com/11OZWan36xw?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897) the rest of us might, and often do, experience as simply and maddeningly absurd — so uncontrollable and incomprehensible as to barely make sense. What are we to make of, and do with, the absurdity of life that swarms us daily? Oliver Sacks believed that [“the most we can do is to write — intelligently, creatively, evocatively — about what it is like living in the world at this time.”](https://us.list-manage.com/lrEBUsL1rgF?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897) And yet parsing the what-it-is-like can itself drive us to despair. Still, parse we must. 

More than a decade before **Albert Camus** (November 7, 1913–January 4, 1960) became the second-youngest laureate of the Nobel Prize in Literature, [awarded](https://us.list-manage.com/17CgUS2cAK2?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897) him for work that “with clear-sighted earnestness illuminates the problems of the human conscience in our times,” he contemplated the relationship between absurdity and redemption in a 1945 interview by the French journalist Jeanine Delpech, included at the end of his [***Lyrical and Critical Essays***](https://us.list-manage.com/huIsuz4tBNZ?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897) ([*public library*](https://us.list-manage.com/QNSCsniy5J5?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897)) — the superb posthumous collection that gave us Camus on [how to strengthen our character in difficult times](https://us.list-manage.com/1CsmcZSMWiP?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897) and [happiness, despair, and the love of life](https://us.list-manage.com/ErM6jRNb2F8?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897).

[![](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/albertcamus.jpg?resize=680%2C457)](https://us.list-manage.com/_AtCp4umELP?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897)

Albert Camus

Three years before the interview, twenty-eight-year-old Camus had stunned the world with his revolutionary philosophical essay *The Myth of Sisyphus*, which begins with [one of the most powerful opening sentences in all of literature](https://us.list-manage.com/v0vXkCaCqHA?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897) and explores the paradox of the absurd in life. *“I draw from the absurd three consequences, which are my revolt, my freedom, and my passion,”* he [writes](https://us.list-manage.com/J4cYev7zwNK?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897) — something that prompted his interviewer to ask whether a philosophy predicated on absurdity might incline people to despair. 

Camus — who years earlier had asserted that [“there is no love of life without despair of life”](https://us.list-manage.com/7q5JtZ_N7N7?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897) — answers:

> ![](https://gallery.mailchimp.com/13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1/images/2e292385-dc1c-4cfe-b95e-845f6f98c2ec.png)All I can do is reply on my own behalf, realizing that what I say is relative. Accepting the absurdity of everything around us is one step, a necessary experience: it should not become a dead end. It arouses a revolt that can become fruitful. An analysis of the idea of revolt could help us to discover ideas capable of restoring a relative meaning to existence, although a meaning that would always be in danger.

Speaking at the close of the meaningless brutality of World War II, six years before he formulated his ideas on [solidarity and what it really means to be a rebel](https://us.list-manage.com/5pR_yL31AMF?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897), Camus considers the only act of courage and rebellion worth undertaking:

> ![](https://gallery.mailchimp.com/13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1/images/2e292385-dc1c-4cfe-b95e-845f6f98c2ec.png)In a world whose absurdity appears to be so impenetrable, we simply must reach a greater degree of understanding among men, a greater sincerity. We must achieve this or perish. To do so, certain conditions must be fulfilled: men must be frank (falsehood confuses things), free (communication is impossible with slaves). Finally, they must feel a certain justice around them.

I have often wondered whether Camus had read W.H. Auden’s poem [“September 1, 1939,”](https://us.list-manage.com/V6yk4CKppwF?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897) written in 1940, which includes this searing stanza so kindred to Camus’s sentiment:

> ![](https://gallery.mailchimp.com/13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1/images/2e292385-dc1c-4cfe-b95e-845f6f98c2ec.png)All I have is a voice  
> To undo the folded lie,  
> The romantic lie in the brain  
> Of the sensual man-in-the-street  
> And the lie of Authority  
> Whose buildings grope the sky:  
> There is no such thing as the State  
> And no one exists alone;  
> Hunger allows no choice  
> To the citizen or the police;  
> We must love one another or die.

Complement this particular fragment of Camus’s endlessly rewarding [***Lyrical and Critical Essays***](https://us.list-manage.com/NXaNsgfw632?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897) with Albert Einstein on [our mightiest counterforce against injustice](https://us.list-manage.com/6gRxJZ_ZF6_?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897) and Naomi Shihab Nye on [choosing kindness over fear](https://us.list-manage.com/15DaqbFsebw?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897), then revisit Camus’s abiding ideas on [happiness, unhappiness, and our self-imposed prisons](https://us.list-manage.com/zj9yLn991Dl?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897), [the most important question of existence](https://us.list-manage.com/FqyiCjT1Fxs?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897), [the lacuna between truth and meaning](https://us.list-manage.com/JQIBflxlxKS?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897), and the [touching letter of gratitude](https://us.list-manage.com/tG38Kh3fssc?e=f6b8863c15&c2id=607b4d825b51e20caffeac7a567c9897) he sent to his boyhood teacher shortly after receiving the Nobel Prize.
