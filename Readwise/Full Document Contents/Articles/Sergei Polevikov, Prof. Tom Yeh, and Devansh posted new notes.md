Kimi K2 thinking just broke the internet with its performance. But how did a Chinese Lab beat hyperscalers without the hyperscaler investments? The answer is in a trick called Muon. The choice of optimizer in deep learning is not trivial. The industry standard, AdamW, has been adopted widely because it provides stable convergence across diverse architectures and minimal risk of catastrophic divergence. Yet stability comes at the cost of efficiency — AdamW is token-hungry, slowly adapting to the geometry of the loss surface due to its reliance on first-order gradient information. Muon, the optimizer adopted by Kimi, presents a stark alternative.
