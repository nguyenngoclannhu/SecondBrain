##### There. I knew it was an acquired taste.

Possibly the most talked-about topic of 2025 among brand strategists was “taste.” The streets are saying you can conquer every social media platform if you have taste, but who gets to define good or bad taste? Algorithm? Audience?

The matter of fact is you get to define it. Taste is inherently subjective, but the conversation around it has become very performative and kind of dumb.

Why is that? Because people are talking about taste as audience segments and information, which is partially because the current movement of “taste is all that matters” is rooted in brand growth and anti-AI marketing. 

Taste can’t liberate us from the algorithms and all the obstacles we face as artists, consumers, and brands. When we haven’t made the effort to accept our differences, 2025 was also the year when hate against modern and contemporary art rose to new levels. People went viral by saying, “My art teacher hated my work, see my XYZ type of art.” Same goes for people loving or hating Benson Boone.

We can’t make arguments for taste and then ridicule people for having a different taste than us. But that’s another story, meant to be told by an artist.

***For brands and creatives who want to market their work, the strategy of taste stands at implication.*** In social media content and marketing, you have to imply that this (your product or music) is the new/good taste. In a way, taste is just the new term for cool, and just like the teens do at school, you need to communicate the good taste of your content by saying it’s good taste.

You can’t leave the interpretation of your content to the audience: you reassert and imply, over and over, that this is good taste, which won’t be a lie. Because taste is subjective, and you have to put that forward to make people curious. If you don’t, no one is going to take you seriously.

A lot of streamers, YouTubers, and musicians already do this through [clipping](https://substack.com/redirect/67864241-1eb1-475f-8599-b4dd1b4e44f0?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), which I talked about in this old post.

##### The Rage, the Act and the KPI

I want you to stop thinking of social media marketing in metrics. It is a spectacle. And the question, as usual, is whether you can sustain it.

Ragebait is what you get from a system that failed cancel culture and moderation. Nothing is truly cancelable nowadays. There are always anomalies, but you get the point. Ragebait was a marketing movement born out of inaction and people treating everything as a spectacle.

That is why Friend.com and American Eagle can do whatever they want for attention. Even if they fail, which is inevitable, they achieve what they wanted: a spectacle featuring their brand. The same goes for brands in comments. People in comments will be enraged, and some folks will like and leave.

Both actions help the brand see the green light of KPIs and brand awareness. Why would any brand sign up for this waste of marketing spend? Because this is part of our current culture. We like and repost all the essays about leaving social media, touching grass, taking action against various problems. But most people never actually do anything.

Tone-deaf and ragebait marketing is the co-product of the internet scrolling away its beliefs and values by simply commenting and liking. Most people think brands only care about what consumers do, but the smart brands also care about what they do not do.

I know American Eagle and Friend.com both got visible backlash that did hurt their brand in some way, but the scale of those controversies reflects the inaction of the masses turning into the action of a few people.

I am not advocating for this type of marketing. It is just that we cannot say ragebait marketing does not work by using the argument that people did not buy. It is a marketing strategy born out of inaction. It cannot lead to much action. Simple.

##### There’s always money in the nostalgia stand

The question is not even whether we should do a nostalgia play. It is how long we should wait before making that play. Just like 2024, this year was full of brands recycling their old identities and people falling for it.

But people often forget the cost of nostalgia marketing. Brands like [GAP](https://substack.com/redirect/57f8eef2-9d3c-4dd5-bf7a-9f1d5b143384?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) and [Burberry](https://substack.com/redirect/8fe9258e-3f09-4aa1-8844-56b241615fec?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) are getting all the love these days, and half of their strategy is to maintain their heritage and stick to what works. How did that happen? Well, years of downturn set you up for a rest that the audience happily welcomes.

If not that, the biggest cost of nostalgia is the present. The saying “Every moment you live in the past is a moment you waste in the present” rings true for every brand. Most brands cannot feed off the nostalgia people feel for their past identity.

That is why brands that do not have a heritage follow the trends, the aesthetics, and the generations. Ben Stiller’s new beverage brand for aesthetics, SKIMS and Crocs for trends, Airbnb for generations. At the end of the day, any use of non-branded nostalgia is a content strategy. It works well for small brands as they try to establish new consumer touchpoints.

Nostalgia as a heritage brand is not sustainable. Nostalgia as a new brand is the default content strategy to establish presence among the legacy players.

Nostalgia as a cultural force is what we get when people are constantly trying to escape the present and fear change. One of the reasons brands keep going back to nostalgia is simply that people feel overwhelmed, and the default response is a rewriting of the past. It is a mess, and we will go through it again in 2026.

##### Eleven rules and strategies for the new brands

**1. Try to be different than other brands, not the market.** 

New brands often confuse brand differentiation with market differentiation. Unless you have the next big product, your focus should be on positioning your brand against one or two competitors, not the entire market. The only exceptions are brands that work in anti-XYZ markets. Vegan and sustainable brands are part of this category. If you are one of them, feel free to use anti-/market differentiation.

Differentiate your brand from other companies using good copywriting, and don’t use product positioning that makes people think too hard about making a shift from X to Y product. Keep it minimal.

**2. Distribution is a function, not a strategy.** 

We’ve all heard the saying, good products market themselves. The same could be said about branding. Good branding integrates well with different media formats and, in return, helps with natural distribution.

If you don’t invest early in branding and design, you will be losing out on revenue for years. Branding/design integrates into all the elements mentioned above, which determine how you get pushed in media and the real world.

**3. Storytelling isn’t everything.** 

Everyone is going to tell you, “You need a story.” And yes, you do need a story. But it’s not the final stroke we think it is. Storytelling is a tactic, it’s used in every form of advertising and marketing. You can’t tell the same story in each format.

That’s why you need agencies and creatives to help you adapt your story into different formats. Also, storytelling is often an individual trait. Creative briefs don’t fill themselves, it’s often the people who feel too passionate about their brand and don’t dare to filter their thoughts. Storytellers need their tools, which are other creative people.

**4. Social Media is brand and marketing.**

Most brands fall into two categories:

1. Brands that are good at social media and people like them as a specific brand.
2. Brands that are good at social media and people treat them as a “social media” brand.

The first brand attracts a wider audience because people like their marketing and will make up their minds about the brand later. Meanwhile, the second brand attracts an audience that might like the marketing, but they’ve already made up their minds about your brand being a social-media-coded/viral brand. If you do marketing focused on trends and online culture, you will be part of this group.

You have to make a decision: either only market on social media or turn social into your brand, like Olipop, Ritual, and Poppi.

**5. Being Accessible is more important than being relevant.** 

You’re a new brand; you need to be on top of consumers’ minds. How do you do that? Brand awareness. Most companies will do anything to be more relevant.

But relevance without direct access to customers is a lost cause. The big brands you see investing in marketing that doesn’t prove ROI on paper do so because they’ve bought access to customers through retailers, creators, and distributors.

Even if the marketing fails, brand awareness transforms into brand memory if customers keep seeing the product/brand in the wild. As a new brand, it’s hard to buy direct access to customers. The alternatives are an increased focus on e-commerce experience, collaborations, and online retargeting efforts.

**6. Focus on one big idea.**

That’s it. Now, What are Ideas? 

Mark pollard: Ideas are thoughts but not all thoughts are “ideas.” Here’s an example of the use of the word “idea” in an agency setting: “I have an idea — let’s do something with augmented reality or Blockchain or make a special lens.” This isn’t wrong; it’s sloppy.

In the traditional industry sense, “idea” means a novel concept. But when it’s used as in this example, it masks the lack of an actual idea  —  like when someone dumps in the word “strategic” before they say something that’s not strategic. It ups the importance of what comes next. The problem: sometimes this works as a meeting tactic but does not lead to good or clear thinking.

**7. Focus on the 3Cs.**

* Culture
	+ Strategy: Your plan to create a meaningful exchange between the brand and culture.
* Consumer
	+ Research: Know your buyer.
* Creativity
	+ Consistency: Campaigns from the [most consistent brands](https://substack.com/redirect/2d6f9565-c382-4da9-ac2b-514f71538230?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) create +27% more Very Large Brand Effects, generating more brand awareness, differentiation, brand values and salience.

**8. Don’t do anything just to prove you are a brand.** 

* Do not hire influencers to prove you are here.
* Do not neglect marketing channels just because someone or a trend report said so.
* Do not scale distribution for the sake of scaling.
* Do not write a long creative brief for the sake of professionalism.
* Do not listen to marketers on what people like.
* Do not use a word like authentic without attributing meaning to it.
* Do not give up on rethinking.

In short, don’t stick to the playbook for the sake of trying to look like an established brand.

**9. Efficiency & Short-termism kills.**

Meta, Google, and other types of ad platforms sell efficiency as a product to new brands and marketers. Your dashboards in the short term will be filled with positive metrics. Meanwhile, long-term growth will stall sooner or later.

Every brand needs a good mix of marketing channels to sustain its long-term growth. Your zero-dollar efforts like putting posters in local event spaces, sending out stickers that customers can use, and referral marketing tactics are the efforts that sustain brand growth.

Keep a marketing mix of channels that provide both short and long-term results.

**10. Know your brand traits and optimise for loyalty.**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!SLht!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1ddd40b1-c385-4ccc-b6e4-400afa9326fe_1968x1308.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/ade6b860-0c3f-41e9-9ce9-9a0786d5e642?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)Brand experience’s influence on [customer satisfaction and loyalty](https://substack.com/redirect/2f431b0d-f30e-481b-90fa-d4b60ed8151d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g): a mirage in marketing research? **11. Tactics vs Strategy?**

Most brands are stuck in two different situations:

* XY Brand, with a good strategy, keeps adding more and more tactics to their marketing plan.
* YZ Brand, with a bad strategy, hesitates to add new tactics to their marketing plan.

One is planning its downfall by stacking bad tactics at the bottom; the other can prevent its downfall by stacking tactics wisely. When your brand is on fire, use the stairs, do not use the elevator. The tactics are stairs that help you move toward a successful exit.

Focus on adapting your brand to tactics used by social media-first companies. Hire consultants who can help you with digital channels and more. Don’t think big; think small and stay in motion.

##### Week one of sharing marketing glossary: ABC.

* A/B Testing: Creating 2 versions of a digital asset to see which one users respond to better.
* Advertising: The practice and techniques employed to bring attention to a product or service.
* Ad fatigue: Decline in conversion as your audience is seeing the same ad too many times.
* Attribution: The metric/process used to determine how different marketing tactics for channels may contribute to overall marketing goals.
* Affiliate Marketing: A strategy where businesses reward individual partners for bringing in new customers.
* Ad Network: A service that offers online ad space for sale to advertisers.
* Alt Text: Text that shows in place of images or pops up when you hover your mouse over an image.
* Audience Segmentation: The process of grouping people based on shared characteristics.
* Advertorial: An advertisement that resembles a news article or editorial in a print or electronic communication
* Account-based marketing (ABM): A B2B marketing strategy that concentrates resources on a set of target accounts within a market.
* Behavioural Challenge: A problem or issue that can be addressed through an understanding of human behaviour and the application of behavioural science principles.
* Backlinks: The links on websites other than your own that go back to a page on your website.
* Brand Awareness: The level of familiarity consumers have with a particular brand.
* Bounce Rate: The percentage of initial visitors to a site who “bounce” to another site instead of staying on the original website.
* Brand: The sum total of how someone perceives a particular organisation.
* B2B/B2C: Business-to-Business and Business-to-Consumer transactions.
* Brand equity: It encompasses consumers’ individual and collective levels of brand awareness and knowledge.
* Buyer persona: A detailed description of someone who represents your target audience.
* Brand Salience: The propensity of the brand to be thought of by buyers.
* Classified Ads: Ads appearing on the same page and grouped into categories in a list-like format.
* CTR: The percentage of users who click on the link in your digital marketing message after seeing it.
* Content Marketing: Creating and sharing valuable, relevant content with intent of turning online audiences into customers.
* Creatives: A group of individuals being briefed by a brand/agency on a project to create advertising for a product or service. Creative: Ad’s visual and textual elements.
* Choice Paralysis: The phenomenon where we are presented with too many choices/decisions, that our ability to decide is impaired, leading to a poor choice or no choice being made at all.
* Contextual Advertising: A type of targeted advertising that is displayed based on the context of the web page or the user’s browsing behavior.
* Conversion Rate: The percentage of user actions taken after total clicks on a display ad or other digital asset
* Cost per click / conversion rate optimisation / cost per thousand impression / Customer acquisition cost - Different terms used to track and describe marketing costs.
* Comms Planner: A comms planner provides strategic rigor to the implementation of the idea, ensuring integration of the work between creative and media.

The definitions were curated from multiple corners of the Internet and marketing: IPA, Hubspot, Mailchimp, Wikipedia, LinkedIn, AdQuick, Gartner, etc.

<h3 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.375em;">There. I knew it was an acquired taste. </h3>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Possibly the most talked-about topic of 2025 among brand strategists was “taste.” The streets are saying you can conquer every social media platform if you have taste, but who gets to define good or bad taste? Algorithm? Audience?</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">The matter of fact is you get to define it. Taste is inherently subjective, but the conversation around it has become very performative and kind of dumb.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Why is that? Because people are talking about taste as audience segments and information, which is partially because the current movement of “taste is all that matters” is rooted in brand growth and anti-AI marketing. </p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Taste can’t liberate us from the algorithms and all the obstacles we face as artists, consumers, and brands. When we haven’t made the effort to accept our differences, 2025 was also the year when hate against modern and contemporary art rose to new levels. People went viral by saying, “My art teacher hated my work, see my XYZ type of art.” Same goes for people loving or hating Benson Boone.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">We can’t make arguments for taste and then ridicule people for having a different taste than us. But that’s another story, meant to be told by an artist.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><em><strong>For brands and creatives who want to market their work, the strategy of taste stands at implication.</strong></em><span> In social media content and marketing, you have to imply that this (your product or music) is the new/good taste. In a way, taste is just the new term for cool, and just like the teens do at school, you need to communicate the good taste of your content by saying it’s good taste.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">You can’t leave the interpretation of your content to the audience: you reassert and imply, over and over, that this is good taste, which won’t be a lie. Because taste is subjective, and you have to put that forward to make people curious. If you don’t, no one is going to take you seriously.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>A lot of streamers, YouTubers, and musicians already do this through </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/67864241-1eb1-475f-8599-b4dd1b4e44f0?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">clipping</a><span>, which I talked about in this old post. </span></p>
<div style="font-size: 16px;line-height: 26px;"><hr style="margin: 32px 0;padding: 0;height: 1px;background: #e6e6e6;border: none;"/></div>
<h3 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.375em;">The Rage, the Act and the KPI</h3>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">I want you to stop thinking of social media marketing in metrics. It is a spectacle. And the question, as usual, is whether you can sustain it.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Ragebait is what you get from a system that failed cancel culture and moderation. Nothing is truly cancelable nowadays. There are always anomalies, but you get the point. Ragebait was a marketing movement born out of inaction and people treating everything as a spectacle.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">That is why Friend.com and American Eagle can do whatever they want for attention. Even if they fail, which is inevitable, they achieve what they wanted: a spectacle featuring their brand. The same goes for brands in comments. People in comments will be enraged, and some folks will like and leave.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Both actions help the brand see the green light of KPIs and brand awareness. Why would any brand sign up for this waste of marketing spend? Because this is part of our current culture. We like and repost all the essays about leaving social media, touching grass, taking action against various problems. But most people never actually do anything.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Tone-deaf and ragebait marketing is the co-product of the internet scrolling away its beliefs and values by simply commenting and liking. Most people think brands only care about what consumers do, but the smart brands also care about what they do not do.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">I know American Eagle and Friend.com both got visible backlash that did hurt their brand in some way, but the scale of those controversies reflects the inaction of the masses turning into the action of a few people.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">I am not advocating for this type of marketing. It is just that we cannot say ragebait marketing does not work by using the argument that people did not buy. It is a marketing strategy born out of inaction. It cannot lead to much action. Simple.</p>

<h3 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.375em;">There’s always money in the nostalgia stand</h3>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">The question is not even whether we should do a nostalgia play. It is how long we should wait before making that play. Just like 2024, this year was full of brands recycling their old identities and people falling for it.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>But people often forget the cost of nostalgia marketing. Brands like </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/57f8eef2-9d3c-4dd5-bf7a-9f1d5b143384?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">GAP</a><span> and </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/8fe9258e-3f09-4aa1-8844-56b241615fec?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Burberry</a><span> are getting all the love these days, and half of their strategy is to maintain their heritage and stick to what works. How did that happen? Well, years of downturn set you up for a rest that the audience happily welcomes.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">If not that, the biggest cost of nostalgia is the present. The saying “Every moment you live in the past is a moment you waste in the present” rings true for every brand. Most brands cannot feed off the nostalgia people feel for their past identity.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">That is why brands that do not have a heritage follow the trends, the aesthetics, and the generations. Ben Stiller’s new beverage brand for aesthetics, SKIMS and Crocs for trends, Airbnb for generations. At the end of the day, any use of non-branded nostalgia is a content strategy. It works well for small brands as they try to establish new consumer touchpoints.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Nostalgia as a heritage brand is not sustainable. Nostalgia as a new brand is the default content strategy to establish presence among the legacy players.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Nostalgia as a cultural force is what we get when people are constantly trying to escape the present and fear change. One of the reasons brands keep going back to nostalgia is simply that people feel overwhelmed, and the default response is a rewriting of the past. It is a mess, and we will go through it again in 2026.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>5. How to get copywriting experience or get good at it.</strong></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">One big thing; SPEC WORK! Don’t feel the need to wait till you land a gig to start building your own experience (also most copy jobs will ask for a portfolio so…). Think of it as fanfiction for brands. Choose a company you really like (let’s say Duolingo) and write up a piece of copy for them as if YOU’RE REALLY writing for them. Make sure you label it as spec work and add it to your portfolio. Keep doing this till you have at least 4-5 really solid pieces in there. But also combine it with your creative writing; if you’ve got a blog or newsletter - feature it on there too. Show off all your writing skills!</p>

<h3 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.375em;">Eleven rules and strategies for the new brands</h3>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>1. Try to be different than other brands, not the market. </strong></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">New brands often confuse brand differentiation with market differentiation. Unless you have the next big product, your focus should be on positioning your brand against one or two competitors, not the entire market. The only exceptions are brands that work in anti-XYZ markets. Vegan and sustainable brands are part of this category. If you are one of them, feel free to use anti-/market differentiation.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Differentiate your brand from other companies using good copywriting, and don’t use product positioning that makes people think too hard about making a shift from X to Y product. Keep it minimal.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>2. Distribution is a function, not a strategy. </strong></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">We’ve all heard the saying, good products market themselves. The same could be said about branding. Good branding integrates well with different media formats and, in return, helps with natural distribution.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">If you don’t invest early in branding and design, you will be losing out on revenue for years. Branding/design integrates into all the elements mentioned above, which determine how you get pushed in media and the real world.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>3. Storytelling isn’t everything. </strong></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Everyone is going to tell you, “You need a story.” And yes, you do need a story. But it’s not the final stroke we think it is. Storytelling is a tactic, it’s used in every form of advertising and marketing. You can’t tell the same story in each format.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">That’s why you need agencies and creatives to help you adapt your story into different formats. Also, storytelling is often an individual trait. Creative briefs don’t fill themselves, it’s often the people who feel too passionate about their brand and don’t dare to filter their thoughts. Storytellers need their tools, which are other creative people.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>4. Social Media is brand and marketing.</strong></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Most brands fall into two categories:</p>
<ol style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;">
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Brands that are good at social media and people like them as a specific brand.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Brands that are good at social media and people treat them as a “social media” brand.</p></li>
</ol>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">The first brand attracts a wider audience because people like their marketing and will make up their minds about the brand later. Meanwhile, the second brand attracts an audience that might like the marketing, but they’ve already made up their minds about your brand being a social-media-coded/viral brand. If you do marketing focused on trends and online culture, you will be part of this group.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">You have to make a decision: either only market on social media or turn social into your brand, like Olipop, Ritual, and Poppi.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>5. Being Accessible is more important than being relevant. </strong></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">You’re a new brand; you need to be on top of consumers’ minds. How do you do that? Brand awareness. Most companies will do anything to be more relevant.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">But relevance without direct access to customers is a lost cause. The big brands you see investing in marketing that doesn’t prove ROI on paper do so because they’ve bought access to customers through retailers, creators, and distributors.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Even if the marketing fails, brand awareness transforms into brand memory if customers keep seeing the product/brand in the wild. As a new brand, it’s hard to buy direct access to customers. The alternatives are an increased focus on e-commerce experience, collaborations, and online retargeting efforts.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>6. Focus on one big idea.</strong></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">That’s it. Now, What are Ideas? </p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Mark pollard: Ideas are thoughts but not all thoughts are “ideas.” Here’s an example of the use of the word “idea” in an agency setting: “I have an idea — let’s do something with augmented reality or Blockchain or make a special lens.” This isn’t wrong; it’s sloppy.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">In the traditional industry sense, “idea” means a novel concept. But when it’s used as in this example, it masks the lack of an actual idea  —  like when someone dumps in the word “strategic” before they say something that’s not strategic. It ups the importance of what comes next. The problem: sometimes this works as a meeting tactic but does not lead to good or clear thinking.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>7. Focus on the 3Cs.</strong></p>
<ul style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;">
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;">Culture</p>
<ul style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;"><li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Strategy: Your plan to create a meaningful exchange between the brand and culture. </p></li></ul>
</li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;">Consumer </p>
<ul style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;"><li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Research: Know your buyer.</p></li></ul>
</li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;">Creativity</p>
<ul style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;"><li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;"><span>Consistency: Campaigns from the </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/2d6f9565-c382-4da9-ac2b-514f71538230?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">most consistent brands</a><span> create +27% more Very Large Brand Effects, generating more brand awareness, differentiation, brand values and salience.</span></p></li></ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>8. Don’t do anything just to prove you are a brand. </strong></p>
<ul style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;">
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Do not hire influencers to prove you are here.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Do not neglect marketing channels just because someone or a trend report said so.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Do not scale distribution for the sake of scaling.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Do not write a long creative brief for the sake of professionalism.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Do not listen to marketers on what people like.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Do not use a word like authentic without attributing meaning to it.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Do not give up on rethinking.</p></li>
</ul>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">In short, don’t stick to the playbook for the sake of trying to look like an established brand.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>9. Efficiency &amp; Short-termism kills.</strong></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Meta, Google, and other types of ad platforms sell efficiency as a product to new brands and marketers. Your dashboards in the short term will be filled with positive metrics. Meanwhile, long-term growth will stall sooner or later.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Every brand needs a good mix of marketing channels to sustain its long-term growth. Your zero-dollar efforts like putting posters in local event spaces, sending out stickers that customers can use, and referral marketing tactics are the efforts that sustain brand growth.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Keep a marketing mix of channels that provide both short and long-term results.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>10. Know your brand traits and optimise for loyalty.</strong></p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>11. Tactics vs Strategy?</strong></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Most brands are stuck in two different situations:</p>
<ul style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;">
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">XY Brand, with a good strategy, keeps adding more and more tactics to their marketing plan.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">YZ Brand, with a bad strategy, hesitates to add new tactics to their marketing plan.</p></li>
</ul>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">One is planning its downfall by stacking bad tactics at the bottom; the other can prevent its downfall by stacking tactics wisely. When your brand is on fire, use the stairs, do not use the elevator. The tactics are stairs that help you move toward a successful exit.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Focus on adapting your brand to tactics used by social media-first companies. Hire consultants who can help you with digital channels and more. Don’t think big; think small and stay in motion.</p>

<ul style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;">
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">A/B Testing: Creating 2 versions of a digital asset to see which one users respond to better.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Advertising: The practice and techniques employed to bring attention to a product or service.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Ad fatigue: Decline in conversion as your audience is seeing the same ad too many times. </p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Attribution: The metric/process used to determine how different marketing tactics for channels may contribute to overall marketing goals.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Affiliate Marketing: A strategy where businesses reward individual partners for bringing in new customers.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Ad Network: A service that offers online ad space for sale to advertisers. </p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Alt Text: Text that shows in place of images or pops up when you hover your mouse over an image.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Audience Segmentation: The process of grouping people based on shared characteristics.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Advertorial: An advertisement that resembles a news article or editorial in a print or electronic communication</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Account-based marketing (ABM): A B2B marketing strategy that concentrates resources on a set of target accounts within a market.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Behavioural Challenge: A problem or issue that can be addressed through an understanding of human behaviour and the application of behavioural science principles.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Backlinks: The links on websites other than your own that go back to a page on your website.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Brand Awareness: The level of familiarity consumers have with a particular brand.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Bounce Rate: The percentage of initial visitors to a site who “bounce” to another site instead of staying on the original website.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Brand: The sum total of how someone perceives a particular organisation.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">B2B/B2C: Business-to-Business and Business-to-Consumer transactions.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Brand equity: It encompasses consumers’ individual and collective levels of brand awareness and knowledge.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Buyer persona: A detailed description of someone who represents your target audience. </p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Brand Salience: The propensity of the brand to be thought of by buyers.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Classified Ads: Ads appearing on the same page and grouped into categories in a list-like format.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">CTR: The percentage of users who click on the link in your digital marketing message after seeing it. </p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Content Marketing: Creating and sharing valuable, relevant content with intent of turning online audiences into customers. </p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Creatives: A group of individuals being briefed by a brand/agency on a project to create advertising for a product or service. Creative: Ad’s visual and textual elements.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Choice Paralysis: The phenomenon where we are presented with too many choices/decisions, that our ability to decide is impaired, leading to a poor choice or no choice being made at all.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Contextual Advertising: A type of targeted advertising that is displayed based on the context of the web page or the user’s browsing behavior.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Conversion Rate: The percentage of user actions taken after total clicks on a display ad or other digital asset</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Cost per click / conversion rate optimisation / cost per thousand impression / Customer acquisition cost - Different terms used to track and describe marketing costs. </p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Comms Planner: A comms planner provides strategic rigor to the implementation of the idea, ensuring integration of the work between creative and media. </p></li>
</ul>
