By now, we’ve all probably seen either this year’s AI-generated Coca-Cola ad campaign or last year’s. No matter what you think of the ad, it’s likely that Coke will continue to release this kind of campaign every year. And we should stop looking only at the end product and follow the discourse.

I tell myself this all the time: we’re living in an era of discourse over discussion. What’s happening matters more than what’s happened.

##### Consumers don’t really care about AI, Marketing or Brand.

The funny thing about marketing and brand is that everything contradicts everything. We have research from Byron Sharp, Kantar, Oxford, and a couple of other sources showing that market availability, consistency, and marketing budgets influence what people buy and how often they do so. That is what makes people buy and eventually care about brands that inhabit their daily lives. It is practical and it works every time.

But those same strategies are often co-opted by marketers and executives who abandon creativity and culture for higher profit. And that leaves a window open for other brands to create change, which does not happen by merely asking people whether they care about some XYZ brand purpose.

Brands like Nike, Signal, Patagonia, Ben & Jerry’s, Apple, and many others are not just asking people to care about what they do. They are posing another question: Why do you not care about XYZ?

They are speaking in negatives and making people question the other brands. Tony’s Chocolonely and Oatly are almost obsessed with making people care more about what their industry is doing in comparison to what they do.

> All that to say: we have known for decades that the average consumer cares only about price and product, but the purpose of a brand is always to make them care about something more than the product/service.

> Not only because all humans desire to be purposeful, but because the financial market demands that companies have brand identities holding imaginary values of attention, status, and culture.

> Whenever someone asks me why brand purpose matters so much, I’m like, look around you, watch movies, and look into human history. We’ve gone to war for purpose and identity.

But one of the biggest brands has already answered, and they are clearly trying to make people care about AI advertising in one way or another.

##### Marketers are killing this industry and its creativity with their consumers-only mentality.

Our industry is co-opting Amazon’s customer-obsessed mentality. The same people who say we’re in the business of entertainment, information, and culture turn around and claim that all that matters is what the customer thinks.

First, we try to escape accountability by shifting keywords. Consumers not caring about AI ads is different from people not caring about AI ads, which is different again from critics or viewers not caring about the ad or the brand.

Different roles deliver different kinds of feedback to the brand. And in a media ecosystem that is now thoroughly decentralised, you cannot ignore the feedback you get from non-customers, because there is zero chance your campaign will succeed in isolation.

If you truly believe in the idea of brands engaging with entertainment, information, and culture, you cannot only care about the customer living inside a human. You have to account for the full range of perspectives.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!pJLC!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8fc669be-c8b7-47d2-b2cf-481b78eb065e_2336x1364.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/dddecab3-b10e-4b9a-b56e-d38b9da120ae?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)The diagram above explains everything wrong and right with marketing in 2025. Most brands are focused on producing work that either fits the marketer’s model of marketing/brand or the consumer’s model of marketing/brand. The results are familiar: work that gets noticed only at Cannes versus work that goes viral and attracts “marketing genius” comments. Both products are mainstream in their own way.

Whether good or bad, this is still human, and it still respects the interface, which is culture, human behaviour, and societal norms. We would have no artistic progression at all if we only cared about the audience. The interface between marketers and consumers is inhabited by critics, standards, and forms of behaviour that exist to determine how low or high we aim as a brand or as a media business. Interface matters as much as the people. 

The AI slop Coca-Cola ad, Tilly Norwood and Sora are not threatening the marketer or the consumer. They are making us question whether we should continue to respect the interface, meaning culture and industry practices, or leave it behind.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!0mdU!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F26db567a-9671-48d6-92c3-5a95a4a42836_1345x299.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/6b4391ef-e1f5-4764-8fe9-5c2bb1cac294?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)Marketing is not the only industry facing this question. Every industry is reconsidering the role of the interface. Even the discourse [around](https://substack.com/redirect/88833e14-0632-45b0-8510-6ccb24656ee1?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) this question or threat is a [mess](https://substack.com/redirect/a820f596-d5ed-4c63-8833-0de0beb72f70?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g).

##### Mamdani, Trump & Bannon: Can brands win with the strategy that will continue to dominate the 2020s?

While brands have always embraced neutrality, their actions and audiences eventually give them certain political and non-political affiliations. That is the old story.

What we learn from the strategies used by Zohran, Trump and the new mainstream media of podcasters and creators is that the rules of neutral engagement have changed. While we do not yet know how far the Overton window has shifted for brands, we can already see that the public is far more accepting of politicians using a wider range of platforms for distribution and dialogue.

The same goes for media companies. Last week, we saw [Vogue retreat](https://substack.com/redirect/7c9e4948-6b53-40eb-9d12-06193828d7ab?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) from politics, and a month ago, Bari Weiss joined CBS. Reuters, NBC and The New York Times have launched [campaigns focused on brand messaging](https://substack.com/redirect/45b2c160-9992-4332-91bb-854cabb683e3?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) that is neutral and shifts attention away from political alignments toward news as a form of entertainment. The big force behind all of this change is Trump, and no one is denying that. But this updated dynamic of wanting everyone is a long time coming, because it is one of the only viable paths to growth for large brands, especially when most consumers are struggling.

On the flip side, this strategy of distribution-focused collaborations meets a media cycle that is fast, inconsistent and designed to overwhelm people on all sides while advancing whatever issue is in play. That is Steve Bannon’s strategy of [Muzzle Velocity](https://substack.com/redirect/751550f7-8c83-44ea-bd8a-51dd32d3de4d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g).

It might not look like this is happening in marketing, but we are slowly moving toward it. *[Wicked](https://substack.com/redirect/2b3f6831-498f-4b37-b801-98543192f8ef?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)* (the movie) has reportedly partnered with over [400 brands](https://substack.com/redirect/69a57da6-7815-4227-a2f9-f0af5adc8724?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), Crocs is launching a new IP collaboration every other month, and AI companies are releasing more press releases than actual products. The list goes on.

It is the new copypasta strategy that everyone is already using to some degree. The media cycle becomes visual noise that overwhelms the target audience, and the content cycle becomes universal, catching the interest of every group.

Another mainstream example of this dynamic is simply noticing how many creators and newsletters you follow that report either on what is happening or on what they are consuming. Everyone feels overwhelmed, yet they keep buying into it.

##### Glazing & Goon-baiting to win online audiences.

Duolingo, who? There are new [AI-powered language-learning apps](https://substack.com/redirect/ca67cd3f-0cb4-42ed-969c-0b3d1fdcd736?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) marketing to Gen Z by deliberately mispronouncing words and sentences in different languages. The lines are usually about sex or private parts, delivered in a broken accent for comedic effect.

The startups have hired a batch of UGC creators who run multiple accounts where they “[learn](https://substack.com/redirect/ca67cd3f-0cb4-42ed-969c-0b3d1fdcd736?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)” different languages, from Spanish to Hindi. In the videos, creators respond to an AI Teacher that roasts them for their mispronunciations, prompting the inevitable teary reaction. A line like “learning XYZ language is so hard” is always overlaid on the screen, as predictable as the joke itself.

The companies change from LingoTok to PingoAI to Airlearn, but the strategy remains the same. Airlearn has pushed things a little further with a new [billboard campaign](https://substack.com/redirect/b8532ff2-636a-4727-bbbe-b7181d95658c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) in NYC and a major influencer collaboration in [India](https://substack.com/redirect/f785a97e-f3ce-45a5-a0d2-371c104bb92e?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). The OOH approach mirrors their online strategy: billboards feature the words *kock*, *pussi* and *dik*, accompanied by subheadings explaining their meanings in Swedish, Dutch, and other languages.

But these startups aren’t goon-baiting to the extent of the [Chinese language teachers](https://substack.com/redirect/555f347e-974f-400d-be13-149bcfa2a85d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) and some other English tutors on Instagram and TikTok, who are attracting new audiences mostly by [teaching](https://substack.com/redirect/88537c22-7e24-4584-bb7b-01e22c199a85?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) NSFW phrases that you [literally](https://substack.com/redirect/e9c09ad6-2cbf-4c0e-ae4e-c983f7952328?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) can’t use in public. The videos are funny, to say the least, though [sometimes the joke](https://substack.com/redirect/f87b7e18-afd9-414b-919e-9295db423ae2?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) seems to be on the viewer.

What did I just say, “literally”? That’s the word. I’ve seen so many creators in the tech world use it to describe new launches, and it’s the [whole strategy](https://substack.com/redirect/c570b6f6-2b80-408d-9fe9-71a78ad1becc?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) of this creator. It gets him engagement because glazing is the default clickbait strategy.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!rzAA!,w_492,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff0d1fd83-51bb-4f43-8611-d08c9bdb5cf2_1201x854.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/5d7f8c72-5bbd-4ded-8a3b-785436fa360c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)Adverbs like literally are a key element of product launches and influencer campaigns because they ask you to re-emphasize what you just read.

Social media–first brands also adopt this strategy whenever they talk about running a billboard campaign. The type of copywriting that dominates social media often asks people to rethink and look deeper into things that might not be that deep.

##### Is dating the next stop for brand-building?

Meta saw how [everyone](https://substack.com/redirect/96683389-2169-449c-85b6-d640c94406eb?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) was referring to [LinkedIn as the new dating app](https://substack.com/redirect/b1d95067-c1c2-43e6-a4c2-4054ae663a91?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) in 2024, and they took their chance. The [NYT article](https://substack.com/redirect/26d49d80-ec77-47aa-9a46-cd77f04378c4?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) somewhat exaggerates the success of Facebook Dating by comparing it to the launch of Threads, but the new dating service is clearly benefiting from Meta’s ecosystem.

But as you see in this tweet and in the charts mentioned by [Michael J. Miraflor](https://substack.com/redirect/d8baede5-ccdb-44ef-9fcc-ba966c86e162?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), dating companies are struggling at the same time that more people than ever are online and seeking new relationships. Why is that?

Financially, Match Group is doing well in terms of marketing and organic user growth, but its paid subscription business is struggling.

A lot of this can be attributed to AI chatbots, since people are using ChatGPT and Perplexity for research, tips and general help in making their profiles look good. The dating platforms are essentially struggling to upsell their premium services.

On the behavioural and cultural side, I do not know much beyond the obvious: people continue to struggle with finding new dates, and millions of singles are now on the [waiting list for Raya](https://substack.com/redirect/a51c2c18-40ae-4e4c-ae2b-47c85cfe5321?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). Everything is [a dating app](https://substack.com/redirect/96683389-2169-449c-85b6-d640c94406eb?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) now.

The question is whether brands can help with this or monetise it. So far, brands have taken a more entertainment and influencer-focused approach, which is mostly limited to NYC and LA. Cava now has a social media series called [Bowlmates](https://substack.com/redirect/30111e81-d284-41c3-9564-cef81ba37f02?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) and multiple brands have sponsored [Street Hearts with Tiff](https://substack.com/redirect/3ab70d89-9249-4486-a598-de3cef47b891?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), another series from Fallen Media. 

As I wrote in [my last newsletter](https://substack.com/redirect/62f76fc9-98d8-45d8-bfe0-a86501d7ddb0?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), this is not solving the real problem. It is the old model of socialising through content and becoming the content. This, or Hinge sponsoring Substack creators, is not the solution. Most audiences want a private route, and brands need to solve for that through collaborations that encourage real-life engagement rather than more content.

##### Can the social media strategies that everyone is talking about actually work for you?

1. The ManyChat/Comment-for-DM: The conversation around this strategy has grown since it was [reported](https://substack.com/redirect/8aa1bda8-ea78-424c-b170-dd586b6324ef?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) that Zohran Mamdani used it to convert online followers into actual volunteers and voters.
	1. This strategy relies heavily on the type of content you create. You have to present a resource that makes sense for the audience. In most cases it is a listicle or a curated resource that people want to access without the hassle of visiting a website. So listicle-style content needs to be part of your content strategy if you want this lead generation approach to work.
		1. Creators who do not already use this content format often struggle to get comments from their audience, especially if they introduce it halfway through their journey. If you plan to use this strategy, it will take time for your audience to begin commenting. And you should comment from your other alt accounts to signal demand. That is the basic social-proof manufacturing every social media manager engages in.
2. Distributing content on Reddit: Media publishers are now actively posting links to their content in a range of subreddits, from r/WorldNews to r/fauxmoi.
	1. If you do not have much brand recognition and are a small business, link sharing alone will not achieve anything on Reddit. A large number of niche communities do not allow links or visual content. Some communities are moderated by business owners or affiliates who may ban you for posting about your brand.
		1. The Reddit strategy for brands is still built around text posts and comments. You have to do the social listening to find appropriate threads to comment on.
3. Repurpose your Instagram content to Pinterest: This strategy is old as f\*\*k, but it has never held real merit when it comes to actually gaining something from that additional visibility.
	1. Pinterest is more user-centric than Instagram and less consumption-centric than TikTok. Uploading content often leads to nothing but attention that sends viewers nowhere and leaves the brand with no real presence. The platform rewards user behaviour in much the same way Tumblr did in the 2010s.
		1. Brands that succeed on Pinterest are not simply repurposing their existing content and arranging it into new boards. They behave like users and pin non-branded content to signal to both the algorithm and the audience that they intend to curate something meaningful.

##### Is it Anti-AI marketing? Labubu 3.0 and Collaborations

I have a working theory that anything *recognisably human* done by brands will soon be interpreted as anti-AI marketing, even when AI is never mentioned. People are already proving this theory right. Apple creating the new [Apple TV logo](https://substack.com/redirect/b3321238-a985-49b8-93b0-c3e12e528c4f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) with real glass and practical effects had very little to do with being anti-AI. The same week, it was reported that they would pay a billion dollars to [integrate Gemini AI](https://substack.com/redirect/926f88de-7dcf-416f-9573-98662c105edd?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) into Siri. Most of the public overlooked that in order to praise the craft.

Similarly, luxury brands like [Hermès](https://substack.com/redirect/aaf53e96-e47e-46fc-96bd-2fd7c200a870?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), [Burberry](https://substack.com/redirect/c7f49c69-3a94-4edf-8539-b95e169773df?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) and [Bottega Veneta](https://substack.com/redirect/de53e521-04e7-436f-b548-75c7fecc01ee?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) have been leaning into more craft-focused collaborations, and the comments say this is anti-AI as well. As you can tell from the brand names, they already have long histories of working with artists and craftsmen, yet they are being reinterpreted as holding an anti-AI stance.

Then you have Labubu. Every month we get a new Labubu in a different shape or form. The trend of accessories and consumption as a status symbol is not going anywhere. The new replacement is the [Starbucks Bear Cup](https://substack.com/redirect/551c89a1-c76d-4ba3-95d0-ca639561f337?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), and Apple has joined the match with its [Issey Miyake collaboration](https://substack.com/redirect/c9503389-8d98-4335-939d-c13ecc84e3da?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). We have [Trader Joe’s micro tote bag](https://substack.com/redirect/23e0a044-31fb-47ed-a322-c7e325b6aae8?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), and IKEA recently launched [collectible plush keychains](https://substack.com/redirect/f4535895-e215-479c-8cc3-8418fe7967c3?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) that everyone went crazy for.

Accessories that sit outside the core product of a brand are going to be a major part of the marketing funnel in the future. This is borrowed partly from sports culture and partly from the old strategy of brand giveaways, where everyone ends up with a small symbol of brand affiliation. Have you ever wondered why every British kitchen has a [Sports Direct mug](https://substack.com/redirect/0d6f44bc-48df-4a7d-8de4-6ecfde2ea1c7?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)? 

That is my short take with a long yap. First, you do not need to state explicitly that your campaign is anti-AI; if it is good, people will decide that for you. Second, there is a new Labubu every month, and you should invest in accessories that can serve as long-term badges of consumption for your customers. It is a sad reality, but here we are.

##### You need these 10 hooks to get 18M+ views on IG Reels

Instagram is getting filled with creators giving you “Hooks” to capture attention. This video on the left got 10M views but the right one with same concept got 100M views.

“Hook changed everything”You don’t need more concepts and hooks to grow your account. More specifically, you don’t need to comment “XYZ” for a pdf of same old hooks. What you need is...

One: The best hook you have is “You.”

The perspective and references you have matter. Educate yourself about something called “stream of consciousness.” It’s one of many interesting subjects in literature. When you let your writing flow with details, you make people pay attention. It’s not easy, but adding a simple reference is a start and makes a difference.

Two: The strength of your idea and fake promises.

Don’t make a fake promise unless you are going to switch back and attack the audience that was looking for that specific answer. Always evaluate the strength of your idea by posting about it on different online forums or mentioning it cleverly in a post on Reddit or Twitter. Don’t be afraid of your idea getting stolen. Everything already exists in a different form and context. Your idea is true to you; know that. But you need to evaluate its strength to make sure the promise made at the start of your video or post is fulfilled.

Third: Understand the attention thresholds of different platforms and formats.

The biggest problem in marketing and the creator economy today is that people don’t understand how attention works and how it differs on each platform (psychology). Marketers are being loud on social media platforms where, no matter what, people’s attention levels are never high.

Winning with viral hooks and getting millions of views doesn’t make a difference if you don’t understand what type of attention you are capturing. That’s why you need to read more about the subject of attention and ask your audience.

Fourth: You need writing structure, not hooks.

Don’t watch a creator doing a hook breakdown. Watch someone doing a pun or joke. Break that writing structure down. I personally love creators doing weird breakdowns of movie plots. There are similar threads on Reddit where people describe movies in odd ways. Those types of writing practices will give you an edge over whatever hook is going viral. Stop focusing on one or two words to hook people; you are losing the opportunity to win people with the other words in your writing.

Fifth: Visual hooks are the new trend.

Some online gurus have moved from “Use this text hook” to “Visual hooks are the new thing.” It’s all about the context. Pottery creators can drop pots and get millions of views because they are selling attention.

Creators who have nothing to sell and rely on visual hooks are playing with and selling attention. It’s an easy product to sell, and often people don’t even buy it, but it appears to be an attention-sell. Unless you have the context and intent to use a visual hook, don’t do it. You are selling a real product first, attention second. If you sell attention first, it’s going to be a long journey.

Sixth: Symbolism is the second best hook ever.

Here are a few books on symbolism that will help you integrate more meaning and attention into your work: Ways of Seeing: John Berger, The Book of Symbols. Reflections on Archetypal Images, Man and His Symbols, and The Power of Myth.

The reason people end up using Hooks is “quick work”. Right?

##### Brands giving influencers and celebrities fake honorary titles for campaigns and brand activations.

The only marketing idea challenging the never-ending trend of “Let’s make a novelty candles for our brand.” is these [silly titles](https://substack.com/redirect/0d3f5867-fbcd-4f3c-8997-e695d9628728?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) that have lasted longer than I ever expected.

So is the “Chief Super Officer” title for Liza Koshy. If it’s part of the campaign, it’s 90% valid. The lack of creativity can always make it invalid, Hey Dude?

Meanwhile, only a few brands do a good job when they hire creators and customers. John Deere is an anomaly among all the brands that did the “let’s pay someone to create content and hype it all up with silly titles.”

It’s always a mixed bag. I do think it’s a risk worth taking, but now that we have so many examples, brands should take a look at the failures and do better. *What do you think about this campaign strategy?*

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">By now, we’ve all probably seen either this year’s AI-generated Coca-Cola ad campaign or last year’s. No matter what you think of the ad, it’s likely that Coke will continue to release this kind of campaign every year. And we should stop looking only at the end product and follow the discourse.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">I tell myself this all the time: we’re living in an era of discourse over discussion. What’s happening matters more than what’s happened.</p>
<h3 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.375em;">Consumers don’t really care about AI, Marketing or Brand. </h3>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">The funny thing about marketing and brand is that everything contradicts everything. We have research from Byron Sharp, Kantar, Oxford, and a couple of other sources showing that market availability, consistency, and marketing budgets influence what people buy and how often they do so. That is what makes people buy and eventually care about brands that inhabit their daily lives. It is practical and it works every time.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">But those same strategies are often co-opted by marketers and executives who abandon creativity and culture for higher profit. And that leaves a window open for other brands to create change, which does not happen by merely asking people whether they care about some XYZ brand purpose.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Brands like Nike, Signal, Patagonia, Ben &amp; Jerry’s, Apple, and many others are not just asking people to care about what they do. They are posing another question: Why do you not care about XYZ?</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">They are speaking in negatives and making people question the other brands. Tony’s Chocolonely and Oatly are almost obsessed with making people care more about what their industry is doing in comparison to what they do.</p>
<blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid #e85c57;margin: 20px 0;padding: 0;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);margin-left: 20px;line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">All that to say: we have known for decades that the average consumer cares only about price and product, but the purpose of a brand is always to make them care about something more than the product/service.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);margin-left: 20px;line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Not only because all humans desire to be purposeful, but because the financial market demands that companies have brand identities holding imaginary values of attention, status, and culture.</p>

A line like “learning XYZ language is so hard” is always overlaid on the screen, as predictable as the joke itself.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>The companies change from LingoTok to PingoAI to Airlearn, but the strategy remains the same. Airlearn has pushed things a little further with a new </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/b8532ff2-636a-4727-bbbe-b7181d95658c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">billboard campaign</a><span> in NYC and a major influencer collaboration in </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/f785a97e-f3ce-45a5-a0d2-371c104bb92e?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">India</a><span>. <p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>But these startups aren’t goon-baiting to the extent of the </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/555f347e-974f-400d-be13-149bcfa2a85d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Chinese language teachers </a><span>and some other English tutors on Instagram and TikTok, who are attracting new audiences mostly by </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/88537c22-7e24-4584-bb7b-01e22c199a85?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">teaching</a><span> NSFW phrases that you </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/e9c09ad6-2cbf-4c0e-ae4e-c983f7952328?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">literally</a><span> can’t use in public. <p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>What did I just say, “literally”? That’s the word. <p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>Meta saw how </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/96683389-2169-449c-85b6-d640c94406eb?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">everyone</a><span> was referring to </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/b1d95067-c1c2-43e6-a4c2-4054ae663a91?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">LinkedIn as the new dating app</a><span> in 2024, and they took their chance. The </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/26d49d80-ec77-47aa-9a46-cd77f04378c4?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">NYT article</a><span> somewhat exaggerates the success of Facebook Dating by comparing it to the launch of Threads, but the new dating service is clearly benefiting from Meta’s ecosystem.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>But as you see in this tweet and in the charts mentioned by </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/d8baede5-ccdb-44ef-9fcc-ba966c86e162?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Michael J. Miraflor</a><span>, dating companies are struggling at the same time that more people than ever are online and seeking new relationships. Why is that?</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Financially, Match Group is doing well in terms of marketing and organic user growth, but its paid subscription business is struggling.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">A lot of this can be attributed to AI chatbots, since people are using ChatGPT and Perplexity for research, tips and general help in making their profiles look good. The dating platforms are essentially struggling to upsell their premium services.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>On the behavioural and cultural side, I do not know much beyond the obvious: people continue to struggle with finding new dates, and millions of singles are now on the </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/a51c2c18-40ae-4e4c-ae2b-47c85cfe5321?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">waiting list for Raya</a><span>. Everything is </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/96683389-2169-449c-85b6-d640c94406eb?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">a dating app</a><span> now.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>The question is whether brands can help with this or monetise it. So far, brands have taken a more entertainment and influencer-focused approach, which is mostly limited to NYC and LA. Cava now has a social media series called </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/30111e81-d284-41c3-9564-cef81ba37f02?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Bowlmates</a><span> and multiple brands have sponsored </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/3ab70d89-9249-4486-a598-de3cef47b891?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Street Hearts with Tiff</a><span>, another series from Fallen Media. </span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>As I wrote in </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/62f76fc9-98d8-45d8-bfe0-a86501d7ddb0?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">my last newsletter</a><span>, this is not solving the real problem. It is the old model of socialising through content and becoming the content. This, or Hinge sponsoring Substack creators, is not the solution. Most audiences want a private route, and brands need to solve for that through collaborations that encourage real-life engagement rather than more content.</span></p>

<h3 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.375em;">Can the social media strategies that everyone is talking about actually work for you? </h3>
<ol style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;">
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;"><span>The ManyChat/Comment-for-DM: The conversation around this strategy has grown since it was </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/8aa1bda8-ea78-424c-b170-dd586b6324ef?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">reported</a><span> that Zohran Mamdani used it to convert online followers into actual volunteers and voters.</span></p>
<ol style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;list-style-type: lower-alpha;"><li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;">This strategy relies heavily on the type of content you create. You have to present a resource that makes sense for the audience. In most cases it is a listicle or a curated resource that people want to access without the hassle of visiting a website. So listicle-style content needs to be part of your content strategy if you want this lead generation approach to work.</p>
<ol style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;list-style-type: lower-roman;"><li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Creators who do not already use this content format often struggle to get comments from their audience, especially if they introduce it halfway through their journey. If you plan to use this strategy, it will take time for your audience to begin commenting. And you should comment from your other alt accounts to signal demand. That is the basic social-proof manufacturing every social media manager engages in. </p></li></ol>
</li></ol>
</li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;">Distributing content on Reddit: Media publishers are now actively posting links to their content in a range of subreddits, from r/WorldNews to r/fauxmoi.</p>
<ol style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;list-style-type: lower-alpha;"><li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;">If you do not have much brand recognition and are a small business, link sharing alone will not achieve anything on Reddit. A large number of niche communities do not allow links or visual content. Some communities are moderated by business owners or affiliates who may ban you for posting about your brand. </p>
<ol style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;list-style-type: lower-roman;"><li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">The Reddit strategy for brands is still built around text posts and comments. You have to do the social listening to find appropriate threads to comment on. </p></li></ol>
</li></ol>
</li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;">Repurpose your Instagram content to Pinterest: This strategy is old as f\*\*k, but it has never held real merit when it comes to actually gaining something from that additional visibility.</p>
<ol style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;list-style-type: lower-alpha;"><li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;">Pinterest is more user-centric than Instagram and less consumption-centric than TikTok. Uploading content often leads to nothing but attention that sends viewers nowhere and leaves the brand with no real presence. The platform rewards user behaviour in much the same way Tumblr did in the 2010s. </p>
<ol style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;list-style-type: lower-roman;"><li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Brands that succeed on Pinterest are not simply repurposing their existing content and arranging it into new boards. They behave like users and pin non-branded content to signal to both the algorithm and the audience that they intend to curate something meaningful.</p></li></ol>

<h3 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.375em;">Is it Anti-AI marketing? Labubu 3.0 and Collaborations</h3>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>I have a working theory that anything </span><em>recognisably human</em><span> done by brands will soon be interpreted as anti-AI marketing, even when AI is never mentioned. People are already proving this theory right. Apple creating the new </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/b3321238-a985-49b8-93b0-c3e12e528c4f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Apple TV logo</a><span> with real glass and practical effects had very little to do with being anti-AI. The same week, it was reported that they would pay a billion dollars to </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/926f88de-7dcf-416f-9573-98662c105edd?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">integrate Gemini AI</a><span> into Siri. Most of the public overlooked that in order to praise the craft.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>Similarly, luxury brands like </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/aaf53e96-e47e-46fc-96bd-2fd7c200a870?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Hermès</a><span>, </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/c7f49c69-3a94-4edf-8539-b95e169773df?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Burberry</a><span> and </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/de53e521-04e7-436f-b548-75c7fecc01ee?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Bottega Veneta</a><span> have been leaning into more craft-focused collaborations, and the comments say this is anti-AI as well. As you can tell from the brand names, they already have long histories of working with artists and craftsmen, yet they are being reinterpreted as holding an anti-AI stance.</span></p>

<h3 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.375em;">You need these 10 hooks to get 18M+ views on IG Reels</h3>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Instagram is getting filled with creators giving you “Hooks” to capture attention. This video on the left got 10M views but the right one with same concept got 100M views.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">“Hook changed everything”You don’t need more concepts and hooks to grow your account. More specifically, you don’t need to comment “XYZ” for a pdf of same old hooks. What you need is...</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">One: The best hook you have is “You.”</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">The perspective and references you have matter. Educate yourself about something called “stream of consciousness.” It’s one of many interesting subjects in literature. When you let your writing flow with details, you make people pay attention. It’s not easy, but adding a simple reference is a start and makes a difference.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Two: The strength of your idea and fake promises.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Don’t make a fake promise unless you are going to switch back and attack the audience that was looking for that specific answer. Always evaluate the strength of your idea by posting about it on different online forums or mentioning it cleverly in a post on Reddit or Twitter. Don’t be afraid of your idea getting stolen. Everything already exists in a different form and context. Your idea is true to you; know that. But you need to evaluate its strength to make sure the promise made at the start of your video or post is fulfilled.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Third: Understand the attention thresholds of different platforms and formats.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">The biggest problem in marketing and the creator economy today is that people don’t understand how attention works and how it differs on each platform (psychology). Marketers are being loud on social media platforms where, no matter what, people’s attention levels are never high.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Winning with viral hooks and getting millions of views doesn’t make a difference if you don’t understand what type of attention you are capturing. That’s why you need to read more about the subject of attention and ask your audience.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Fourth: You need writing structure, not hooks.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Don’t watch a creator doing a hook breakdown. Watch someone doing a pun or joke. Break that writing structure down. I personally love creators doing weird breakdowns of movie plots. There are similar threads on Reddit where people describe movies in odd ways. Those types of writing practices will give you an edge over whatever hook is going viral. Stop focusing on one or two words to hook people; you are losing the opportunity to win people with the other words in your writing.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Fifth: Visual hooks are the new trend.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Some online gurus have moved from “Use this text hook” to “Visual hooks are the new thing.” It’s all about the context. Pottery creators can drop pots and get millions of views because they are selling attention.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Creators who have nothing to sell and rely on visual hooks are playing with and selling attention. It’s an easy product to sell, and often people don’t even buy it, but it appears to be an attention-sell. Unless you have the context and intent to use a visual hook, don’t do it. You are selling a real product first, attention second. If you sell attention first, it’s going to be a long journey.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Sixth: Symbolism is the second best hook ever.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Here are a few books on symbolism that will help you integrate more meaning and attention into your work: Ways of Seeing: John Berger, The Book of Symbols. Reflections on Archetypal Images, Man and His Symbols, and The Power of Myth.</p>
