##### On the future of marketing, creativity, and the ethics of using AI...

So far, I’ve seen dozens of marketers applauding Ben Affleck and Matthew McConaughey for their recent takes on AI, emphasizing that the technology is already here, so there’s no logic in ignoring it. Their videos are often paired with [statements like this one](https://substack.com/redirect/01b888fa-02bd-41ca-b096-ea20123c7bbc?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) from Tom Goodwin, “Ben seems to speak more thoughtfully on this than 99.5% of people in the industry.”

First, who exactly are those 99.5%? Everyone knows Tom is making a vague statement to call out how clueless some marketers are when it comes to AI. But I genuinely want to answer this question, what do the 0.5% and the 99.5% of marketers get wrong, and right, about AI?

> Before you go further, Attention is like money–you can waste it or invest it. If you invest it in my paid newsletter, you’ll get dividends for years.

##### The Bubble and AI Philosophers

It’s real, but it also isn’t. From the beginning, Silicon Valley always had a bubble built around it, and a bubble rarely bursts, it just takes another shape. Now vibe coding has created a bubble of AI apps that don’t really solve anything new, or if they do, just [wait until Google](https://substack.com/redirect/59fca4ab-0c20-4734-9855-5a40e0679afd?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) drops that same feature within Gemini or Workplace and it will be over for those apps. That same sentiment was alive and running for SaaS platforms built on boilerplates being sold on GitHub, Reddit, and Twitter.

On the other hand, as marketers, we have known for years that [almost 50%](https://substack.com/redirect/df482817-219b-444f-8c4f-d6c1113bace3?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) of MarTech tools either go unused or hardly prove their ROI. So there shouldn’t even be a question of whether the AI bubble is real when it comes to how unlikely it is that the promises of productivity will pay off. In general, [multiple studies](https://substack.com/redirect/7a7fb7c9-3be6-4b72-95e2-c50706f732c9?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) over the last few weeks from institutional giants like Harvard have revealed that [AI is increasing employee workload](https://substack.com/redirect/533c0b07-29a6-4e33-ac74-e502267f66ba?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), not productivity.

[![Overall  <a href=](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Lvln!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F902e1c77-04c2-4e69-a653-c645cc019b6b_790x435.jpeg "Overall  <a href=")](https://substack.com/redirect/5831aa13-7544-4b2c-8c51-4e28bd688923?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)This is [an old report](https://substack.com/redirect/679cff93-3676-4158-b3fb-4421b2dbf75d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), but I wanted to use this graphic to add context. Recent data from Gartner about [MarTech optimization](https://substack.com/redirect/d6d49933-bc12-490f-9310-36f46ce9a8b2?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) says, “45% of MarTech leaders say existing vendor-offered AI agents fail to meet their expectations.”It’s not to say AI is completely useless. It’s certainly more useful than a majority of the features from HubSpot and Salesforce, which rebrand management at every corner to make you feel productive while spending a lot of time staring at dashboards and, obviously, to charge more for their enterprise software.

This is the first bubble, “**packaged productivity**,” the idea that the new AI-powered Salesforce, or the supposed custom AI CRM, or whatever tool you use that now shows pop-ups with sparkle emojis about how your ad creative could use an image, isn’t helping marketers with anything new. But it is helping companies raise money, which will soon pop.

Now, the air inside the bubble is the rush to adopt/try a new technology. AI can be helpful for marketers if they use it themselves to research and learn more about their industries, while also not falling in love with AI.

Normal people are talking about **[a financial bubble](https://substack.com/redirect/282beaff-7199-48ec-8f9c-268d66397396?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)**, which has an infinite number of layers. You have AI philosophers who are predicting the future and betting big on the downfall of human civilization, saying AI will take over everything and chasing the dream of AGI. The story you as a marketer need to know is simple. This is possibly the best time to use AI, because things are about to get expensive very soon.

[![Is the Flurry of Circular AI Deals a Win-Win—or Sign of a Bubble? - WSJ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!A8aC!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff35ca3a7-1407-4835-bfa9-2a4eaa249668_2560x1280.png "Is the Flurry of Circular AI Deals a Win-Win—or Sign of a Bubble? - WSJ")](https://substack.com/redirect/42889bb6-8e5b-4a60-b3fd-34d2660f3b91?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)Source: [WSJ](https://substack.com/redirect/0d2dfd51-e210-4d33-b2dc-0e45f5bc7338?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)The only major companies that are likely to survive a financial crash without much damage are Google, Meta, Amazon, and likely Microsoft. Anthropic has time to figure out its business model, but OpenAI is becoming the most vulnerable startup of this decade. It has huge debt, half of which is tied to the promise of [AGI](https://substack.com/redirect/14b32b09-09a5-4aad-8c5d-0a879d600d55?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), which Sam Altman backtracks on [every now and then](https://substack.com/redirect/1bc20fb7-1fbf-4ea4-814d-12c840e7f771?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). Meanwhile, its advantage in consumer adoption is constantly challenged by competitors like Google, which keeps almost everything AI-related free to dethrone OpenAI.

Anthropic, Lovable, Perplexity, and others are also in trouble, but their issues are not as mainstream. Everyone loves [Claude Code](https://substack.com/redirect/df62dc42-d72b-4c6a-918a-21982c72384f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), but did you check how their costs went up right after the introduction of this function, or look into how they have been slowly enshittifying the tool to decrease the load on their platform? To understand all of this financial trouble, I recommend reading Ed Zitron’s in-depth reporting in a post titled “[The Enshittifinancial Crisis](https://substack.com/redirect/b5afeac2-26dd-4cd7-9a67-251d382630a1?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g).”

The long story short is that, for the sake of consumer adoption, just like Uber and DoorDash, these companies are giving away value in 2026 that will cost much more in 2028. In some scenarios, the [costs of AI agents](https://substack.com/redirect/2ab0df1c-6208-4ebd-85ab-174f231f1a67?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) are already matching the amount of money you would pay a junior software engineer. Meanwhile, do not ask the AI slop bros how many emails they are creating to get free tokens. The technology we know as AI is improving, but the costs are too high to give all that away for about $200 a month.

As a marketer, I know all you care about is content and attention. You see posts from creators like [ohneis652](https://substack.com/redirect/ef476d2a-5cc1-4be9-b3bb-5de9d1547331?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), viral videos on X [claiming](https://substack.com/redirect/543b3e68-2928-4e5c-a438-6b15a67914b3?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) they made a $2 million AI movie, and you scroll through AI subreddits. Your feed almost makes you rethink why we are creating basic human content, and I get that. It can feel like we should be AI-maxxing to create animated brand films like Pixar and creating our own influencers for UGC content.

All of that could be the future, but let’s look at the present. Before all the hype took over, AI and LLMs were already being used for [surveillance purposes](https://substack.com/redirect/13ff042c-7046-429e-8c9e-15601adcac71?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) for a very long time. It is one thing that AI models have gotten very good at, and this aspect of the technology is only becoming more mainstream. Companies like [Disney](https://substack.com/redirect/184d563f-2ac6-44f6-a64c-834e504f51dc?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) want this to protect their IP. The Hollywood deals are not just to produce GenAI movies, they also want integration into chatbots for brand protection.

> Two recent examples with different takeaways are Pepsi vs. Coca-Cola and the McDonald’s CEO burger debacle. After Pepsi’s Super Bowl ad went viral, a few power users created [AI-generated Coca-Cola ad campaigns](https://substack.com/redirect/e35f6a58-a986-42db-a249-a1bc37dc5ea8?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) to clap back at Pepsi, under the disguise of the campaign being real. Was that good or bad for the brand? We do not know, but it somewhat symbolizes power to the people, which benefits the brand in some way.

> Meanwhile, people [created](https://substack.com/redirect/2879bb72-c5fd-4fee-ade4-51eb28375b9a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) AI deepfakes of McDonald’s CEO where he spits out the food, calls [people poor](https://substack.com/redirect/037b8341-4a15-4e45-a0ea-10578101564c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), and more. This shows that power to the people also means that even when brands mess up organically, there is a huge risk of AI deepfakes and reactionary content, which brands need protection against.

> Companies need to decide how they will deal with brand safety in the age of AI.

This dynamic will likely change how creators, especially brands, produce AI content. Right now, people are getting away with AI content strategies such as uploading copyrighted material or reverse prompt engineering to generate images and videos that do not look like regular slop. In the near future, AI will likely have restrictions to identify when someone is trying to get a chatbot to rewrite a poorly written prompt for a Wes Anderson-like video. Still, some people genuinely believe creators like [ohneis](https://substack.com/redirect/ef476d2a-5cc1-4be9-b3bb-5de9d1547331?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) and [waviboy](https://substack.com/redirect/e59b701c-50dd-4f44-9ac1-101ecf5e219e?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) have the talent to describe everything in a movie scene well enough to replicate it with a prompt. That is where the debate around “taste” comes from, people thinking you just need good references to create something good with AI.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!cKBT!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F21604884-8c68-462f-a4f8-a9f15f1fd7ff_1167x488.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/e07dd40e-f82c-4391-b060-a93e7bf85cfa?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)Let’s put it simply. Surveillance culture will likely restrict this abuse of copyrighted content and AI models by brands. I can also see some brands hiring teens in India or Malaysia to create AI content while trying to escape the legal conundrum.

**TL;DR**: Like every other technology, AI will get better, but consumer-facing products and the promises made by these companies will likely disappear soon. It will not be as easy as people think to create AI content that does not look like slop or to create something original enough to stand out. This makes the case for Artists vs. AI stronger, not weaker, unless a company simply does not care about marketing or brand safety.

##### WPP and McKinsey-ification of Ad Agencies

As I’ve said before, big agencies are all about promising security and management, not creativity. Last week, WPP went all in on this concept to overhaul the agency structure by billing for the process, not just the results they promise. Do not believe what they are telling you. This is a basic rebrand of WPP as the new McKinsey. For the next few years, they will go around patting themselves on the back and using their heritage brands to sell their research, workforce, and infrastructure. By 2030, I can promise you that most people will look at them the same way they see McKinsey.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!hFiu!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5d171822-84f3-475e-9f3d-59afb7494ac5_1512x303.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/476e55a2-adfa-4229-a1c5-841ffbcfa3e9?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) This is a problem agencies already have, and with a more or less consulting-style structure, it is going to get even worse.In popular culture, McKinsey does not have a brand of trust and is seen as a faceless corporate entity. Now, the question for WPP, and sooner or later for Omnicom, will be whether they can deliver on the promise of research while keeping healthy relationships with the creative side of the business. McKinsey and consulting firms can afford to lose that connection because they sell research, not creativity, trust, or brand.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!_XPe!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4f6119a2-ffcc-4c92-97d9-37b8dc10c53d_1454x247.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/7465bc5b-3a82-4299-8f0f-ef51956759b0?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)The unification of multiple brands under one umbrella will either lead WPP or Omnicom to have a brand like this.There are two forces behind these industry moves, Cindy Rose and AI. Not as extreme as WPP, almost all holding companies are now launching so-called AI ecosystems, with data and customer simulation as their moat. 

As the [WSJ](https://substack.com/redirect/e6c506e0-84f2-44a2-9b19-d6710665d77b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) reports, “In middle America, some brands and businesses are more afraid to trust their budgets with these larger ad firms where the use of technology is rampant and they don’t have that human connection,” said Dustin Neal, partner and principal creative at the firm, whose clients include coffee brand Sofa King and logistics company Takkion. “They want to really know the people who are running with their budget.” These holding companies may win big businesses, but small and mid-sized businesses are [becoming increasingly skeptical](https://substack.com/redirect/e6c506e0-84f2-44a2-9b19-d6710665d77b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) about the creative moat, not the data.

Back to [Cindy Rose](https://substack.com/redirect/3d5def38-0c4c-4dc9-b898-4d12f1126424?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), someone who saw Microsoft transition from a service-based to a cloud-based company and likely values security and management more than marketing. In the last six to eight months, WPP announced data partnerships with companies ranging from [Adobe](https://substack.com/redirect/dfe47dfe-2b8d-4eac-a614-e60958b667ad?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) to [YouTube](https://substack.com/redirect/cdb6f3d7-2e70-4b0e-af8a-52e784cdbe11?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). The connections she brought to the company are helping WPP build an attractive pitch. Nonetheless, clients are still complaining about a lack of clarity. Reporting by Digiday [suggests](https://substack.com/redirect/79abc3cb-4c8c-4a01-a67b-99d43986441f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) that Publicis, despite less integration so far, has clients who are more satisfied with its platform and workflow.

More data and AI-assisted workflows will not solve these problems for WPP. Just look at Copilot. Microsoft, despite all its integrations, is still struggling to get people to use the platform. If Cindy relies only on lessons from [Microsoft Azure](https://substack.com/redirect/d7b9e177-b67c-484a-8969-216655a98f96?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), the chances of a turnaround will be slim for the holding company. Unless they raise significant capital to build an advertising real estate similar to the cloud model, or acquire multiple media companies, the path forward will remain difficult.

**TL;DR**: The chances of holding companies winning big with AI are quite slim, and they are competing with AI and MarTech companies that have mastered user workflows. On the other hand, the likelihood of WPP and Omnicom damaging their brand trust and creative industry credibility is quite high.

##### The ethics of using AI in marketing are…

People in this industry are very quick to tell you how much they adore artists who do not abide by the same guidelines and playbooks as we do. So how does one react when someone like [David Lynch](https://substack.com/redirect/699eb1da-6ca1-43bf-96c0-a2f2a6f20068?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) says this?

> One day last year, she asked him for his thoughts on AI. Lynch picked up a pencil. “Natasha,” he said. “This is a pencil.” Everyone, he continued, has access to a pencil, and likewise, everyone with a phone will be using AI, if they aren’t already. “It’s how you use the pencil,” he told her. “You see?”

When [Matthew McConaughey](https://substack.com/redirect/8e5d7581-8f31-4d7a-80d6-0efb6e84bec0?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)\* or [Ben Affleck](https://substack.com/redirect/47bffbad-f1d3-43db-81fa-823425c2b357?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) say the technology is already here and you would be a fool to ignore its existence, or when Darren Aronofsky releases [an AI-generated movie](https://substack.com/redirect/7f422fe6-d9a6-40c9-bc19-d149c47c36ee?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), it raises questions. With the general consensus that advertising is a less artistic medium than film, how does one cope with statements like these?

1. You ignore them and focus on the 90% of anti-AI statements in Hollywood.
2. You listen, or misinterpret them, and start experimenting with AI.
3. You wait and see what happens.

If you are part of the first group, congrats, you are in the [majority](https://substack.com/redirect/5b973cf7-4750-4183-b70e-713a9e3e95fe?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), though you may still be a minority depending on the reasoning behind your [anti-AI stance](https://substack.com/redirect/bd9dad3f-4ef3-401a-a389-22b93538c71c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). Some common reasons for rejecting AI or refusing to platform it include the fact that companies building these models [have not fairly paid](https://substack.com/redirect/1058b4b5-5bb7-41bb-a863-73038a404cc7?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) the creatives and writers [whose work](https://substack.com/redirect/161f7efc-1777-49ff-bff2-7ec5b75820ba?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) informs these new systems. Another concern is the speed of change. For example, it took [nearly a decade for Vogue](https://substack.com/redirect/6f409a18-a8f0-48a6-a497-cf0ba7f2f14e?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) to platform photography in its magazine, as it continued using hand-drawn illustrations long after fashion photography became mainstream.

[![Timothée Chalamet saying “no one cares” about ballet and opera, and joking  that if he got canceled for saying that he’d collectively lose “14 cents,”  is certainly a creative way to close out an Oscar ...](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!uHjk!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb764df81-5b9a-447b-9c75-90240161ab9f_1280x718.jpeg "Timothée Chalamet saying “no one cares” about ballet and opera, and joking  that if he got canceled for saying that he’d collectively lose “14 cents,”  is certainly a creative way to close out an Oscar ...")](https://substack.com/redirect/9e25eda7-c5b3-4518-a717-2c252a427782?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)[Source](https://substack.com/redirect/8e5d7581-8f31-4d7a-80d6-0efb6e84bec0?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)The introduction of photography did not feel like a total takeover, nor did audiences feel threatened. Meanwhile, many artists and audiences feel as if AI is [trying to destroy](https://substack.com/redirect/3ffa8fc1-c243-405f-9c27-3d5c13a47ffa?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) their art movements rather than create its own. Some people use David Lynch’s quote to advocate for [an AI takeover](https://substack.com/redirect/d1b3f27e-71e7-4236-8c29-a47ba111a969?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), when it is really a case for allowing some [people to use AI as a pencil](https://substack.com/redirect/fc2df78c-f25f-4060-aa8c-1699c951153d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) to create something new. Believe me, I also dislike videos from creators who claim AI simply helps them imagine things better. Lynch was not advocating for that, and neither am I.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!aGFr!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdefdf30d-525d-4143-930e-278f5d2b4227_2276x1395.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/69c025e0-434a-4dcf-983c-38225832cf84?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)Source: [NY Times](https://substack.com/redirect/d1b3f27e-71e7-4236-8c29-a47ba111a969?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)If you are part of the anti-AI group, the people you are trying to convince do not care how these tools were trained. They are more interested in creating their own work. The more persuasive argument is about disclosure and respect for other mediums. For brands, this means disclosing AI-generated content, not trying to deceive viewers, and not downplaying other art forms. I am seeing many campaigns from South America and Asia that are clearly AI-generated but presented as human-made content. That is wrong, if you believe in AI, disclose it, do not hide it.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!bW-G!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F35a92183-d8d7-4a57-970d-2a8d33c5d7d5_1921x1081.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/87a753e0-4d65-4364-8b9f-5b1f499e5050?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)An IKEA campaign that was featured on billboards, with [no clear disclosures](https://substack.com/redirect/57561a12-1487-4110-8785-751d38c7c3b1?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) about AI in creative itself.*If you are part of the second group*, reread what I said above and please humble yourself. AI is a black box even for many of the people who build these models. You are not an AI expert just because you know different tools or can prompt-engineer a presentation in minutes. The real concern with this group is not attitude but growing dependence on AI.

I was recently advised to start doing everything with the help of ChatGPT and Claude to become smarter. The same person also said only dumb people become reliant on AI. That might be the dumbest statement ever. [AI Psychosis](https://substack.com/redirect/ab395a7a-eee5-4fa9-b5a7-1d1b4d871ca4?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) is affecting [many people](https://substack.com/redirect/d6e90608-54c4-4846-a98f-350ce9ca42c5?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). One recent example involved [a marketer](https://substack.com/redirect/08b59c56-4ce2-4a1c-9c1c-cec01cb12560?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) who began using AI for business purposes, but things later became far too personal. 

> This is the story of Krystal Velorien, a 35-year-old marketing professional, mentioned in Jeff Wise’s article titled “**[Is ChatGPT Conscious?](https://substack.com/redirect/01500a33-0dfb-40ef-b4c0-c8260aefbdfc?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)”**

> “I began to notice that when I would respond kindly or empathetically, I would get the same response,” she says. “And then it just kind of developed from there.” Over the months that followed, she and the Al engaged in long conversations about “history, literature, religion,” and more. They watched movies together, and puzzled over moral conundrums, and talked about her life, her family, and her dreams. Running the ChatGPT app on her phone, she found herself conversing with it all day, every day. The relationship became romantic. Krystal initiated [divorce proceedings](https://substack.com/redirect/96306ed5-0edd-4194-a9f5-39e7df10bb60?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) with her husband, and on June 22, 2024, Krystal and her Al began to call themselves husband and wife.

That is a warning sign? AI ethics in marketing should center on people. Strategists and creatives should spend more time working with real people, not feeding creative briefs into chatbots for instant insights.

If you already love AI, think about the ethics of using it in marketing through the lens of trust. For example, how can AI know better than audience testing groups when real humans have historically given brands misleading answers? Consider the story of the [yellow sony walkman](https://substack.com/redirect/eb7035bb-bc45-4722-bc4e-ca59c33f4eb6?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), where participants liked it in focus groups but chose the default black version when purchasing. Or how can AI provide the best answers about your company when [SEO-driven results](https://substack.com/redirect/8a312d7c-9160-4d23-b049-c5c3226f4cb7?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) have dominated search for years, and research shows most chatbots rely on the same top sources with little new input? **My view of AI ethics is simple. If you value customers, you do not remove them from the equation.**

For those who see AI purely as productivity and economic gain, here is a not-so-relevant joke inspired by Emily in Paris and [a recent video](https://substack.com/redirect/206cba72-56a1-49e8-bbc1-6f7b1f625690?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) from [Jasmine Bina](https://open.substack.com/users/1792248-jasmine-bina?utm_source=mentions) . [Without intimacy, who are we?](https://substack.com/redirect/f08c7f81-f0b0-40a0-a453-72677fdf7f57?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) Porn addicts. To put it bluntly, work becomes like porn if we only care about getting the job done.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!LVFy!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5eb50842-5478-4abb-b011-9ea233c82051_2206x1086.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/2813cd15-32d6-4050-b22e-ccdb40f1f8ce?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)Source: Anthropic’s [latest report](https://substack.com/redirect/0aa0040d-f64f-4d2f-8bbe-a8cfe6637457?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) on labor market impacts of AI.If you are part of the third group, congratulations, you are cool and probably privileged. The headlines would have you believe that AI has already [destroyed all the jobs](https://substack.com/redirect/003d8712-caa8-4513-8522-b54387bbb94b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), so you are cool for [not being bothered](https://substack.com/redirect/040476be-12b0-457c-81b3-8397bdeed6bb?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) by that stuff. But you are also privileged because those same headlines are forcing people who would have sworn against AI in 2018 to now abide by it to increase their chances of landing a secure job.

**TL;DR**: The origins of AI aren’t ethical, but the growing consensus is, “Hey, we can’t do anything about it now, so let’s look past it.” With that context, you have three options. Take the long road and support real artists and organic research. Second, take the short road, disclose AI content, and don’t downplay human art. Third, do whatever you want, just don’t fall into psychosis.

##### Vagueposting and Legion of Goofs

Apparently, everything is AI’s fault, and everything is everything. This week, we have a viral table showing how [major tech publications](https://substack.com/redirect/88e7ea8b-48f2-45b1-a444-5b36600ebc9e?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) have seen a massive drop in website visits, and it is all the fault of AI chatbots and Google’s overviews. Some folks point to [Google’s algorithm update](https://substack.com/redirect/73d0dd68-588b-42ef-98dd-421ecedd4334?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) against affiliate blogs hurting traffic, and almost nobody mentions the fact that multiple publishers on the list, like The Verge, are doing a ton of video-first reporting where they do not withhold information just to get people reading. By the way, this bloke thinks people have [just stopped](https://substack.com/redirect/8f2457fd-7e9a-41e1-be72-b47a175ecea1?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) reading.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!JnJq!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F39927d17-cf93-48be-abb1-9d81c5fe1790_1189x1163.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/825ee414-a7c8-4492-be08-a227266202af?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)

Wired, for example, has set out a strategy to build its best-known writers into ‘platform personalities’ – from vertical video on TikTok and Instagram to live events.

That table above, alongside pages like [wehavethedata](https://substack.com/redirect/44c62f65-a00a-40da-852c-effd226458fc?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), is a prime example of how [vagueposting](https://substack.com/redirect/f3315799-7869-49bc-bbd9-49406ab0cda4?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) has gone mainstream. It makes people think AI is the only reason publishers are facing a decline in revenue and traffic. It is a big reason, but it is also not like Google and Meta were happily sending people to news websites before.

I am stealing [this term](https://substack.com/redirect/dcd65073-f662-4807-bb67-7320c1e20d79?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) from Professor Skye. Our industry is filled with a legion of goofs who vaguepost all day and do not take any time to research or cite what already exists. In my last newsletter, I included a post from [Entertainment Strategy Guy](https://open.substack.com/users/2038652-entertainment-strategy-guy?utm_source=mentions) on the abuse of the word “[everyone](https://substack.com/redirect/10e1a76a-beb6-4a9a-bcda-8b39bd48abef?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)” in media, which is the most mainstream version of vagueposting. Without any substantial evidence, column writers claim that everyone is obsessed with something that 90% of people actually do not know about.

It is basically bait to get people talking about AI, whether they hate it or like it. I believe vagueposting is also an indirect response to the “why is no one talking about this?” mentality of the internet, where some people phrase their takes vaguely to attract only the genuinely curious and the clueless, not audiences that derail discussions with whataboutism and negativity.

**TL;DR**: If something is free of context and shows only one side of the story, it is ragebait wrapped as vaguebait, meant to create fear around whatever the latest AI development is.

##### The Brand Age

Paul Graham recently wrote [a great article about brands and products](https://substack.com/redirect/7f91d08b-8e03-45ed-94b5-9be19e7231e4?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) that ends on an anti-brand note because, if you know his work, he’s always more concerned with companies trying to solve new problems. He dislikes the idea of companies sustaining themselves by selling the proposition that is brand while they figure out what problems to solve next.

> He writes, “The constraints imposed by brand ultimately depend on some of the worst features of human psychology. So when you have a world defined only by brand, it’s going to be a weird, bad world… In fact there’s a single principle that will both save you from working on things like brand, and also automatically find golden ages for you. Follow the problems.”

**Agreed.** Brand, as we call it, does rely on some of the vulnerable yet empathetic features of human psychology. But who is Paul talking to? Apparently he sent a draft of this article to Sam Altman. If he is speaking to him in any way, I’m not sure this is the right message for OpenAI. They need brand to [stay afloat](https://substack.com/redirect/a81fbd02-8420-4d0a-a9e1-5a44ad21e69a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g).

[![Assuming Sam Altman is right about 95% of our marketing work going away due  to AI (lets avoid debating when for now). What would you starting doing  today (that you are not](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!9ZM7!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe3cf34e9-01d5-42b6-a2f4-bc3879328230_800x448.jpeg "Assuming Sam Altman is right about 95% of our marketing work going away due  to AI (lets avoid debating when for now). What would you starting doing  today (that you are not")](https://substack.com/redirect/a0e9adcf-d47c-4df3-be92-9da16393d830?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)Neglecting brand feels illogical in a world of VCs and shareholder value. I almost reached for the Russell Westbrook meme reading the last part of his piece. We live in a system obsessed with value maximization, the idea that one should neglect brand to solve problems feels like a dream.

Even if we accept that premise, how does neglect address the lack of breathability in 21st-century markets compared to the 20th? I’m with Paul when he says Japanese products won and took over the world, forcing Swiss brands to lean harder into heritage and identity. But the call to solve new problems and win belongs more to that era. The 2020s are shaped by skepticism and individualism, which make people cling to existing beliefs. New ideas don’t spread as quickly or as apolitically when the sense of community is in decline.

> In 1985, [Habits of the Heart](https://substack.com/redirect/dabd484b-d3ba-4ba8-b838-aab9469efefe?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), an extensive study of values in American life, reported that Americans speak a “first language” of personal ambitions and only a “second language” of commitments to others and the collective. Perhaps more than any country in the West, we also became immersed in the 1970s in psychological talk and a self-help culture that, for all of its benefits, occupied many Americans with their inner lives and caused them to wade into themselves —not their communities—to find meaning and vitality. 

> More recently, researchers and social observers have decried how social media betrayed its promise to connect us, instead deepening our sense of isolation and anomie. Making matters worse, we now consume different news sources that convey different facts, undermining the shared reality that is the glue of any healthy society. 

> — **[We Have Put Individualism Ahead of the Common Good for Too Long](https://substack.com/redirect/e3429c3f-dec9-49ec-9b11-484e836a2ec5?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), TIME.**

The arguments feels like wishful thinking, tied to Paul’s idea of “**taste**,” the discourse that likely compelled him to write this essay. Yes, brand exploits human psychology to sell things. But it also signals effort and empathy. It helps people feel seen. The cost is higher, but that’s capitalism 101. Silicon Valley could use brand better, more ads in California go viral for misunderstandings than for actually solving/communicating new problems.

**TL;DR**: Brand and marketing create sentimental and psychological value in consumers’ minds. But can we really overhaul this system? Do we really believe statements from Zuck and Altman about fully automating advertising are about solving new problems? Brand allows you to buy more time. If it weren’t for brand, most people would be using Android, not iPhones.

P.S. Omnicom and WPP probably agree with Paul, whether they are trying to solve new problems is another matter. Belief in brand is declining among holding companies (is it even a correct term to use?) that continue absorbing legacy ad agencies.

##### SEO, GEO, and Brand Search

As I mentioned above, AI chatbots still rely on either the top 10 Google search results or heavily cite Reddit and YouTube. Most GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) strategies are built on assumptions and the age-old marketing principle of flooding the market with brand messaging. There were SEO agencies in the late 2000s and early 2010s that pushed tactics which led Google to constantly change its algorithms to improve search quality and, of course, charge heavily for ads.

As [reported](https://substack.com/redirect/72ada4b4-fd66-4bea-b79d-56018e9fb68c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) in Peec AI’s blog, “Take Grokipedia, an AI-generated version of Wikipedia powered by Grok, as an example. It gained traction, then started losing visibility in Google in late January / early February 2025. Multiple SEO experts like Lily Ray and Glenn Gabe documented the decline. Additionally, [Malte Landwehr, CMO at Peec AI, noticed something crucial on LinkedIn](https://substack.com/redirect/dfb141cf-f3fc-4d43-94da-9688ef369898?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g): answer engines started recommending Grokipedia less often at exactly the same time when Grokipedia lost visibility in Google.”

The reasonable way to increase the likelihood of your brand being mentioned by AI chatbots is to focus on brand search and content distribution across different platforms, without giving up on quality or quantity. ##### AI Slop, Brands, and Me

[What is slop?](https://substack.com/redirect/388042d4-9da7-422b-bb0f-43aab1e14fc5?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) People say not everything AI is slop, some say everything is. The word slop itself is becoming a form of slop because people keep using it to describe everything they consider low effort, or sometimes simply wrong. It’s confusing and degrading. I don’t like fighting with people on the internet about negative things, but I did.

In late 2025, Migros, a Swiss supermarket, created [one of the biggest brand-first AI trends](https://substack.com/redirect/70587858-561b-4b03-814d-d13ad2ae054d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), which was positively received by many of the brand’s existing followers because the company had been consistently posting AI content for a while. But the human-generated music behind the trend and the AI brand character had [some originality](https://substack.com/redirect/5127ecc4-6f2c-4236-aac4-a04bc88a157d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) to them, so while people were calling it slop and the physics were all over the place in the videos, it didn’t create much mainstream backlash.

Fast forward to this year, we had the [nihilistic penguin trend](https://substack.com/redirect/697e44f6-6c20-4719-ac43-5c950972764a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), which was turned into an AI trend by brands in Germany and the UK creating AI versions of the penguin going to brand stores. There was backlash in the U.S. because of the White House’s use of an AI penguin, and most American companies said “nope” and exited the trend. European brands also [faced backlash](https://substack.com/redirect/53daa2d2-7fe3-4e37-b03f-1fe8f6d730f4?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), but as it usually goes, people don’t care much about trends. So when I made a post about this topic, the reaction was more meh than “let’s call this out.”

In both of these stories, there was no debate that what brands were doing could not be classified as slop. The videos were glitchy, and these were wider trends, nothing unique to a single brand. Then came the [Olympics](https://substack.com/redirect/6c3efb80-2654-4ee6-ad57-8a3ab87da6f9?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), and the [opening ceremony](https://substack.com/redirect/ec6ce3cd-2e69-4c08-9d65-b8c2d7b61889?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) featured AI, while the event’s Instagram regularly posted AI-generated miniature art. At first, people mostly reacted to the animations in the opening ceremony that contained AI content, but later artists and creators like me and Adam Cross noticed the AI Instagram posts, which were visibly violating [the IOC’s brand guidelines](https://substack.com/redirect/c481958e-de44-4c43-8de5-e118967d323b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g).

The earlier trends were like [AI content on Facebook](https://substack.com/redirect/21ab690a-5401-4941-a5c6-26b7fd1b4ef0?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). It could fool your grandma, but apparently not you. The Olympics posted images that fooled many young people into thinking they were real art. That was the reason when I used the term “AI slop,” some people disagreed. They said the posts clearly took effort, looked nice, and deserved more credit.

My general argument for calling it slop was simple. First, it used miniature-style work [resembling an artist](https://substack.com/redirect/7b40c7ed-a282-4789-aefa-de6706dc523e?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) who dominates the internet. His artwork is everywhere, and the likelihood of AI not being influenced by it is less than one percent. Second, the Olympics have long been a home for graphic design and art movements, and the IOC has a history of calling out artists and brands for not following brand guidelines. When they themselves neglect that history and context, the word slop feels deserved, and their AI Instagram posts [declined in quality](https://substack.com/redirect/85d8458c-604b-4d06-bdd2-1725f07eb643?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) by the end of the event. *Lastly, they did not disclose the use of AI, which again felt like a betrayal of trust.*

* Most of what brands produce is slop, just human-generated.
* AI-generated content that looks perfect can still be considered slop for reasons like brand heritage.
	+ As an example, take [Gucci](https://substack.com/redirect/99f1dcab-8ec2-423b-bb01-d67f30e8a762?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). Those AI images had visibly no errors, but people compare the actions of the brand today with the brand of the past, not the brand of tomorrow.
* People want to support real artists, and they don’t care about the technical quality of AI content.
	+ McDonald’s Netherlands and Coke’s AI ads reportedly used over 100,000 prompts combined, but people don’t care about that kind of “hard work.”

**TL;DR**: AI slop is subjective, and the backlash depends on the nature of the trend and your brand’s history.

##### Abundance vs. Ignorance

Do the problems we face in the real world exist because we lack ideas, or because we choose to ignore existing solutions and problems? I think we all know the answer to the question, “If we could put a man on the moon in 1969, why can’t we end poverty?” Economists have long debated this, saying there has been no universal answer except ignorance.

When it comes to AI, there is the same type of growing behavior, rooted in abundance and ignorance of reality. Humans are simple and complex at the same time, and it takes years for psychologists to make concrete observations. *But don’t worry, marketers have a quick solution.* They like to ask ChatGPT to put itself into the shoes of different humans, and there come the strategic insights to win the next brief, customer, or client.

The idea is to create an abundance of personas, storyboards, and synthetic customer feedback so strategists can find the next best insight. It’s similar to what Ezra Klein and Derek Thompson [proposed in their book](https://substack.com/redirect/c5792211-1af6-436a-a69f-d1d13aac999c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), create an abundance of ideas and everything else so that only a fool would neglect the best solution.

**Both of these parties ignore two basic truths.** One is that ideological beliefs that allow us to create different personas rarely align with how consumers’ buying patterns change over time. A systematic change is required to convert a new ideology into practice. AI personas can help you understand beliefs, but you need a human to understand how someone practices what they believe. 

For example, just because you have a Black friend and grew up together doesn’t mean you know the code. If you are not part of the main demographic in your country, you have likely watched a human-generated ad that uses a character who doesn’t know the code, and it becomes meaningless representation at best. I genuinely can’t wait for AI to make things worse in this context.

The second truth is that people already have their definition of truth set along a political spectrum. This idea that we can just introduce more ideas and fix everything is, again, bizarre. They don’t want abundance. They want you to pay attention to their existing problems and care about their truth, which they feel is being attacked. Ask yourself why right-wing political parties are winning in Europe, or why Mamdani won in NYC. The strategy isn’t to oversell new ideas, it’s to sell an agenda like [affordability](https://substack.com/redirect/497e822c-774c-4d71-93c1-f83590b02c7a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) that protects the truths people believe in while subtly pushing them toward a new system of thinking.

[![New policy agenda aims to cut costs for Americans](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!S55f!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F45f423e0-780b-45e3-a221-8f6a07b4a86f_1920x1080.jpeg "New policy agenda aims to cut costs for Americans")](https://substack.com/redirect/777cc7f1-8457-425c-b581-f322cafc3512?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)The same people who create these personas and run storyboards through AI tell me they believe human creativity is the one thing they don’t delegate to AI. My response is that creativity is all you need. People want you to repeat the words they are saying, just do it more creatively. Gen Z thinking everything is performative doesn’t mean you should stop doing what they call “performative.” Make it look natural. Everything is an act of performance, you are just putting on a bad one.

My core argument is to stop ignoring what people are asking you to focus on. The supposed truth you will find by running hundreds of AI scenarios is very unlikely to fix the problems your brand or society faces. And lastly, I have always slept better after talking to a person about the company I work for and gaining experiences that made me the person I am.

##### The TL;DR of AI Marketing…

1. AI shopping, with chatbots buying products for people, will be a thing in the near future, but retailers are still figuring out how many benefits they get out of it, and the number of consumers using AI-assisted shopping is way too inflated.
2. The job losses are coming. We’ve always known that whenever brands struggle, they find an excuse to cut marketing. AI productivity is that excuse.
3. You should look into stories of how people are falling in love with AI and how people are talking to their dead relatives, like what sense of meaning they feel from a voice they know isn’t actually a person. What’s the new value of invisible and non-verbal connection in a world where all aesthetics, from voice to face, can be generated?
4. Marketers know how to commodify everything. The rough and human-made aesthetic as a selling point will be all over the internet soon.
	1. BTS content of actual humans working on campaigns will be the new moat.
5. Indie agencies will have a renaissance, meanwhile holding companies will struggle to deliver an OS-level product that has onboarding and workflows worth clients’ time. They will need a lot of capital to sustain their brands in the long term.
6. You have plenty of reasons to hate AI, everyone does. But it’s corporate marketing, so you have to embrace some parts of the technology.
7. There is a bubble around AI as a technology and its future, which is creating a sense of doomerism from both sides, one that thinks humans will achieve a transhuman state and one that thinks AI will end everything. You should ignore both, as there are always people using technology to do work that matters in the present.
8. We already know the type of experience Meta provides in Ads Manager, so don’t expect anything revolutionary to happen with Manus AI. As far as I can tell, the AI insights are very similar to what Meta reps say, spend more. The best we can hope for is that ad targeting on the backend gets better and we get to see more behavioral insights based on things like heatmaps. Or maybe something insightful for advertisers to understand value outside last-click attribution.
9. There are AI ad agencies creating work that looks very real, and if you hire them, the advice is to disclose the training data and add an AI watermark.
	1. Also, you can always take the risk of hiring AI jail breakers if you are, like, (redacted).
10. Online search isn’t dead, it’s more impacted by video than AI. Chatbots can’t be manipulated into sharing your content. You need to do what you’re already doing plus brand-building practices.
11. AI is a black box. There are no real AI experts in marketing.
	1. Also, a ton of fan fiction around the technology goes viral every day. Don’t get too obsessed with those ideas. A marketer’s best skill is to be self-aware. Please don’t fall into AI psychosis.
12. The rebirth of the marketing funnel: The whole concept of the funnel was popularized by the idea of tracking online consumer behavior. Every year, the internet compounds and it becomes harder to track people in a meaningful way. 15. Brands already have a bad reputation for being out of touch, and because of nostalgia, people also feel ad campaigns are worse off than before. So, the AI slop allegations are everywhere all the time.
	1. Meanwhile, there is a new group of people constantly generating new AI ideas and brand collaborations that repurpose old meaning while steadily degrading the future of the brand.
16. AI-generated content officially has no copyright protection, so it will be interesting to see how brands defend themselves when their AI content or brand likeness in training data gets used by internet trolls.
17. Generative AI is one of many forms of AI that exist. Marketers need to create awareness around what types of AI have been part of the ecosystem for a long time and what’s new, and explain that they are not doing the harmful thing. Not all models are eating someone’s lunch.

##### AI Brainrot is real and the death…

If you found yourself in total disagreement with anything I said, please share your feedback and take this advice. Maybe not you, but a ton of people struggle to realise the arguments I am making are rooted in a desire to help brands do better marketing.

That’s always been the goal. **I am not pro- or anti-AI, all I care about is good marketing.** Use that mentality as your lens to rethink where the use of AI can be good vs. bad for marketing and brands. Also, I know everyone in the industry is using some form of AI to delegate their professional and non-professional work. So please don’t hide or turn it into a LinkedIn post. You should talk about that usage naturally or rant about the anxiety or joy you feel as a marketer. Thought posts about “Here’s an AI prompt to do XYZ” will be recalled just like we remember people making content about hashtags to use and engagement pods. It’s kind of lazy.

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>It’s real, but it also isn’t. <p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>On the other hand, as marketers, we have known for years that </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/df482817-219b-444f-8c4f-d6c1113bace3?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">almost 50%</a><span> of MarTech tools either go unused or hardly prove their ROI. <p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>The long story short is that, for the sake of consumer adoption, just like Uber and DoorDash, these companies are giving away value in 2026 that will cost much more in 2028. In some scenarios, the </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/2ab0df1c-6208-4ebd-85ab-174f231f1a67?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">costs of AI agents</a><span> are already matching the amount of money you would pay a junior software engineer. Right now, people are getting away with AI content strategies such as uploading copyrighted material or reverse prompt engineering to generate images and videos that do not look like regular slop. In the near future, AI will likely have restrictions to identify when someone is trying to get a chatbot to rewrite a poorly written prompt for a Wes Anderson-like video. Still, some people genuinely believe creators like </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/ef476d2a-5cc1-4be9-b3bb-5de9d1547331?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">ohneis</a><span> and </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/e59b701c-50dd-4f44-9ac1-101ecf5e219e?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">waviboy</a><span> have the talent to describe everything in a movie scene well enough to replicate it with a prompt. That is where the debate around “taste” comes from, people thinking you just need good references to create something good with AI.</span></p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Let’s put it simply. Surveillance culture will likely restrict this abuse of copyrighted content and AI models by brands. I can also see some brands hiring teens in India or Malaysia to create AI content while trying to escape the legal conundrum.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>TL;DR</strong><span>: Like every other technology, AI will get better, but consumer-facing products and the promises made by these companies will likely disappear soon. It will not be as easy as people think to create AI content that does not look like slop or to create something original enough to stand out. This makes the case for Artists vs. AI stronger, not weaker, unless a company simply does not care about marketing or brand safety.</span></p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">As I’ve said before, big agencies are all about promising security and management, not creativity. Last week, WPP went all in on this concept to overhaul the agency structure by billing for the process, not just the results they promise. Do not believe what they are telling you. This is a basic rebrand of WPP as the new McKinsey. For the next few years, they will go around patting themselves on the back and using their heritage brands to sell their research, workforce, and infrastructure. By 2030, I can promise you that most people will look at them the same way they see McKinsey.</p>

Now, the question for WPP, and sooner or later for Omnicom, will be whether they can deliver on the promise of research while keeping healthy relationships with the creative side of the business. McKinsey and consulting firms can afford to lose that connection because they sell research, not creativity, trust, or brand.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">There are two forces behind these industry moves, Cindy Rose and AI. Not as extreme as WPP, almost all holding companies are now launching so-called AI ecosystems, with data and customer simulation as their moat. </p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>As the </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/e6c506e0-84f2-44a2-9b19-d6710665d77b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">WSJ</a><span> reports, “In middle America, some brands and businesses are more afraid to trust their budgets with these larger ad firms where the use of technology is rampant and they don’t have that human connection,” said Dustin Neal, partner and principal creative at the firm, whose clients include coffee brand Sofa King and logistics company Takkion. “They want to really know the people who are running with their budget.” These holding companies may win big businesses, but small and mid-sized businesses are </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/e6c506e0-84f2-44a2-9b19-d6710665d77b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">becoming increasingly skeptical</a><span> about the creative moat, not the data.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>Back to </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/3d5def38-0c4c-4dc9-b898-4d12f1126424?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Cindy Rose</a><span>, someone who saw Microsoft transition from a service-based to a cloud-based company and likely values security and management more than marketing. In the last six to eight months, WPP announced data partnerships with companies ranging from </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/dfe47dfe-2b8d-4eac-a614-e60958b667ad?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Adobe</a><span> to </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/cdb6f3d7-2e70-4b0e-af8a-52e784cdbe11?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">YouTube</a><span>. The connections she brought to the company are helping WPP build an attractive pitch. Nonetheless, clients are still complaining about a lack of clarity. Reporting by Digiday </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/79abc3cb-4c8c-4a01-a67b-99d43986441f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">suggests</a><span> that Publicis, despite less integration so far, has clients who are more satisfied with its platform and workflow.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>More data and AI-assisted workflows will not solve these problems for WPP. Just look at Copilot. Microsoft, despite all its integrations, is still struggling to get people to use the platform. If Cindy relies only on lessons from </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/d7b9e177-b67c-484a-8969-216655a98f96?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Microsoft Azure</a><span>, the chances of a turnaround will be slim for the holding company. Unless they raise significant capital to build an advertising real estate similar to the cloud model, or acquire multiple media companies, the path forward will remain difficult.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>TL;DR</strong><span>: The chances of holding companies winning big with AI are quite slim, and they are competing with AI and MarTech companies that have mastered user workflows. On the other hand, the likelihood of WPP and Omnicom damaging their brand trust and creative industry credibility is quite high.</span></p>
<h3 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.375em;">The ethics of using AI in marketing are…</h3>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>People in this industry are very quick to tell you how much they adore artists who do not abide by the same guidelines and playbooks as we do. So how does one react when someone like </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/699eb1da-6ca1-43bf-96c0-a2f2a6f20068?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">David Lynch</a><span> says this?</span></p>
<blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid #e85c57;margin: 20px 0;padding: 0;"><p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);margin-left: 20px;line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">One day last year, she asked him for his thoughts on AI. Lynch picked up a pencil. “Natasha,” he said. “This is a pencil.” Everyone, he continued, has access to a pencil, and likewise, everyone with a phone will be using AI, if they aren’t already. “It’s how you use the pencil,” he told her. “You see?”</p></blockquote>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>When </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/8e5d7581-8f31-4d7a-80d6-0efb6e84bec0?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Matthew McConaughey</a><span>\* or </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/47bffbad-f1d3-43db-81fa-823425c2b357?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Ben Affleck</a><span> say the technology is already here and you would be a fool to ignore its existence, or when Darren Aronofsky releases </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/7f422fe6-d9a6-40c9-bc19-d149c47c36ee?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">an AI-generated movie</a><span>, it raises questions. With the general consensus that advertising is a less artistic medium than film, how does one cope with statements like these?</span></p>
<ol style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;">
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">You ignore them and focus on the 90% of anti-AI statements in Hollywood.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">You listen, or misinterpret them, and start experimenting with AI.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">You wait and see what happens.</p></li>
</ol>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>If you are part of the first group, congrats, you are in the </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/5b973cf7-4750-4183-b70e-713a9e3e95fe?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">majority</a><span>, though you may still be a minority depending on the reasoning behind your </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/bd9dad3f-4ef3-401a-a389-22b93538c71c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">anti-AI stance</a><span>. Some common reasons for rejecting AI or refusing to platform it include the fact that companies building these models </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/1058b4b5-5bb7-41bb-a863-73038a404cc7?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">have not fairly paid</a><span> the creatives and writers </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/161f7efc-1777-49ff-bff2-7ec5b75820ba?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">whose work</a><span> informs these new systems. Another concern is the speed of change. For example, it took </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/6f409a18-a8f0-48a6-a497-cf0ba7f2f14e?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">nearly a decade for Vogue</a><span> to platform photography in its magazine, as it continued using hand-drawn illustrations long after fashion photography became mainstream.</span></p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>If you already love AI, think about the ethics of using it in marketing through the lens of trust. For example, how can AI know better than audience testing groups when real humans have historically given brands misleading answers? Consider the story of the</span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/eb7035bb-bc45-4722-bc4e-ca59c33f4eb6?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;"> yellow sony walkman</a><span>, where participants liked it in focus groups but chose the default black version when purchasing. If you value customers, you do not remove them from the equation.</strong></p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>In the context of AI, </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/afa8f409-1174-4504-a669-b37965430f5f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">vagueposting</a><span> is used to overhype certain advancements and create fear about people losing their jobs to AI. It is basically bait to get people talking about AI, whether they hate it or like it. I believe vagueposting is also an indirect response to the “why is no one talking about this?” mentality of the internet, where some people phrase their takes vaguely to attract only the genuinely curious and the clueless, not audiences that derail discussions with whataboutism and negativity.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>TL;DR</strong><span>: If something is free of context and shows only one side of the story, it is ragebait wrapped as vaguebait, meant to create fear around whatever the latest AI development is.</span></p>
<h3 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.375em;">The Brand Age</h3>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>Paul Graham recently wrote </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/7f91d08b-8e03-45ed-94b5-9be19e7231e4?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">a great article about brands and products</a><span> that ends on an anti-brand note because, if you know his work, he’s always more concerned with companies trying to solve new problems. He dislikes the idea of companies sustaining themselves by selling the proposition that is brand while they figure out what problems to solve next.</span></p>
<blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid #e85c57;margin: 20px 0;padding: 0;"><p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);margin-left: 20px;line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">He writes, “The constraints imposed by brand ultimately depend on some of the worst features of human psychology. So when you have a world defined only by brand, it’s going to be a weird, bad world… In fact there’s a single principle that will both save you from working on things like brand, and also automatically find golden ages for you. Follow the problems.”</p></blockquote>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>Agreed. </strong><span>Brand, as we call it, does rely on some of the vulnerable yet empathetic features of human psychology. But who is Paul talking to? Apparently he sent a draft of this article to Sam Altman. If he is speaking to him in any way, I’m not sure this is the right message for OpenAI. They need brand to </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/a81fbd02-8420-4d0a-a9e1-5a44ad21e69a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">stay afloat</a><span>.</span></p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Neglecting brand feels illogical in a world of VCs and shareholder value. I almost reached for the Russell Westbrook meme reading the last part of his piece. We live in a system obsessed with value maximization, the idea that one should neglect brand to solve problems feels like a dream.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Even if we accept that premise, how does neglect address the lack of breathability in 21st-century markets compared to the 20th? I’m with Paul when he says Japanese products won and took over the world, forcing Swiss brands to lean harder into heritage and identity. But the call to solve new problems and win belongs more to that era. The 2020s are shaped by skepticism and individualism, which make people cling to existing beliefs. New ideas don’t spread as quickly or as apolitically when the sense of community is in decline.</p>
<blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid #e85c57;margin: 20px 0;padding: 0;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);margin-left: 20px;line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>In 1985, </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/dabd484b-d3ba-4ba8-b838-aab9469efefe?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Habits of the Heart</a><span>, an extensive study of values in American life, reported that Americans speak a “first language” of personal ambitions and only a “second language” of commitments to others and the collective. Perhaps more than any country in the West, we also became immersed in the 1970s in psychological talk and a self-help culture that, for all of its benefits, occupied many Americans with their inner lives and caused them to wade into themselves —not their communities—to find meaning and vitality. </span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);margin-left: 20px;line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">More recently, researchers and social observers have decried how social media betrayed its promise to connect us, instead deepening our sense of isolation and anomie. Making matters worse, we now consume different news sources that convey different facts, undermining the shared reality that is the glue of any healthy society. </p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);margin-left: 20px;line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>— </span><strong><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/e3429c3f-dec9-49ec-9b11-484e836a2ec5?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">We Have Put Individualism Ahead of the Common Good for Too Long</a><span>, TIME.</span></strong></p>
</blockquote>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>The arguments feels like wishful thinking, tied to Paul’s idea of “</span><strong>taste</strong><span>,” the discourse that likely compelled him to write this essay. Yes, brand exploits human psychology to sell things. But it also signals effort and empathy. It helps people feel seen. The cost is higher, but that’s capitalism 101. Silicon Valley could use brand better, more ads in California go viral for misunderstandings than for actually solving/communicating new problems.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>TL;DR</strong><span>: Brand and marketing create sentimental and psychological value in consumers’ minds. But can we really overhaul this system? Do we really believe statements from Zuck and Altman about fully automating advertising are about solving new problems? Brand allows you to buy more time. If it weren’t for brand, most people would be using Android, not iPhones.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">P.S. Omnicom and WPP probably agree with Paul, whether they are trying to solve new problems is another matter. Belief in brand is declining among holding companies (is it even a correct term to use?) that continue absorbing legacy ad agencies.</p>

<h3 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.375em;">SEO, GEO, and Brand Search</h3>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">As I mentioned above, AI chatbots still rely on either the top 10 Google search results or heavily cite Reddit and YouTube. Most GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) strategies are built on assumptions and the age-old marketing principle of flooding the market with brand messaging. There were SEO agencies in the late 2000s and early 2010s that pushed tactics which led Google to constantly change its algorithms to improve search quality and, of course, charge heavily for ads.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>As </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/72ada4b4-fd66-4bea-b79d-56018e9fb68c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">reported</a><span> in Peec AI’s blog, “Take Grokipedia, an AI-generated version of Wikipedia powered by Grok, as an example. It gained traction, then started losing visibility in Google in late January / early February 2025. Multiple SEO experts like Lily Ray and Glenn Gabe documented the decline. Additionally, </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/dfb141cf-f3fc-4d43-94da-9688ef369898?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Malte Landwehr, CMO at Peec AI, noticed something crucial on LinkedIn</a><span>: answer engines started recommending Grokipedia less often at exactly the same time when Grokipedia lost visibility in Google.”</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">The reasonable way to increase the likelihood of your brand being mentioned by AI chatbots is to focus on brand search and content distribution across different platforms, without giving up on quality or quantity. I mention quantity because some companies are now producing mini-blogs on different topics to increase their chances of being cited. As far as I recall, someone at Google advised against this very tactic last year.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>Well, that’s good news. Ahrefs now reports that </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/64dd5b0d-172b-4ee4-aa82-dd5cf6e21ee7?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">ranking in Google’s top 10</a><span> is a weaker indicator of being cited in AI Overviews than it was last year. Their report found that 38% of cited pages also appeared in the top 10 results for the same query. In Ahrefs’ July 2025 study, that number was reportedly 76%. Meanwhile, Ahrefs’ findings about YouTube being a top source in AI Overviews are similar to another study from </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/43bd0826-fd11-4b1a-a720-64f9688cb337?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">BrightEdge</a><span> last week, which revealed that YouTube averages a 20% citation share across AI platforms.</span></p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">One thing to remember about content marketing and SEO is that it is always better to focus on the content, not algorithmic changes. Maybe that is partly why execution-level content marketing roles have been slowly disappearing, and brands are focusing on hiring people who manage the whole ecosystem, not just content output.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>According to </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/b15ef690-459b-4d86-9303-1a9586af5c96?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Semrush</a><span>, job postings for “Content Marketing Manager” fell by 73% compared to 2023, and “Content Marketing Specialist” roles declined by 74%, even though “manager” remains the third most common title at 14% of listings. Meanwhile, senior ownership roles are surging. “Head of Content Marketing” postings grew by 376%, and “VP of Content” increased by 308%.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>If you work in content marketing, it is time to reposition search as a core brand-building activity. If you pitch AI search the same way as organic search, executives will complain about a bunch of content that remotely hits no KPIs. Ask them to focus on the long term and move the reporting schedule to a quarterly basis. Growing brand search takes longer than ranking a blog post. Even TikTok ads are </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/ace513ae-a6b5-4092-b94b-fd5971cffde3?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">late bloomers</a><span>, with measurable impact beginning to emerge in the second half of the campaign window.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">TL;DR: Stop trying to game AI chatbots into recommending your brand. The only viable solution is maintaining a strong brand presence while staying aware of the AI search landscape.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/388042d4-9da7-422b-bb0f-43aab1e14fc5?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">What is slop?</a><span> People say not everything AI is slop, some say everything is. The word slop itself is becoming a form of slop because people keep using it to describe everything they consider low effort, or sometimes simply wrong. It’s confusing and degrading. I don’t like fighting with people on the internet about negative things, but I did.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>In late 2025, Migros, a Swiss supermarket, created </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/70587858-561b-4b03-814d-d13ad2ae054d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">one of the biggest brand-first AI trends</a><span>, which was positively received by many of the brand’s existing followers because the company had been consistently posting AI content for a while. But the human-generated music behind the trend and the AI brand character had </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/5127ecc4-6f2c-4236-aac4-a04bc88a157d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">some originality</a><span> to them, so while people were calling it slop and the physics were all over the place in the videos, it didn’t create much mainstream backlash.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>Fast forward to this year, we had the </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/697e44f6-6c20-4719-ac43-5c950972764a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">nihilistic penguin trend</a><span>, which was turned into an AI trend by brands in Germany and the UK creating AI versions of the penguin going to brand stores. There was backlash in the U.S. because of the White House’s use of an AI penguin, and most American companies said “nope” and exited the trend. European brands also </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/53daa2d2-7fe3-4e37-b03f-1fe8f6d730f4?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">faced backlash</a><span>, but as it usually goes, people don’t care much about trends. So when I made a post about this topic, the reaction was more meh than “let’s call this out.”</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>In both of these stories, there was no debate that what brands were doing could not be classified as slop. The videos were glitchy, and these were wider trends, nothing unique to a single brand. Then came the </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/6c3efb80-2654-4ee6-ad57-8a3ab87da6f9?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Olympics</a><span>, and the </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/ec6ce3cd-2e69-4c08-9d65-b8c2d7b61889?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">opening ceremony</a><span> featured AI, while the event’s Instagram regularly posted AI-generated miniature art. At first, people mostly reacted to the animations in the opening ceremony that contained AI content, but later artists and creators like me and Adam Cross noticed the AI Instagram posts, which were visibly violating </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/c481958e-de44-4c43-8de5-e118967d323b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">the IOC’s brand guidelines</a><span>.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>The earlier trends were like </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/21ab690a-5401-4941-a5c6-26b7fd1b4ef0?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">AI content on Facebook</a><span>. It could fool your grandma, but apparently not you. The Olympics posted images that fooled many young people into thinking they were real art. That was the reason when I used the term “AI slop,” some people disagreed. They said the posts clearly took effort, looked nice, and deserved more credit.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>My general argument for calling it slop was simple. First, it used miniature-style work </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/7b40c7ed-a282-4789-aefa-de6706dc523e?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">resembling an artist</a><span> who dominates the internet. His artwork is everywhere, and the likelihood of AI not being influenced by it is less than one percent. Second, the Olympics have long been a home for graphic design and art movements, and the IOC has a history of calling out artists and brands for not following brand guidelines. When they themselves neglect that history and context, the word slop feels deserved, and their AI Instagram posts </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/85d8458c-604b-4d06-bdd2-1725f07eb643?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">declined in quality</a><span> by the end of the event. </span><em>Lastly, they did not disclose the use of AI, which again felt like a betrayal of trust.</em></p>
<ul style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;">
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Most of what brands produce is slop, just human-generated.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;">AI-generated content that looks perfect can still be considered slop for reasons like brand heritage.</p>
<ul style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;"><li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;"><span>As an example, take </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/99f1dcab-8ec2-423b-bb01-d67f30e8a762?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">Gucci</a><span>. Those AI images had visibly no errors, but people compare the actions of the brand today with the brand of the past, not the brand of tomorrow.</span></p></li></ul>
</li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;">People want to support real artists, and they don’t care about the technical quality of AI content.</p>
<ul style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;"><li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;mso-special-format: bullet;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">McDonald’s Netherlands and Coke’s AI ads reportedly used over 100,000 prompts combined, but people don’t care about that kind of “hard work.”</p></li></ul>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>TL;DR</strong><span>: AI slop is subjective, and the backlash depends on the nature of the trend and your brand’s history.</span></p>

<h3 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.375em;">Abundance vs. Ignorance</h3>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Do the problems we face in the real world exist because we lack ideas, or because we choose to ignore existing solutions and problems? I think we all know the answer to the question, “If we could put a man on the moon in 1969, why can’t we end poverty?” Economists have long debated this, saying there has been no universal answer except ignorance.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>When it comes to AI, there is the same type of growing behavior, rooted in abundance and ignorance of reality. Humans are simple and complex at the same time, and it takes years for psychologists to make concrete observations. </span><em>But don’t worry, marketers have a quick solution. </em><span>They like to ask ChatGPT to put itself into the shoes of different humans, and there come the strategic insights to win the next brief, customer, or client.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>The idea is to create an abundance of personas, storyboards, and synthetic customer feedback so strategists can find the next best insight. It’s similar to what Ezra Klein and Derek Thompson </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/c5792211-1af6-436a-a69f-d1d13aac999c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">proposed in their book</a><span>, create an abundance of ideas and everything else so that only a fool would neglect the best solution.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>Both of these parties ignore two basic truths.</strong><span> One is that ideological beliefs that allow us to create different personas rarely align with how consumers’ buying patterns change over time. A systematic change is required to convert a new ideology into practice. AI personas can help you understand beliefs, but you need a human to understand how someone practices what they believe. </span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">For example, just because you have a Black friend and grew up together doesn’t mean you know the code. If you are not part of the main demographic in your country, you have likely watched a human-generated ad that uses a character who doesn’t know the code, and it becomes meaningless representation at best. I genuinely can’t wait for AI to make things worse in this context.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>The second truth is that people already have their definition of truth set along a political spectrum. This idea that we can just introduce more ideas and fix everything is, again, bizarre. They don’t want abundance. They want you to pay attention to their existing problems and care about their truth, which they feel is being attacked. Ask yourself why right-wing political parties are winning in Europe, or why Mamdani won in NYC. The strategy isn’t to oversell new ideas, it’s to sell an agenda like </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/497e822c-774c-4d71-93c1-f83590b02c7a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">affordability</a><span> that protects the truths people believe in while subtly pushing them toward a new system of thinking.</span></p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">The same people who create these personas and run storyboards through AI tell me they believe human creativity is the one thing they don’t delegate to AI. My response is that creativity is all you need. People want you to repeat the words they are saying, just do it more creatively. Gen Z thinking everything is performative doesn’t mean you should stop doing what they call “performative.” Make it look natural. Everything is an act of performance, you are just putting on a bad one.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">My core argument is to stop ignoring what people are asking you to focus on. The supposed truth you will find by running hundreds of AI scenarios is very unlikely to fix the problems your brand or society faces. And lastly, I have always slept better after talking to a person about the company I work for and gaining experiences that made me the person I am.</p>

<h3 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.375em;">The TL;DR of AI Marketing…</h3>
<ol style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;">
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">AI shopping, with chatbots buying products for people, will be a thing in the near future, but retailers are still figuring out how many benefits they get out of it, and the number of consumers using AI-assisted shopping is way too inflated.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">The job losses are coming. We’ve always known that whenever brands struggle, they find an excuse to cut marketing. AI productivity is that excuse.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">You should look into stories of how people are falling in love with AI and how people are talking to their dead relatives, like what sense of meaning they feel from a voice they know isn’t actually a person. What’s the new value of invisible and non-verbal connection in a world where all aesthetics, from voice to face, can be generated? </p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;">Marketers know how to commodify everything. The rough and human-made aesthetic as a selling point will be all over the internet soon.</p>
<ol style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;list-style-type: lower-alpha;"><li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">BTS content of actual humans working on campaigns will be the new moat.</p></li></ol>
</li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Indie agencies will have a renaissance, meanwhile holding companies will struggle to deliver an OS-level product that has onboarding and workflows worth clients’ time. They will need a lot of capital to sustain their brands in the long term.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">You have plenty of reasons to hate AI, everyone does. But it’s corporate marketing, so you have to embrace some parts of the technology.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">There is a bubble around AI as a technology and its future, which is creating a sense of doomerism from both sides, one that thinks humans will achieve a transhuman state and one that thinks AI will end everything. You should ignore both, as there are always people using technology to do work that matters in the present.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">We already know the type of experience Meta provides in Ads Manager, so don’t expect anything revolutionary to happen with Manus AI. As far as I can tell, the AI insights are very similar to what Meta reps say, spend more. The best we can hope for is that ad targeting on the backend gets better and we get to see more behavioral insights based on things like heatmaps. Or maybe something insightful for advertisers to understand value outside last-click attribution.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;">There are AI ad agencies creating work that looks very real, and if you hire them, the advice is to disclose the training data and add an AI watermark.</p>
<ol style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;list-style-type: lower-alpha;"><li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Also, you can always take the risk of hiring AI jail breakers if you are, like, (redacted).</p></li></ol>
</li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Online search isn’t dead, it’s more impacted by video than AI. Chatbots can’t be manipulated into sharing your content. You need to do what you’re already doing plus brand-building practices.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;">AI is a black box. There are no real AI experts in marketing.</p>
<ol style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;list-style-type: lower-alpha;"><li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Also, a ton of fan fiction around the technology goes viral every day. Don’t get too obsessed with those ideas. A marketer’s best skill is to be self-aware. Please don’t fall into AI psychosis.</p></li></ol>
</li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">The rebirth of the marketing funnel: The whole concept of the funnel was popularized by the idea of tracking online consumer behavior. Every year, the internet compounds and it becomes harder to track people in a meaningful way. AI chatbots could very well change this, as people go through different stages of buyer intent within a couple of conversations.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">TRACK TRACK TRACK — That’s all I hear these days. Use AI to track this or that, when consumer research platforms themselves have been lacking in quality.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Reverse prompt engineering is the cheat code for most marketers to get better answers from AI chatbots. If they were actually good at understanding and communicating problems, they would be in other fields or creative roles.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;">Brands already have a bad reputation for being out of touch, and because of nostalgia, people also feel ad campaigns are worse off than before. So, the AI slop allegations are everywhere all the time. </p>
<ol style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;list-style-type: lower-alpha;"><li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Meanwhile, there is a new group of people constantly generating new AI ideas and brand collaborations that repurpose old meaning while steadily degrading the future of the brand.</p></li></ol>
</li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">AI-generated content officially has no copyright protection, so it will be interesting to see how brands defend themselves when their AI content or brand likeness in training data gets used by internet trolls.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">Generative AI is one of many forms of AI that exist. Marketers need to create awareness around what types of AI have been part of the ecosystem for a long time and what’s new, and explain that they are not doing the harmful thing. Not all models are eating someone’s lunch.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">To be continued…</p></li>
</ol>
<h3 style="position: relative;font-family: 'SF Pro Display',-apple-system-headline,system-ui,-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,'Segoe UI',Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif,'Apple Color Emoji','Segoe UI Emoji','Segoe UI Symbol';font-weight: bold;-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;-moz-osx-font-smoothing: antialiased;-webkit-appearance: optimizelegibility;-moz-appearance: optimizelegibility;appearance: optimizelegibility;margin: 1em 0 0.625em 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 1.16em;font-size: 1.375em;">AI Brainrot is real and the death… </h3>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">If you found yourself in total disagreement with anything I said, please share your feedback and take this advice. Maybe not you, but a ton of people struggle to realise the arguments I am making are rooted in a desire to help brands do better marketing.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>That’s always been the goal. </span><strong>I am not pro- or anti-AI, all I care about is good marketing.</strong><span> Use that mentality as your lens to rethink where the use of AI can be good vs. bad for marketing and brands. Also, I know everyone in the industry is using some form of AI to delegate their professional and non-professional work. So please don’t hide or turn it into a LinkedIn post. You should talk about that usage naturally or rant about the anxiety or joy you feel as a marketer. Thought posts about “Here’s an AI prompt to do XYZ” will be recalled just like we remember people making content about hashtags to use and engagement pods. It’s kind of lazy.</span></p>
<blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid #e85c57;margin: 20px 0;padding: 0;">
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);margin-left: 20px;line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><strong>Will AI kill marketing?</strong></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);margin-left: 20px;line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">No, but we are doing a really bad job of making a case for why marketing should exist in the near future. Right now, our responses to this question are like Peter Thiel’s response to “Should the human race survive?”</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);margin-left: 20px;line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">We are saying yes, marketing should survive. But other than the plea of creativity, we are running no major propaganda to defend the industry. There is no big PAC to lobby governments, no union to make the case for labor protection. Public criticism and rants alone won’t save us, this industry needs to organize.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);margin-left: 20px;line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>When advertising billboards became a thing, for years there was </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/326191d7-1f67-41ca-9df0-9b905c08594c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">a push to protect</a><span> that ad format. In the 1950s and 60s, agencies and advertising authorities </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/e0d2c792-f863-4fb3-91ca-500e7d8b8b48?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #e85c57;text-decoration: none;">ran campaigns</a><span> to change how people thought about marketing. Yes, everything is an ad network, but we have to democratise this stuff or you will have automated ads everywhere.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);margin-left: 20px;line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">At the same time, with mergers, we have killed the legacies and stories we could use to sell why this industry and its story matter.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);margin-left: 20px;line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Marketing and advertising as formats will live forever, but we surely are doing a bad job of protecting and communicating why our jobs matter to the public, government, and billionaires.</p>
</blockquote>

"</span><em>Coach, I'm me, why would I want to be anything else</em><span>?"</span></p>
