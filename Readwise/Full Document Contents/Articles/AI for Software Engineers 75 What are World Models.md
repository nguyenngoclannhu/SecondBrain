##### Highlights, current events, industry trends, and career resources for 12/23/2025

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!aeuC!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F082f01bf-a0cc-4628-926f-eacc6453611e_1280x720.png)](https://substack.com/redirect/6b79bf69-6200-43e1-9c5f-e3c836458cf5?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g)Yann LeCun has confirmed his startup, Advanced Machine Intelligence (AMI), will develop world models and is currently [seeking fundraising at a $5B valuation](https://substack.com/redirect/069bf2cc-faec-452c-9f11-d2a88f7fe5ce?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). While headlines focus on the $5B valuation, I care much more about the work.

Crazy valuations aren’t uncommon in the world of AI. The potential for this technology is astronomical even if the roadmap to get there is still being discovered. I view AI’s potential as shifting humanity’s problem-solving from O(n) (or greater!) complexity potentially to O(1). Once we can solve problems quickly, our rate of advancement will compound and discovery will take off. If this happens, AI will be worth far more than even crazy valuations place it at.

LeCun is now directly pursuing the same area of research and industry as [Fei-Fei Li’s World Labs](https://substack.com/redirect/143abe07-db9c-4e7c-b978-abb5c3f33201?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). He also joins other great minds in AI who have said AI needs a breakthrough beyond scaling the research we currently have. Many are placing a bet on this being world models.

Put succinctly, world models aim to understand the 3D world and learn more like a human child instead of like a machine. Instead of understanding a statistical correlation between inputs to generate a representative output, world models seek to understand causal physics and spatial reasoning. World Labs puts it well on [their website](https://substack.com/redirect/7da32973-47b4-4cea-9636-dabcb22acb1d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g):

> “We build foundational world models that can perceive, generate, reason, and interact with the 3D world — unlocking AI’s full potential through spatial intelligence by transforming seeing into doing, perceiving into reasoning, and imagining into creating.”

This means world models aren’t generative but instead make predictions based on abstract representations of the concepts they’ve learned. Instead of guessing at pixels, [they focus on higher-level concepts](https://substack.com/redirect/b2e7af11-3177-45b5-94a3-10289797c40f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g).

Here’s an example to illustrate what this means: If we’re considering a car moving down a street, a generative model wastes compute estimating pixel movement for every leaf on the road. A world model would instead ignore unimportant details and focus on the latent variables that impact its understanding such as the car’s velocity or the friction between the car and the road. Those details would be used to predict the world’s next state.

Practically, this means two things:

1. World models don’t waste resources on things that are unimportant for a task.
2. We can use spatial intelligence to train other real-world AI applications without needing to collect new data. Instead, world models can generate (or “dream”) their own. This is both very efficient and safer than the alternative (think about an application like self-driving where there is always inherent risk with data collection).

I predict we’ll be seeing a lot more about world models in 2026 and I’m curious to see how far we’ll get. Enjoy this week’s resources!

##### New & Trends

**Google released Gemini 3 Flash, outperforming its previous Pro models**

* Google released [Gemini 3 Flash](https://substack.com/redirect/1893f6ee-5304-4916-9a7c-0ae7a1bca5a0?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g), the new generation of its smaller model for faster, cheaper applications. It is multimodal, uses 30% fewer tokens than previous models, and is significantly cheaper ($0.50/1M input tokens). The advancements of smaller, cheaper models are much more important than the advancements of large models when it comes to applications and are key to democratizing the technology.

**AI coding assistants ship more, but also more bugs**

* CodeRabbit’s [recent report](https://substack.com/redirect/639c18c7-2f8f-4387-b6ba-092a288b812a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) shows that AI introduces 1.7x more issues than human-written code. Specifically, AI introduces 1.7x more issues, including 75% more logic errors and 3x the readability issues. AI can produce more code faster, but the code tends to be significantly worse. This volume of pull requests also causes reviewer fatigue making it more likely for errors to reach production.

**New York governor Kathy Hochul signed RAISE act**

* Despite an [executive order from President Trump](https://substack.com/redirect/5a2d3c5a-871a-468e-b67e-f5fa6465b6d0?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) to consolidate AI regulation to the federal level, New York governor Kathy Hochul [signed the RAISE Act](https://substack.com/redirect/1cff9f94-7099-45fa-a712-4b6fc64baad0?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) to establish safety standards for large AI companies in New York. This act requires companies with over $500 million in revenue to comply with specific safety standards.

**AI data centers have a carbon footprint that matches a small European country**

* A [new study](https://substack.com/redirect/2aad6dc6-a96e-4cd3-bbc3-d70e5362e548?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) shows that AI systems could produce roughly the same amount of carbon dioxide as New York City or Norway (about 80 million tons). While this is an estimate (as exact numbers are hard to come by), it shows the environmental impact AI could have and emphasizes that the real long-term problem AI needs to solve is power and energy.

**AI has entered the game industry**

* Roblox Studio is [integrating AI into their platform](https://substack.com/redirect/9f1a3853-828f-455a-b73f-0f47911bd8ce?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g). This enables users to generate assets with a prompt, use AI for real-time voice translation, and orchestrate work across other creative tools via MCP. The goal of this shift is to enable users to get to market faster and be more profitable.
* Contrarily, Larian Studios (the creators of Baldur’s Gate 3) CEO Swen Vincke [explained Larian only uses of AI in game development](https://substack.com/redirect/758e3614-baa3-4acb-95a0-602488ffb1f7?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) for content exploration similar to how they use Google images and art books. This was after they were accused of trying to use AI to replace artists which Vincke explained was false and they’re actually looking to hire more artists, not replace them.
* The video game industry has many potential applications for AI, but gamers aren’t excited about how they’ll impact the actual games produced. In 2026, I’m certain we will see it used more as it becomes more cost effective and I’m curious to see what the usage will be.

**Not everyone is convinced by AI coding**

* This is an [interesting article](https://substack.com/redirect/b6558053-08e4-4c8d-9620-eb0579d4863b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI_zv_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g) I actually helped contribute to that highlights the gap in expectation versus reality for AI in software engineering. The expectation is set very high that engineers should be able to greatly increase their productivity by using AI coding tools. In reality, it takes time for developers to figure out these tools and how to use them productively. In fact, the initial onboarding can even cause a development velocity hit that isn’t reflected in performance expectations.

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>Yann LeCun has confirmed his startup, Advanced Machine Intelligence (AMI), will develop world models and is currently </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/069bf2cc-faec-452c-9f11-d2a88f7fe5ce?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #eb5757;text-decoration: none;">seeking fundraising at a $5B valuation</a><span>. While headlines focus on the $5B valuation, I care much more about the work.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Crazy valuations aren’t uncommon in the world of AI. The potential for this technology is astronomical even if the roadmap to get there is still being discovered. I view AI’s potential as shifting humanity’s problem-solving from O(n) (or greater!) complexity potentially to O(1). Once we can solve problems quickly, our rate of advancement will compound and discovery will take off. If this happens, AI will be worth far more than even crazy valuations place it at.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>LeCun is now directly pursuing the same area of research and industry as </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/143abe07-db9c-4e7c-b978-abb5c3f33201?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #eb5757;text-decoration: none;">Fei-Fei Li’s World Labs</a><span>. He also joins other great minds in AI who have said AI needs a breakthrough beyond scaling the research we currently have. Many are placing a bet on this being world models.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>Put succinctly, world models aim to understand the 3D world and learn more like a human child instead of like a machine. Instead of understanding a statistical correlation between inputs to generate a representative output, world models seek to understand causal physics and spatial reasoning. World Labs puts it well on </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/7da32973-47b4-4cea-9636-dabcb22acb1d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #eb5757;text-decoration: none;">their website</a><span>:</span></p>
<blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid #eb5757;margin: 20px 0;padding: 0;"><p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);margin-left: 20px;line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">“We build foundational world models that can perceive, generate, reason, and interact with the 3D world — unlocking AI’s full potential through spatial intelligence by transforming seeing into doing, perceiving into reasoning, and imagining into creating.”</p></blockquote>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;"><span>This means world models aren’t generative but instead make predictions based on abstract representations of the concepts they’ve learned. Instead of guessing at pixels, </span><a href="https://substack.com/redirect/b2e7af11-3177-45b5-94a3-10289797c40f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnE2ZWdsIn0.JeUaI03hkTZe3g0Y-WbKcpI\_zv\_M4N2A50pyRXMoP1g" rel="" style="color: #eb5757;text-decoration: none;">they focus on higher-level concepts</a><span>.</span></p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Here’s an example to illustrate what this means: If we’re considering a car moving down a street, a generative model wastes compute estimating pixel movement for every leaf on the road. A world model would instead ignore unimportant details and focus on the latent variables that impact its understanding such as the car’s velocity or the friction between the car and the road. Those details would be used to predict the world’s next state.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">Practically, this means two things:</p>
<ol style="margin-top: 0;padding: 0;">
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">World models don’t waste resources on things that are unimportant for a task.</p></li>
<li style="margin: 8px 0 0 32px;"><p style="color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;margin-bottom: 0;box-sizing: border-box;padding-left: 4px;font-size: 16px;margin: 0;">We can use spatial intelligence to train other real-world AI applications without needing to collect new data. Instead, world models can generate (or “dream”) their own. This is both very efficient and safer than the alternative (think about an application like self-driving where there is always inherent risk with data collection).</p></li>
</ol>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px 0;color: rgb(54,55,55);line-height: 26px;font-size: 16px;">I predict we’ll be seeing a lot more about world models in 2026 and I’m curious to see how far we’ll get. Enjoy this week’s resources!</p>
