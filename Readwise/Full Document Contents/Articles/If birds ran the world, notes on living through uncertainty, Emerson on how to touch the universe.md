### [Emerson on How to Touch the Universe](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=85ac48a43e&e=f6b8863c15)

The astonishing thing is that even though we will never truly know what it is like to be another creature or another person or any configuration of chemistry and chance other than ourselves, we are made of the same matter as the granite that will mark our graves and share 98% of our DNA with the moss that will cover them. We share with them and with each other more than atoms — we share the wild luck of having drawn from the cosmic lottery this world of birdsong and waterfalls and [lichen](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=09f0de6058&e=f6b8863c15) and [spring](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=f906382016&e=f6b8863c15), none of which had to exist, all of which could have been and can always be [otherwise](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=895fc52a0d&e=f6b8863c15). 

To know this, to place the firm hand of the mind on this banister of reality, is to steady yourself amid the daily shocks of living. To feel it is something else entirely — it is to press this perishable hand against the beating heart of the universe that made it and tremble with its pulse in your veins.

![](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/perihelion_TheMarginalian.jpg?resize=680%2C907)

[Perihelion](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=aed5c9899e&e=f6b8863c15) over Patagonia, January 12, 2026.

That is what [Ralph Waldo Emerson](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=a96ab352fd&e=f6b8863c15) (May 25, 1803–April 27, 1882) offers in an exquisite passage from his [journals](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=0f0abc43b3&e=f6b8863c15), penned after visiting Paris’s famous botanical garden just as its new mineralogy gallery was being built to house six hundred thousand stones, gems, and fossils. 

A century after William Blake saw the universe in a grain of sand, before William Henry Hudson saw “the wonderfulness and eternal mystery of life itself” [in a nautilus](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=98c6c5b0fd&e=f6b8863c15), before Charles Darwin invited us to see nature as a living library of “endless forms most beautiful and most wonderful,” the thirty-year-old Emerson writes:

> ![](https://gallery.mailchimp.com/13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1/images/2e292385-dc1c-4cfe-b95e-845f6f98c2ec.png)The universe is a more amazing puzzle than ever, as you glance along this bewildering series of animated forms — the hazy butterflies, the carved shells, the birds, beasts, fishes, insects, snakes, the upheaving principle of life everywhere incipient, in the very rock aping organized forms. Not a form so grotesque, so savage, nor so beautiful but is an expression of some property inherent in man the observer, an occult relation between the very scorpions and man. I feel the centipede in me, cayman, carp, eagle and fox. I am moved by strange sympathies.

[![](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/PillarsOfCreation_infrared.jpg?resize=680%2C638)](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=31bb6d3203&e=f6b8863c15)

Pillars of Creation, Eagle Nebula, Messier 16. Infrared photograph. NASA / Hubble Space Telescope. (Available as [a print](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=d4d0cd2960&e=f6b8863c15) and as [stationery cards](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=693b14ee99&e=f6b8863c15).)

To feel this universal kinship bestows upon us a kind of moral obligation to live our own lives as fully and rightly as possible — something Emerson would come to articulate nearly a decade later in his essay “Compensation”:

> ![](https://gallery.mailchimp.com/13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1/images/2e292385-dc1c-4cfe-b95e-845f6f98c2ec.png)The universe is represented in everyone of its particles. Everything in nature contains all the powers of nature. Everything is made of one hidden stuff… Each new form repeats not only the main character of the type, but part for part all the details, all the aims, furtherances, hindrances, energies, and whole system of every other. Every occupation, trade, art, transaction, is a compend of the world and a correlative of every other. Each one is an entire emblem of human life; of its good and ill, its trials, its enemies, its course and its end. And each one must somehow accommodate the whole [man](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=6bf62b6c12&e=f6b8863c15), and recite all his destiny. 

> The world globes itself in a drop of dew. The microscope cannot find the animalcule which is less perfect for being little. Eyes, ears, taste, smell, motion, resistance, appetite, and organs of reproduction that take hold on eternity, — all find room to consist in the small creature. So do we put our life into every act… The value of the universe contrives to throw itself into every point… Thus is the universe alive.

Couple with quantum pioneer Erwin Schrödinger on [how to know the universe in you](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=46a47bbcfa&e=f6b8863c15), then revisit Emerson on [transcendence](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=cc92222db0&e=f6b8863c15), [authenticity](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=048113266a&e=f6b8863c15), [how to trust yourself](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=af9579c304&e=f6b8863c15).

### [How to Hold the Darkness: Notes on Living Through Uncertainty](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=62d9affeed&e=f6b8863c15)

[![](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/ordinary_weller.jpg?fit=320%2C494)](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=d5b0581a44&e=f6b8863c15)

There are times in life when the continent of certainty parts underfoot and, as the ash cloud of the old world rains darkness upon us, we are asked to swim in the rivers of lava that will make the new. “Almost it would appear that it is useless in such confusion to ask,” wrote Virginia Woolf of [such times](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=dfabb788eb&e=f6b8863c15), “those questions as to what, and why, and wherefore.” Unlike her staunchly secularized contemporaries, who shuddered to speak of the soul for fear of being seen as anti-intellectual, Woolf devoted her life to communicating from and with “all these wayward parts that constitute the human soul,” which she knew [lives on a level deeper than the self to make us who we are](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=4c10da1098&e=f6b8863c15). It is what is left to us and of us in those volcanic times of darkness and uncertainty. It is what rebuilds the world, within and without, and what always has. It *is* the world. We still use Kepler’s laws of planetary motion to land rovers on Mars, but we are yet to catch up to [his model of the world as an ensouled body](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=a0009f841d&e=f6b8863c15) — a notion dating back to Plato, whose political precepts we still use and whose concept of *anima mundi*, or “world soul,” we are yet to heed. 

[![](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Hildegard_TheMarginalian2.jpg?resize=680%2C849)](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=28d84e8b61&e=f6b8863c15)

One of Hildegard of Bingen’s [enchanted ecologies](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=a760c8113f&e=f6b8863c15)

Epochs after Plato and Kepler and Woolf, trauma therapist Francis Weller offers a field guide to fortifying the soul in his essay collection [***In the Absence of the Ordinary: Soul Work for Times of Uncertainty***](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=bf672ac131&e=f6b8863c15) ([*public library*](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=4f3e3fba46&e=f6b8863c15)). Two centuries after Alexander von Humboldt [invented modern nature](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=57a35fac1f&e=f6b8863c15) with his recognition that “in this great chain of causes and effects, no single fact can be considered in isolation,” Weller insists that a correct view of human nature must be rooted in a recognition of “our ongoing relationship with the *anima mundi*,” of “how fully our lives are entangled with one another, with the stand of oaks, the night herons, the marginalized, the brokenhearted.” 

Observing that “soul navigates the twining trail between sovereignty and intimacy,” he writes:

> ![](https://gallery.mailchimp.com/13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1/images/2e292385-dc1c-4cfe-b95e-845f6f98c2ec.png)We have clearly entered the Long Dark… It is the realm of soul — of whispers and dreams, mystery and imagination, death and ancestors. It is an essential territory, both inevitable and required, offering a form of soul gestation that may gradually give shape to our deeper lives, personally and communally. Certain things can happen only in this grotto of darkness. Think of the wild network of roots and microbes, mycelia, and minerals, making possible all that we see in the day world, or the extensive networks within our own bodies, bringing blood, nutrients, oxygen, and thought to our corporeal lives. All of it happening in the darkness. We must become fluent in the manners and ways of soul.

> […]

> We are tumbling through a rough initiation. Radical alterations are occurring in our inner and outer landscapes. It is simultaneously deeply personal and wildly collective, binding us to one another.

[![](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Grackle_roots.jpg?w=680)](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=7fc70860da&e=f6b8863c15)

Art from *[An Almanac of Birds: 100 Divinations for Uncertain Days](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=0c10065995&e=f6b8863c15)*. (Available as [a print](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=2065534bb5&e=f6b8863c15) and [stationery cards](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=7be28108d3&e=f6b8863c15))

A century after Bertrand Russell called for “a largeness of contemplation” in his wonderful [calibration of perspective](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=9538d2f7e4&e=f6b8863c15) amid the darkness of the world’s first global war, Weller writes:

> ![](https://gallery.mailchimp.com/13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1/images/2e292385-dc1c-4cfe-b95e-845f6f98c2ec.png)It is a time to become immense. 

> To become immense means to recall how embedded we are in an animate world — a world that dreams and enchants, a world that excites our imaginations and conjures our affections through its stunning beauty. Everything we need is here. We only need to remember the wider embrace of our belonging to woodlands and prairies, marshlands, and neighborhoods, to the old stories and the tender gestures of a friend. To become immense also includes the radical act of welcoming all of who we are into the story. Nothing excluded. We become large through accepting all aspects of our being — weakness and need, loneliness and sorrow, shame and fear — everything seen as essential to our wholeness, our immensity.

This immensity, Weller insists, is singularly called forth by precisely those periods of darkness and uncertainty we feel too small to fathom, to fight, to break through — the times when the order of the world as we know it has turned to chaos, out of which a new world can’t but be born. He writes:

> ![](https://gallery.mailchimp.com/13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1/images/2e292385-dc1c-4cfe-b95e-845f6f98c2ec.png)When the ordinary fades, when the familiar rhythms and patterns of shared living erode, something is activated within the soul. Hidden invitations and initiations arise in a time of uncertainty. The soul recognizes the markers of descent — darkness, sorrow, anxiety — as requiring radical change. The conditions of trouble and uncertainty activate some profound movement toward alterations in the psychic landscape. These are the precise times when the possibility for shifts in the collective field occurs.

![](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/CirrocumulusSea_MariaPopova.jpg?resize=680%2C667)

Couple [***In the Absence of the Ordinary***](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=80d075b0d9&e=f6b8863c15), in the remainder of which Weller goes on to offer “ways to foster an intimacy with the world of soul and the soul of the world.” with this [lighthouse for dark times](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=86d6e5276d&e=f6b8863c15), then revisit Tibetan Buddhist teacher Pema Chödrön on [transformation through difficult times](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=5f885158b6&e=f6b8863c15) and Swiss poet, philosopher, and linguist Jean Gebser’s vision for [the evolution of our civilizational consciousness](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=f25f64b790&e=f6b8863c15).

[![](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/derekwalcott_collectedpoems.jpg?w=680)](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=da3f07b22a&e=f6b8863c15)Soaring hollow-boned and prehistoric over our infant species, birds live their lives indifferent to ours. They are not giving us signs, but we [make of them omens](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=7dd7481606&e=f6b8863c15) and [draw from them divinations](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=be53092860&e=f6b8863c15). They furnish [our best metaphors](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=ba158f3703&e=f6b8863c15) and [the neural infrastructure of our dreams](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=e5d1818329&e=f6b8863c15). They challenge our assumptions about [the deepest measure of intelligence](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=2ac8d1224a&e=f6b8863c15). 

Because birds so beguile us, they magnetize our attention, and anything we polish with attention becomes a mirror. In every reflection, a reckoning; in every reckoning, a possibility — a glimpse of us better than ourselves. 

That is what Nobel laureate **Derek Walcott** (January 23, 1930–March 17, 2017) conjures up in his shamanic poem “The Season of Phantasmal Peace” — an eternal vision for reprieve from the worst in us, written in the final years of the Cold War, the war that could have ended the world but was abated, not because we are perfect but because we are perfectible, because [peace is possible](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=237af7446a&e=f6b8863c15), because, as Maya Angelou wrote in another eternal mirror of a poem, [we are the possible](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=91a6ba7928&e=f6b8863c15).

> ![](https://gallery.mailchimp.com/13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1/images/2e292385-dc1c-4cfe-b95e-845f6f98c2ec.png)**THE SEASON OF PHANTASMAL PEACE**  
> *by Derek Walcott*

> Then all the nations of birds lifted together  
> the huge net of the shadows of this earth  
> in multitudinous dialects, twittering tongues,  
> stitching and crossing it. They lifted up  
> the shadows of long pines down trackless slopes,  
> the shadows of glass-faced towers down evening streets,  
> the shadow of a frail plant on a city sill —  
> the net rising soundless as night, the birds’ cries soundless, until  
> there was no longer dusk, or season, decline, or weather,  
> only this passage of phantasmal light  
> that not the narrowest shadow dared to sever.

> And men could not see, looking up, what the wild geese drew,  
> what the ospreys trailed behind them in silvery ropes  
> that flashed in the icy sunlight; they could not hear  
> battalions of starlings waging peaceful cries,  
> bearing the net higher, covering this world  
> like the vines of an orchard, or a mother drawing  
> the trembling gauze over the trembling eyes  
> of a child fluttering to sleep;  
>  it was the light  
> that you will see at evening on the side of a hill  
> in yellow October, and no one hearing knew  
> what change had brought into the raven’s cawing,  
> the killdeer’s screech, the ember-circling chough  
> such an immense, soundless, and high concern  
> for the fields and cities where the birds belong,  
> except it was their seasonal passing, Love,  
> made seasonless, or, from the high privilege of their birth,  
> something brighter than pity for the wingless ones  
> below them who shared dark holes in windows and in houses,  
> and higher they lifted the net with soundless voices  
> above all change, betrayals of falling suns,  
> and this season lasted one moment, like the pause  
> between dusk and darkness, between fury and peace,  
> but, for such as our earth is now, it lasted long.

“The Season of Phantasmal Peace” appears in Walcott’s indispensable [***Collected Poems: 1948–1984***](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=1e670b3bd2&e=f6b8863c15) ([*public library*](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=be71ba2deb&e=f6b8863c15)), which also gave us his [“Love After Love”](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=f5b26955db&e=f6b8863c15) — one of the greatest poems ever written. 

For a kindred vision of a more harmonious world, lensed through the possible in us, savor Marie Howe’s poem [“Hymn.”](https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&id=d9e5a147d5&e=f6b8863c15)

<div style="margin:0 40px">
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#262626; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; line-height:165%; text-align:left">The astonishing thing is that even though we will never truly know what it is like to be another creature or another person or any configuration of chemistry and chance other than ourselves, we are made of the same matter as the granite that will mark our graves and share 98% of our DNA with the moss that will cover them. We share with them and with each other more than atoms — we share the wild luck of having drawn from the cosmic lottery this world of birdsong and waterfalls and <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=09f0de6058&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">lichen</a> and <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=f906382016&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">spring</a>, none of which had to exist, all of which could have been and can always be <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=895fc52a0d&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">otherwise</a>. </p>
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#262626; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; line-height:165%; text-align:left">To know this, to place the firm hand of the mind on this banister of reality, is to steady yourself amid the daily shocks of living. To feel it is something else entirely — it is to press this perishable hand against the beating heart of the universe that made it and tremble with its pulse in your veins.</p>
<img height="auto" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/perihelion\_TheMarginalian.jpg?resize=680%2C907" style="-ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; border:0; height:auto; line-height:100%; outline:none; text-decoration:none; display:inline; width:100%" width="100%"/><p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#8C8C8C; font-family:Arial, Sans-serif; font-size:0.8em; line-height:165%; text-align:left; border-bottom:1px solid #bfbfbf; padding:0 0 4px; -webkit-margin-before:0.35em"><a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=aed5c9899e&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">Perihelion</a> over Patagonia, January 12, 2026.</p>
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#262626; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; line-height:165%; text-align:left">That is what <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=a96ab352fd&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">Ralph Waldo Emerson</a> (May 25, 1803–April 27, 1882) offers in an exquisite passage from his <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=0f0abc43b3&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">journals</a>, penned after visiting Paris’s famous botanical garden just as its new mineralogy gallery was being built to house six hundred thousand stones, gems, and fossils. </p>
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#262626; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; line-height:165%; text-align:left">A century after William Blake saw the universe in a grain of sand, before William Henry Hudson saw “the wonderfulness and eternal mystery of life itself” <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=98c6c5b0fd&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">in a nautilus</a>, before Charles Darwin invited us to see nature as a living library of “endless forms most beautiful and most wonderful,” the thirty-year-old Emerson writes:</p>
<blockquote style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; display:block; -webkit-margin-before:0; -webkit-margin-after:0; -webkit-margin-start:40px; -webkit-margin-end:0"><p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#595959; font-family:Arial, Sans-serif; font-size:15px; line-height:170%; text-align:left"><img align="left" height="auto" src="https://gallery.mailchimp.com/13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1/images/2e292385-dc1c-4cfe-b95e-845f6f98c2ec.png" style="-ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; border:0; height:auto; line-height:100%; outline:none; text-decoration:none; padding:5px 30px 10px 0; display:inline; width:50px !important" width="50px !important"/>The universe is a more amazing puzzle than ever, as you glance along this bewildering series of animated forms — the hazy butterflies, the carved shells, the birds, beasts, fishes, insects, snakes, the upheaving principle of life everywhere incipient, in the very rock aping organized forms. Not a form so grotesque, so savage, nor so beautiful but is an expression of some property inherent in man the observer, an occult relation between the very scorpions and man. I feel the centipede in me, cayman, carp, eagle and fox. I am moved by strange sympathies.</p></blockquote>

<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#262626; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; line-height:165%; text-align:left">To feel this universal kinship bestows upon us a kind of moral obligation to live our own lives as fully and rightly as possible — something Emerson would come to articulate nearly a decade later in his essay “Compensation”:</p>
<blockquote style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; display:block; -webkit-margin-before:0; -webkit-margin-after:0; -webkit-margin-start:40px; -webkit-margin-end:0">
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#595959; font-family:Arial, Sans-serif; font-size:15px; line-height:170%; text-align:left"><img align="left" height="auto" src="https://gallery.mailchimp.com/13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1/images/2e292385-dc1c-4cfe-b95e-845f6f98c2ec.png" style="-ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; border:0; height:auto; line-height:100%; outline:none; text-decoration:none; padding:5px 30px 10px 0; display:inline; width:50px !important" width="50px !important"/>The universe is represented in everyone of its particles. Eyes, ears, taste, smell, motion, resistance, appetite, and organs of reproduction that take hold on eternity, — all find room to consist in the small creature. So do we put our life into every act… The value of the universe contrives to throw itself into every point… Thus is the universe alive.</p>
</blockquote>

<div style="margin:0 40px">
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#262626; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; line-height:165%; text-align:left"><a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=d5b0581a44&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline"><img align="right" height="auto" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/ordinary\_weller.jpg?fit=320%2C494" style="-ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; border:0; height:auto; line-height:100%; outline:none; text-decoration:none; display:inline; width:150px; margin:5px 0 10px 30px" width="150"/></a></p>
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#262626; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; line-height:165%; text-align:left">There are times in life when the continent of certainty parts underfoot and, as the ash cloud of the old world rains darkness upon us, we are asked to swim in the rivers of lava that will make the new. “Almost it would appear that it is useless in such confusion to ask,” wrote Virginia Woolf of <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=dfabb788eb&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">such times</a>, “those questions as to what, and why, and wherefore.” Unlike her staunchly secularized contemporaries, who shuddered to speak of the soul for fear of being seen as anti-intellectual, Woolf devoted her life to communicating from and with “all these wayward parts that constitute the human soul,” which she knew <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=4c10da1098&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">lives on a level deeper than the self to make us who we are</a>. It is what is left to us and of us in those volcanic times of darkness and uncertainty. It is what rebuilds the world, within and without, and what always has. It <em>is</em> the world. We still use Kepler’s laws of planetary motion to land rovers on Mars, but we are yet to catch up to <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=a0009f841d&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">his model of the world as an ensouled body</a> — a notion dating back to Plato, whose political precepts we still use and whose concept of <em>anima mundi</em>, or “world soul,” we are yet to heed. </p>
<a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=28d84e8b61&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline"><img height="auto" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Hildegard\_TheMarginalian2.jpg?resize=680%2C849" style="-ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; border:0; height:auto; line-height:100%; outline:none; text-decoration:none; display:inline; width:100%" width="100%"/></a><p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#8C8C8C; font-family:Arial, Sans-serif; font-size:0.8em; line-height:165%; text-align:left; border-bottom:1px solid #bfbfbf; padding:0 0 4px; -webkit-margin-before:0.35em">One of Hildegard of Bingen’s <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=a760c8113f&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">enchanted ecologies</a></p>
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#262626; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; line-height:165%; text-align:left">Epochs after Plato and Kepler and Woolf, trauma therapist Francis Weller offers a field guide to fortifying the soul in his essay collection <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=bf672ac131&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline"><strong><em>In the Absence of the Ordinary: Soul Work for Times of Uncertainty</em></strong></a> (<a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=4f3e3fba46&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline"><em>public library</em></a>). Two centuries after Alexander von Humboldt <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=57a35fac1f&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">invented modern nature</a> with his recognition that “in this great chain of causes and effects, no single fact can be considered in isolation,” Weller insists that a correct view of human nature must be rooted in a recognition of “our ongoing relationship with the <em>anima mundi</em>,” of “how fully our lives are entangled with one another, with the stand of oaks, the night herons, the marginalized, the brokenhearted.” </p>
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#262626; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; line-height:165%; text-align:left">Observing that “soul navigates the twining trail between sovereignty and intimacy,” he writes:</p>
<blockquote style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; display:block; -webkit-margin-before:0; -webkit-margin-after:0; -webkit-margin-start:40px; -webkit-margin-end:0">
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#595959; font-family:Arial, Sans-serif; font-size:15px; line-height:170%; text-align:left"><img align="left" height="auto" src="https://gallery.mailchimp.com/13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1/images/2e292385-dc1c-4cfe-b95e-845f6f98c2ec.png" style="-ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; border:0; height:auto; line-height:100%; outline:none; text-decoration:none; padding:5px 30px 10px 0; display:inline; width:50px !important" width="50px !important"/>We have clearly entered the Long Dark… It is the realm of soul — of whispers and dreams, mystery and imagination, death and ancestors. It is an essential territory, both inevitable and required, offering a form of soul gestation that may gradually give shape to our deeper lives, personally and communally. Certain things can happen only in this grotto of darkness. Think of the wild network of roots and microbes, mycelia, and minerals, making possible all that we see in the day world, or the extensive networks within our own bodies, bringing blood, nutrients, oxygen, and thought to our corporeal lives. All of it happening in the darkness. We must become fluent in the manners and ways of soul.</p>
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#595959; font-family:Arial, Sans-serif; font-size:15px; line-height:170%; text-align:left">[…]</p>

Radical alterations are occurring in our inner and outer landscapes. It is simultaneously deeply personal and wildly collective, binding us to one another.</p>

<blockquote style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; display:block; -webkit-margin-before:0; -webkit-margin-after:0; -webkit-margin-start:40px; -webkit-margin-end:0">

<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#595959; font-family:Arial, Sans-serif; font-size:15px; line-height:170%; text-align:left">To become immense means to recall how embedded we are in an animate world — a world that dreams and enchants, a world that excites our imaginations and conjures our affections through its stunning beauty. Everything we need is here. We only need to remember the wider embrace of our belonging to woodlands and prairies, marshlands, and neighborhoods, to the old stories and the tender gestures of a friend. To become immense also includes the radical act of welcoming all of who we are into the story. Nothing excluded. We become large through accepting all aspects of our being — weakness and need, loneliness and sorrow, shame and fear — everything seen as essential to our wholeness, our immensity.</p>
</blockquote>

He writes:</p>
<blockquote style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; display:block; -webkit-margin-before:0; -webkit-margin-after:0; -webkit-margin-start:40px; -webkit-margin-end:0"><p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#595959; font-family:Arial, Sans-serif; font-size:15px; line-height:170%; text-align:left"><img align="left" height="auto" src="https://gallery.mailchimp.com/13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1/images/2e292385-dc1c-4cfe-b95e-845f6f98c2ec.png" style="-ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; border:0; height:auto; line-height:100%; outline:none; text-decoration:none; padding:5px 30px 10px 0; display:inline; width:50px !important" width="50px !important"/>When the ordinary fades, when the familiar rhythms and patterns of shared living erode, something is activated within the soul. Hidden invitations and initiations arise in a time of uncertainty. The soul recognizes the markers of descent — darkness, sorrow, anxiety — as requiring radical change. The conditions of trouble and uncertainty activate some profound movement toward alterations in the psychic landscape. These are the precise times when the possibility for shifts in the collective field occurs.</p></blockquote>

<div style="margin:0 40px">
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#262626; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; line-height:165%; text-align:left"><a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=da3f07b22a&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline"><img align="right" height="auto" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/derekwalcott\_collectedpoems.jpg?w=680" style="-ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; border:0; height:auto; line-height:100%; outline:none; text-decoration:none; display:inline; width:150px; margin:5px 0 10px 30px" width="150"/></a>Soaring hollow-boned and prehistoric over our infant species, birds live their lives indifferent to ours. They are not giving us signs, but we <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=7dd7481606&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">make of them omens</a> and <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=be53092860&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">draw from them divinations</a>. They furnish <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=ba158f3703&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">our best metaphors</a> and <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=e5d1818329&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">the neural infrastructure of our dreams</a>. They challenge our assumptions about <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=2ac8d1224a&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">the deepest measure of intelligence</a>. </p>
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#262626; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; line-height:165%; text-align:left">Because birds so beguile us, they magnetize our attention, and anything we polish with attention becomes a mirror. In every reflection, a reckoning; in every reckoning, a possibility — a glimpse of us better than ourselves. </p>
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#262626; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; line-height:165%; text-align:left">That is what Nobel laureate <strong>Derek Walcott</strong> (January 23, 1930–March 17, 2017) conjures up in his shamanic poem “The Season of Phantasmal Peace” — an eternal vision for reprieve from the worst in us, written in the final years of the Cold War, the war that could have ended the world but was abated, not because we are perfect but because we are perfectible, because <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=237af7446a&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">peace is possible</a>, because, as Maya Angelou wrote in another eternal mirror of a poem, <a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=91a6ba7928&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline">we are the possible</a>.</p>
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#262626; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; line-height:165%; text-align:left"><a href="https://themarginalian.us2.list-manage.com/track/click?u=13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1&amp;id=07045f818c&amp;e=f6b8863c15" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#C33737; text-decoration:underline"><img height="auto" src="https://mcusercontent.com/13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1/images/b0746a25-442b-571b-fdfb-3f5f4dcdcad5.png" style="-ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; border:0; height:auto; line-height:100%; outline:none; text-decoration:none; display:inline; width:100%" width="100%"/></a></p>
<blockquote style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; display:block; -webkit-margin-before:0; -webkit-margin-after:0; -webkit-margin-start:40px; -webkit-margin-end:0">
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#595959; font-family:Arial, Sans-serif; font-size:15px; line-height:170%; text-align:left"><img align="left" height="auto" src="https://gallery.mailchimp.com/13eb080d8a315477042e0d5b1/images/2e292385-dc1c-4cfe-b95e-845f6f98c2ec.png" style="-ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; border:0; height:auto; line-height:100%; outline:none; text-decoration:none; padding:5px 30px 10px 0; display:inline; width:50px !important" width="50px !important"/><strong>THE SEASON OF PHANTASMAL PEACE</strong><br/><em>by Derek Walcott</em></p>
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#595959; font-family:Arial, Sans-serif; font-size:15px; line-height:170%; text-align:left">Then all the nations of birds lifted together<br/>the huge net of the shadows of this earth<br/>in multitudinous dialects, twittering tongues,<br/>stitching and crossing it. They lifted up<br/>the shadows of long pines down trackless slopes,<br/>the shadows of glass-faced towers down evening streets,<br/>the shadow of a frail plant on a city sill —<br/>the net rising soundless as night, the birds’ cries soundless, until<br/>there was no longer dusk, or season, decline, or weather,<br/>only this passage of phantasmal light<br/>that not the narrowest shadow dared to sever.</p>
<p align="left" style="-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; -ms-text-size-adjust:100%; color:#595959; font-family:Arial, Sans-serif; font-size:15px; line-height:170%; text-align:left">And men could not see, looking up, what the wild geese drew,<br/>what the ospreys trailed behind them in silvery ropes<br/>that flashed in the icy sunlight; they could not hear<br/>battalions of starlings waging peaceful cries,<br/>bearing the net higher, covering this world<br/>like the vines of an orchard, or a mother drawing<br/>the trembling gauze over the trembling eyes<br/>of a child fluttering to sleep;<br/>                         it was the light<br/>that you will see at evening on the side of a hill<br/>in yellow October, and no one hearing knew<br/>what change had brought into the raven’s cawing,<br/>the killdeer’s screech, the ember-circling chough<br/>such an immense, soundless, and high concern<br/>for the fields and cities where the birds belong,<br/>except it was their seasonal passing, Love,<br/>made seasonless, or, from the high privilege of their birth,<br/>something brighter than pity for the wingless ones<br/>below them who shared dark holes in windows and in houses,<br/>and higher they lifted the net with soundless voices<br/>above all change, betrayals of falling suns,<br/>and this season lasted one moment, like the pause<br/>between dusk and darkness, between fury and peace,<br/>but, for such as our earth is now, it lasted long.</p>
</blockquote>
