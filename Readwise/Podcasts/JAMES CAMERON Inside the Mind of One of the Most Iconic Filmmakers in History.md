  - Cameron compares the dreaming/creative mind to a generative diffusion model that recombines life experience into new imagery. 
  - He treats part of the brain as creator and another part as the audience experiencing the simulation.
  Transcript:
  James Cameron
  I personally think that we're kind of like large language models. So all the training data of our life, it just goes into a kind of diffusion state, which is how generative AI works. It goes into a kind of a very noisy state and then out of that coalesces new things. And I think the brain is just constantly creating in the way that a generative AI works. But who's creating it and who is it being created for? So you're simultaneously the creator and the watcher, which is kind of amazing. I'm creating a simulated experience for myself. One part of my brain is. - **Decoding Whale Language**
  - James Cameron highlights a marine biology project led by David Gruber that uses AI and machine learning to decode whale vocalizations.
  - Researchers are analyzing thousands of hours of sperm whale vocalizations, paired with contextual footage, to understand their complex communication.
  - They've discovered that whales use clicks (codas) in sequences with verbs and syntax, suggesting a language as complex as human language.
  - Cameron states that nature is far more complex than we realize, and consciousness is shared by higher mammals and even some birds.
  - Recognition in a mirror is a key indicator of higher consciousness, a trait observed in elephants, chimpanzees and dolphins.
  - Studying great whales is challenging because of their size.
