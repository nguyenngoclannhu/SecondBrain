- **Precommitment Changes Identity and Eases Influence**
  - Pre-committing shifts behavior by folding actions into identity (e.g., "I am the kind of person who goes to the gym" vs "I'll go tomorrow").
  - Pre-commitments work both to yourself and socially: publicly declaring intentions increases follow-through.
  - Chase frames persuasion as building two walls—anxiety and cognitive dissonance—with the persuasive path offering relief between them.
  - Anxiety = fear of social rejection or breaking a social contract if you don't follow through.
  - Cognitive dissonance = mismatch between who you say you are and what you do, which reinforces identity-consistent behavior.
